AP Physics 2 · Electric Force, Field, and Potential · Step 5

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area $0.020\ \text{m}^2$ separated by 1.0 mm of vacuum. It is charged by a 12 V battery, and then the battery is disconnected. The plates are slowly pulled apart until the separation is 2.0 mm. Using $\epsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\ \text{F/m}$, find the energy stored in the capacitor at the final separation, in nanojoules.

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. What stays constant once the battery is disconnected?

Hint 2. Find $C$ initially from $\epsilon_0 A/d$, then $Q = CV$.

Hint 3. Final $C = \epsilon_0 A/d'$ and $U = Q^2/(2C)$.

Solution

Initially $C_1 = \epsilon_0A/d = 1.770\times10^{-10}\ \text{F}$ and $Q = C_1V = 2.124\times10^{-9}\ \text{C}$; the charge stays fixed after disconnection. At $d' = 2.0$ mm the capacitance halves to $8.850\times10^{-11}$ F. The stored energy is $U = Q^2/(2C') = 2.549\times10^{-8}\ \text{J} = 25.5\ \text{nJ}$, twice the initial energy, since work was done pulling the plates apart.

Answer: 25.488 nJ
