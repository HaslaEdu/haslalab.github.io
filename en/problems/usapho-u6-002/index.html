USAPhO · Modern Physics — practice problem 27

USAPhO · Modern Physics · Step 6

In 'muonic hydrogen' a negative muon ($m_\mu c^2=105.66$ MeV) replaces the electron and orbits a proton ($m_pc^2=938.27$ MeV). Using the Bohr model, with the reduced mass treated correctly, find the energy of the photon emitted in the $n=2\to n=1$ transition, in keV. (For an electron around an infinitely heavy nucleus, the ground-state binding energy is $13.606$ eV, and $m_ec^2=0.5110$ MeV.)

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. The Bohr energy levels scale in proportion to the reduced mass of the orbiting pair.

Hint 2. $\mu=\frac{m_\mu m_p}{m_\mu+m_p}$; the energies become $E_n=-13.606\,\text{eV}\,(\mu/m_e)/n^2$.

Hint 3. $E_{2\to1}=\tfrac34\times13.606\text{ eV}\times\mu/m_e$.

Solution

The reduced mass is $\mu c^2=\frac{105.66\times938.27}{1043.93}\approx94.96$ MeV, which is $185.8\,m_e$. Bohr levels scale with $\mu$, so $E_{2\to1}=\tfrac34(13.606\text{ eV})(185.8)\approx1896$ eV $\approx1.90$ keV, an X-ray. The orbit is also about 186 times smaller than in ordinary hydrogen.

Answer: 1.896 keV
