F=ma · Energy and Work · Step 6

A small block slides without friction from rest down a track and into a vertical circular loop of radius $R$. It is released from a height $h$ above the lowest point of the loop. For what value of $h/R$ does the track push on the block with a force equal to $mg$ when the block is at the top of the loop?

  1. 2.5
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 3
  5. 3.5
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. At the top, both gravity and the normal force point toward the center.

Hint 2. Use energy conservation to relate the speed at the top to $h$.

Hint 3. $N+mg=mv^2/R$ with $v^2=2g(h-2R)$.

Solution

At the top, $N+mg=mv^2/R$. With $N=mg$, $v^2=2gR$. Energy gives $v^2=2g(h-2R)$, so $h-2R=R$ and $h=3R$. (2.5 would just barely keep contact, $N=0$.)

Answer: D. 3
