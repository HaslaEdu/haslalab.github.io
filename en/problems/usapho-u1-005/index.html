USAPhO · Mechanics — practice problem 5

USAPhO · Mechanics · Step 7

A wedge of mass $M$ with incline angle $\theta$ rests on a frictionless horizontal floor. A uniform solid cylinder of mass $m$ is placed on the incline with its axis horizontal and along the incline's edge direction, and is released from rest. There is enough friction between cylinder and incline for it to roll without slipping. What is the magnitude of the wedge's horizontal acceleration?

  1. $\dfrac{mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{M+m\sin^2\theta}$
  2. $\dfrac{2mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{3M+m+2m\sin^2\theta}$
  3. $\dfrac{2mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{3(M+m)+2m\sin^2\theta}$
  4. $\dfrac{2mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{3M+2m\sin^2\theta}$
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Horizontal momentum is conserved, and with rolling the cylinder has kinetic energy of rotation too. Lagrange's equations with coordinates $X$ (wedge) and $s$ (distance along the incline) are clean.

Hint 2. Cylinder velocity: $(\dot X+\dot s\cos\theta,\,-\dot s\sin\theta)$; spin $\omega=\dot s/r$ with $I=\tfrac12 mr^2$.

Hint 3. Momentum: $(M+m)\dot X+m\dot s\cos\theta=0$. The $s$-equation: $\tfrac32 m\ddot s+m\ddot X\cos\theta=mg\sin\theta$.

Solution

Take $X$ for the wedge and $s$ for the distance down the incline relative to the wedge. Then $T=\tfrac12M\dot X^2+\tfrac12m[(\dot X+\dot s\cos\theta)^2+\dot s^2\sin^2\theta]+\tfrac14m\dot s^2$ and $V=-mgs\sin\theta$. The equations of motion are $(M+m)\ddot X+m\ddot s\cos\theta=0$ and $\tfrac32\ddot s+\ddot X\cos\theta=g\sin\theta$. Eliminating $\ddot s$ gives $|\ddot X|=\dfrac{2mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{3M+m+2m\sin^2\theta}$. Check: if the $\tfrac12$ in $I$ were set to zero (a sliding block), the formula would reduce to $mg\sin\theta\cos\theta/(M+m\sin^2\theta)$.

Answer: B. $\dfrac{2mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{3M+m+2m\sin^2\theta}$
