AP Physics 1 · Work, Energy, and Power · Step 3

A 1200 kg car speeds up from 10 m/s to 20 m/s. How much net work is done on the car?

  1. 60 kJ
  2. 180 kJ
  3. 240 kJ
  4. 360 kJ
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Use the work-energy theorem.

Hint 2. Net work equals the change in kinetic energy; square each speed before subtracting, not the difference of the speeds.

Hint 3. $W=\tfrac12 m(v_f^2-v_i^2)$.

Solution

By the work-energy theorem $W_{net}=\Delta K=\tfrac12 m(v_f^2-v_i^2)=\tfrac12(1200)(400-100)=1.8\times10^5$ J $=180$ kJ. Note $\tfrac12m(\Delta v)^2$ would give 60 kJ, which is wrong because kinetic energy is not linear in speed.

Answer: B. 180 kJ
