AP Physics 2 · Magnetism and Electromagnetism · Step 5

Two long, straight, parallel wires carry equal currents $I$ in opposite directions. The magnetic force on a length of one wire due to the other has magnitude $F$. Now both currents are doubled and the separation between the wires is also doubled. What is the new force on the same length of wire, and is it attractive or repulsive?

  1. Repulsive, with magnitude $2F$.
  2. Attractive, with magnitude $2F$.
  3. Repulsive, with magnitude $8F$.
  4. Repulsive, with magnitude $F/2$.
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Opposite currents attract or repel?

Hint 2. The field of one wire is proportional to $I/d$, and the force on the other is $BI_2L$.

Hint 3. The force scales as $I_1I_2/d$.

Solution

The force per length between parallel wires is $F/L = \mu_0 I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$. Doubling both currents multiplies it by $2\times2 = 4$, and doubling $d$ multiplies it by $1/2$, for a net factor of 2. Antiparallel currents repel, and this does not change, so the force is repulsive with magnitude $2F$.

Answer: A. Repulsive, with magnitude $2F$.
