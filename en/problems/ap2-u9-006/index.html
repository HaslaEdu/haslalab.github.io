AP Physics 2 · Thermodynamics · Step 5

A gas is held in a cylinder with thermally insulating walls and a movable piston. The piston is pushed in quickly, compressing the gas. Which statement correctly describes the process?

  1. The temperature stays constant because the work done on the gas leaves as heat.
  2. The internal energy decreases because the gas does negative work on the piston.
  3. The heat transferred is positive because the compressed gas is hotter than the walls, so $\Delta U = Q$.
  4. The heat transferred is zero, the surroundings do positive work on the gas, so the internal energy and temperature of the gas increase.
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. What does insulating walls tell you about $Q$?

Hint 2. Compression means the piston does work on the gas.

Hint 3. With $Q=0$, $\Delta U = W_{on}$, and for an ideal gas $U$ depends on $T$.

Solution

Insulating walls mean $Q = 0$. The piston does positive work on the gas, so $\Delta U = Q + W_{on} = W_{on} > 0$. Internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, so $T$ rises. The isothermal case would require heat to flow out, which the insulation prevents.

Answer: D. The heat transferred is zero, the surroundings do positive work on the gas, so the internal energy and temperature of the gas increase.
