AP Physics 2 · Modern Physics · Step 5

An electron starts from rest and is accelerated through a potential difference of 150 V. Use $h = 6.63\times10^{-34}$ J·s, $m_e = 9.11\times10^{-31}$ kg and $e = 1.60\times10^{-19}$ C, and ignore relativistic effects. What is the electron's de Broglie wavelength, in nanometers?

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Where does the electron's kinetic energy come from?

Hint 2. Relate the accelerating voltage to the momentum.

Hint 3. $eV = p^2/(2m)$ and $\lambda = h/p$.

Solution

The kinetic energy gained is $eV = p^2/(2m_e)$, so $p = \sqrt{2m_e eV} = \sqrt{2(9.11\times10^{-31})(1.60\times10^{-19})(150)} = 6.613\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$. The de Broglie wavelength is $\lambda = h/p = (6.63\times10^{-34})/(6.613\times10^{-24}) = 1.003\times10^{-10}\ \text{m} = 0.100\ \text{nm}$, comparable to atomic spacings.

Answer: 0.100261 nm
