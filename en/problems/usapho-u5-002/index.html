USAPhO · Waves and Optics — practice problem 22

USAPhO · Waves and Optics · Step 7

A radio antenna stands on top of a sea cliff, $100$ m above a calm sea, and observes a distant radio source at a wavelength of $1.5$ m as the source rises above the horizon. It receives the direct wave and a wave reflected from the sea surface. Treat the sea as a flat mirror that reverses the phase of the reflected wave. At what elevation angle of the source above the horizon is the first interference MAXIMUM received? Give the answer in degrees.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. The reflected wave seems to come from an image antenna $100$ m below sea level, so this is a two-source interference problem.

Hint 2. The geometric path difference for a distant source at elevation $\alpha$ is $2h\sin\alpha$. Add the half-wave shift from the reflection.

Hint 3. The first maximum is at $2h\sin\alpha+\lambda/2=\lambda$.

Solution

The direct and reflected rays differ in path by $2h\sin\alpha$, and the reflection adds $\lambda/2$. So at the horizon ($\alpha=0$) the signal is a minimum, and the first maximum is at $2h\sin\alpha=\lambda/2$: $\sin\alpha=\lambda/(4h)=1.5/400$, giving $\alpha\approx0.215^\circ$ (about 13 arc-minutes). This is the sea-cliff interferometer of early radio astronomy.

Answer: 0.2149 deg
