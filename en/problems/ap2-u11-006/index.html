AP Physics 2 · Electric Circuits · Step 6

An ideal battery is connected across a series combination of resistors R$_1$ and R$_2$. A third resistor R$_3$ is then connected in parallel with R$_2$ (its two ends are attached to the two ends of R$_2$). Compared with before R$_3$ was added, what happens to the current through the battery and the current through R$_2$?

  1. The battery current increases and the current through R$_2$ increases.
  2. The battery current decreases and the current through R$_2$ decreases.
  3. The battery current is unchanged and the current through R$_2$ decreases.
  4. The battery current increases and the current through R$_2$ decreases.
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. How does adding a parallel branch change the equivalent resistance of that part?

Hint 2. Find what happens to the total current, then to the voltage across R$_1$.

Hint 3. The battery emf is fixed: $V_1 + V_2 = \varepsilon$.

Solution

Adding R$_3$ in parallel with R$_2$ lowers the equivalent resistance of that section, so $R_{eq}$ of the whole circuit drops and the battery current $I = \varepsilon/R_{eq}$ rises. The larger current through R$_1$ increases its potential difference $V_1 = IR_1$. Since $V_1 + V_2 = \varepsilon$, the voltage across R$_2$ falls, and so does the current through R$_2$ (the extra current goes through R$_3$).

Answer: D. The battery current increases and the current through R$_2$ decreases.
