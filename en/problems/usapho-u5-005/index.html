USAPhO · Waves and Optics · Step 6

A long uniform heavy rope of length $L$ hangs vertically from a ceiling hook, with its lower end free. A small transverse pulse is started at the bottom end. How long does the pulse take to reach the top? (The rope's own weight provides the tension.)

  1. $\sqrt{L/g}$
  2. $\sqrt{2L/g}$
  3. $2\sqrt{L/g}$
  4. $2\sqrt{2L/g}$
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. The tension at height $y$ above the bottom supports the rope below that point: $T=\mu g y$.

Hint 2. So the wave speed is $v=\sqrt{T/\mu}=\sqrt{gy}$, and it increases going up.

Hint 3. $t=\int_0^L dy/\sqrt{gy}$.

Solution

At distance $y$ above the free end the tension is $\mu gy$, so the pulse speed is $v=\sqrt{gy}$, independent of the rope's density. The travel time is $t=\int_0^L\frac{dy}{\sqrt{gy}}=2\sqrt{L/g}$. Since $v^2=gy$, the pulse moves with uniform acceleration $g/2$, which checks: $L=\tfrac12(g/2)t^2$.

Answer: C. $2\sqrt{L/g}$
