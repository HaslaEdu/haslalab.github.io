USAPhO · Modern Physics · Step 8

A particle is in the ground state of a one-dimensional infinite square well occupying $0<x<L$. Suddenly (much faster than any quantum timescale) the right wall is moved from $x=L$ to $x=2L$. What is the probability that a subsequent energy measurement finds the particle in the ground state of the new, wider well? Give a decimal number.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Sudden approximation: the wavefunction does not change during the move, but it must now be expanded in the eigenstates of the new well.

Hint 2. Old: $\psi=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(\pi x/L)$ on $[0,L]$, zero beyond. New ground state: $\phi_1=\sqrt{1/L}\sin(\pi x/2L)$ on $[0,2L]$.

Hint 3. Compute $c_1=\int_0^L\psi\phi_1dx$ using $\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)]$; the probability is $|c_1|^2$.

Solution

The overlap is $c_1=\frac{\sqrt2}{L}\int_0^L\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\sin\frac{\pi x}{2L}dx=\frac{\sqrt2}{L}\cdot\frac{4L}{3\pi}=\frac{4\sqrt2}{3\pi}$. So $P=|c_1|^2=\frac{32}{9\pi^2}\approx0.360$. Most of the remaining probability is in the new $n=2$ state, which has exactly the old wavefunction's shape on $[0,L]$: $P_2=1/2$.

Answer: 0.3603
