AP Physics 1 · Work, Energy, and Power · Step 6

A 2.0 kg block is released from rest at a height of 1.5 m on a frictionless curved track. At the bottom it crosses a rough horizontal section 2.0 m long (coefficient of kinetic friction 0.40), then moves onto a frictionless horizontal surface where it strikes a horizontal spring of constant 800 N/m, compressing it. Use $g=9.8$ m/s$^2$. What is the maximum compression of the spring? Answer in meters.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Use energy: initial gravitational energy minus friction loss equals the final spring energy.

Hint 2. The block is momentarily at rest at maximum compression, and the spring section is frictionless.

Hint 3. $mgh-\mu mgL=\tfrac12 kx^2$.

Solution

Initial energy $mgh=2.0(9.8)(1.5)=29.40$ J. Friction removes $\mu mgL=0.40(2.0)(9.8)(2.0)=15.68$ J. The rest, $13.72$ J, becomes $\tfrac12 kx^2$, so $x=\sqrt{2(13.72)/800}=0.185$ m.

Answer: 0.1852 m
