USAPhO · Mechanics — practice problem 3

USAPhO · Mechanics · Step 7

A garden-hose nozzle at the edge of a vertical wall $h=20$ m above flat ground ejects water at speed $v_0=15$ m/s. The gardener may aim the nozzle at any angle. Neglecting air resistance, what is the largest horizontal distance from the foot of the wall at which the water can hit the ground? Give the answer in m.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. A launch angle of $45^\circ$ is optimal only when launch and landing heights are equal.

Hint 2. Use energy conservation: every jet lands with the same speed $v_1=\sqrt{v_0^2+2gh}$. The range is maximized when the launch and landing velocities are perpendicular.

Hint 3. With perpendicular velocities, the triangle formed by $\vec v_0$, $\vec v_1$ and $\vec g t$ gives $R=v_0v_1/g$.

Solution

Every water jet lands with speed $v_1=\sqrt{v_0^2+2gh}=\sqrt{225+392}\approx24.8$ m/s. Since $\vec v_1=\vec v_0+\vec g t$, the triangle of these vectors has area $\tfrac12 g t\,v_{0x}=\tfrac12 gR$, and that area is also $\tfrac12 v_0v_1\sin\phi$, where $\phi$ is the angle between $\vec v_0$ and $\vec v_1$. The range is therefore largest when $\phi=90^\circ$: $R_{max}=v_0\sqrt{v_0^2+2gh}/g=15\times24.84/9.8\approx38.0$ m.

Answer: 38.02 m
