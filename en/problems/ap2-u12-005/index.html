AP Physics 2 · Magnetism and Electromagnetism · Step 5

The north pole of a bar magnet is moved straight toward a stationary circular loop of wire, along the loop's axis. Which describes the induced current in the loop and the magnetic force between the loop and the magnet?

  1. The current is clockwise as seen from the magnet's side, and the loop repels the magnet.
  2. The current is clockwise as seen from the magnet's side, and the loop attracts the magnet.
  3. The current is counterclockwise as seen from the magnet's side, and the loop attracts the magnet.
  4. The current is counterclockwise as seen from the magnet's side, and the loop repels the magnet.
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Is the flux through the loop increasing or decreasing?

Hint 2. Lenz's law: the induced field opposes the change in flux.

Hint 3. The loop should act like a north pole facing the approaching north pole.

Solution

As the north pole approaches, the flux through the loop directed toward the loop increases. By Lenz's law, the induced current produces a field that opposes this change, so the loop's face toward the magnet becomes a north pole. Field pointing toward the viewer requires a counterclockwise current. Two north poles facing each other repel, which opposes the magnet's motion, as required by energy conservation.

Answer: D. The current is counterclockwise as seen from the magnet's side, and the loop repels the magnet.
