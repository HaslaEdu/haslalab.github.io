USAPhO · Electricity and Magnetism — practice problem 19

USAPhO · Electricity and Magnetism · Step 6

A capacitor $C_1$ is charged to voltage $V_0$, and a second capacitor $C_2$ is also charged to $V_0$. They are then connected in a single loop, positive plate of each to the negative plate of the other, through a resistor $R$ and an inductor $L$ in series. The current oscillates and eventually dies away. How much heat is produced in the resistor in total?

  1. $\dfrac{C_1C_2V_0^2}{2(C_1+C_2)}$
  2. $\dfrac{2C_1C_2V_0^2}{C_1+C_2}$
  3. $\dfrac{4C_1C_2V_0^2}{C_1+C_2}$
  4. $\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V_0^2$
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Because of how they are connected, the loop starts with a total voltage of $2V_0$ driving current.

Hint 2. The final state has no current, so the capacitor voltages must be equal around the loop. Use charge conservation on the connected plates.

Hint 3. Heat equals the initial minus the final stored energy; this works out to $\tfrac12 C_s(\Delta V)^2$ with $C_s=C_1C_2/(C_1+C_2)$.

Solution

Each connected pair of plates holds net charge $\pm(C_1-C_2)V_0$. At the end the voltages are equal: $V_f=(C_1-C_2)V_0/(C_1+C_2)$. Then $Q=\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V_0^2-\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V_f^2=\frac{2C_1C_2V_0^2}{C_1+C_2}$, which is the same as $\tfrac12C_s(2V_0)^2$. The result does not depend on $R$ or $L$, because any finite resistance eventually dissipates all the energy available. Treating the initial voltage difference as $V_0$ instead of $2V_0$ gives the first option.

Answer: B. $\dfrac{2C_1C_2V_0^2}{C_1+C_2}$
