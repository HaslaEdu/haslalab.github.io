USAPhO · Mechanics — practice problem 2

USAPhO · Mechanics · Step 8

A raindrop falls from rest through a stationary cloud of fine mist. It stays spherical, and it gains mass only by sweeping up the mist droplets in its path, so the rate of mass gain is proportional to its cross-sectional area times its speed. The mist density is uniform, air drag is negligible, and the initial radius of the drop is negligible. After a long time the drop falls with constant acceleration. Find that acceleration in m/s$^2$ (take $g=9.8$ m/s$^2$).

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. The mist is at rest, so the momentum equation is $d(mv)/dt=mg$ (not $m\,dv/dt=mg$).

Hint 2. Write $m\propto r^3$ and $dm/dt\propto r^2 v$; this gives $dr/dt\propto v$, so $r$ grows like a distance travelled.

Hint 3. Try $v=at$, so that $r\propto t^2$ and $m\propto t^6$, and substitute into $d(mv)/dt=mg$.

Solution

Since the mist starts at rest, $\frac{d}{dt}(mv)=mg$. With $m=\tfrac43\pi\rho_w r^3$ and $dm/dt=\rho_m\pi r^2 v$ we get $dr/dt=(\rho_m/4\rho_w)v$, so $r$ grows in proportion to the distance fallen. Try constant acceleration: $v=at$, so the distance is $at^2/2$, $r\propto t^2$ and $m\propto t^6$. Then $\frac{d}{dt}(mv)=\frac{d}{dt}(Ct^6\cdot at)=7Cat^6=7ma$, and $7ma=mg$ gives $a=g/7\approx1.4$ m/s$^2$.

Answer: 1.4 m/s^2
