USAPhO · Thermodynamics and Statistical Physics — practice problem 13

USAPhO · Thermodynamics and Statistical Physics · Step 7

Impurity ions in a crystal behave as independent two-level systems with energy gap $\varepsilon=0.010$ eV and nondegenerate levels; in thermal equilibrium, the levels are populated according to the Boltzmann distribution. Their contribution to the heat capacity (a 'Schottky anomaly') rises from zero at low temperature, peaks, and falls off again at high temperature. At what temperature does it peak? Give the answer in K ($k_B=8.617\times10^{-5}$ eV/K).

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Mean energy per ion: $\bar E=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{e^{x}+1}$ with $x=\varepsilon/k_BT$.

Hint 2. $C/k_B=\dfrac{x^2e^x}{(e^x+1)^2}$. Maximize this over $x$.

Hint 3. Setting the derivative to zero gives the transcendental equation $x\tanh(x/2)=2$, i.e. $x(e^x-1)=2(e^x+1)$; solve it numerically.

Solution

$\bar E=\varepsilon/(e^x+1)$ with $x=\varepsilon/k_BT$, so $C=k_B\frac{x^2e^x}{(e^x+1)^2}$. Setting $dC/dx=0$ gives $x\tanh(x/2)=2$, whose root is $x\approx2.399$. Hence $T_{peak}=\varepsilon/(k_Bx)=0.010/(8.617\times10^{-5}\times2.399)\approx48.4$ K.

Answer: 48.37 K
