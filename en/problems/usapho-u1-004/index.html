USAPhO · Mechanics — practice problem 4

USAPhO · Mechanics · Step 7

A satellite of mass $500$ kg is in a near-circular orbit at an altitude of $400$ km above the Earth (radius $6371$ km, $GM_E=3.986\times10^{14}$ m$^3$/s$^2$). Thin residual air exerts a constant drag force of $1.0\times10^{-3}$ N opposite to its velocity. The orbit stays nearly circular as it slowly decays. By how much does the altitude decrease per day? Give the answer in m. (Notice what happens to the satellite's speed.)

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. For a circular orbit the total energy is $E=-GMm/(2r)$, and the drag removes energy at the rate $Fv$.

Hint 2. Differentiate: $dE/dt=\frac{GMm}{2r^2}\frac{dr}{dt}=-Fv$, with $v=\sqrt{GM/r}$.

Hint 3. $|dr/dt|=2Fvr^2/(GMm)$; multiply by 86400 s.

Solution

With $E=-GMm/2r$ and $dE/dt=-Fv$ we get $\frac{GMm}{2r^2}\dot r=-Fv$, so $|\dot r|=\frac{2Fvr^2}{GMm}$. Here $r=6.771\times10^6$ m and $v=\sqrt{GM/r}\approx7673$ m/s, which gives $|\dot r|\approx3.53\times10^{-3}$ m/s, or about $305$ m per day. The speed actually increases as the orbit sinks (kinetic energy $=-E$ grows): gravity does twice as much positive work as the drag does negative work.

Answer: 305 m
