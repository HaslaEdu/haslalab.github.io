AP Physics 2 · Electric Circuits · Step 5

An initially uncharged 20 $\mu$F capacitor is connected in series with a 5.0 kΩ resistor, an ideal 12 V battery, and a closed switch. At the instant the potential difference across the capacitor has reached 8.0 V, what is the current in the circuit, in milliamperes?

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Apply the loop rule: battery emf equals the sum of the voltage drops.

Hint 2. The voltage across the resistor is what is left after the capacitor takes its share.

Hint 3. $I = (\varepsilon - V_C)/R$.

Solution

The loop rule gives $\varepsilon = IR + V_C$. With $V_C = 8.0$ V, the resistor has $12 - 8.0 = 4.0$ V across it. The current is $I = 4.0/(5.0\times10^3) = 8.0\times10^{-4}\ \text{A} = 0.80\ \text{mA}$. The capacitance does not change this instantaneous result; it sets how quickly the current decays.

Answer: 0.8 mA
