USAPhO · Mechanics — practice problem 1

USAPhO · Mechanics · Step 7

A uniform flexible chain of length $L=2.0$ m lies straight on a horizontal table, perpendicular to the edge, with a length $a=0.60$ m hanging over the smooth rounded edge. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the chain and the tabletop is $\mu=0.20$; the edge itself is frictionless. The chain is released from rest and starts to slide. Treat the chain as a line of mass that stays in contact with the table and hangs vertically. Find the speed of the chain at the instant its last link leaves the table, in m/s.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Use the work-energy theorem with the hanging length $x$ as the coordinate; the friction force changes as the chain leaves the table.

Hint 2. With linear density $\lambda$, the hanging part has potential energy $-\lambda g x^2/2$ and the friction force is $\mu\lambda g(L-x)$.

Hint 3. $\tfrac12\lambda L v^2=\tfrac12\lambda g(L^2-a^2)-\int_a^L\mu\lambda g(L-x)\,dx$.

Solution

Let $x$ be the hanging length and $\lambda$ the mass per length. Gravity does work $\tfrac12\lambda g(L^2-a^2)$ as $x$ grows from $a$ to $L$, while friction on the part still on the table does work $-\int_a^L\mu\lambda g(L-x)dx=-\tfrac12\mu\lambda g(L-a)^2$. The whole chain moves with one speed, so $\tfrac12\lambda Lv^2=\tfrac12\lambda g[(L^2-a^2)-\mu(L-a)^2]$, giving $v=\sqrt{g[(L^2-a^2)-\mu(L-a)^2]/L}$. (It starts moving because $a>\mu(L-a)$.) Numerically $v=\sqrt{9.8(3.64-0.392)/2.0}\approx3.99$ m/s.

Answer: 3.989 m/s
