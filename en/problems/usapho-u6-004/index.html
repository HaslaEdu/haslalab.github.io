USAPhO · Modern Physics — practice problem 29

USAPhO · Modern Physics · Step 6

Today natural uranium has an atom ratio $^{235}$U/$^{238}$U $=0.00725$. The half-lives are $7.04\times10^8$ yr for $^{235}$U and $4.468\times10^9$ yr for $^{238}$U. Suppose (as a crude model of nucleosynthesis) that the two isotopes were produced in equal numbers in a single event and that no uranium was added afterwards. How long ago did that event occur? Give the answer in billions of years ($10^9$ yr).

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Each isotope decays as $N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$ with $\lambda=\ln2/T_{1/2}$.

Hint 2. The ratio decays as $e^{-(\lambda_5-\lambda_8)t}$, starting from 1.

Hint 3. $t=\ln(1/0.00725)/(\lambda_5-\lambda_8)$.

Solution

With equal initial numbers, $N_5/N_8=e^{-(\lambda_5-\lambda_8)t}$. Here $\lambda_5=9.846\times10^{-10}$/yr and $\lambda_8=1.551\times10^{-10}$/yr, so $t=\frac{\ln(137.9)}{8.29\times10^{-10}}\approx5.94\times10^9$ yr. This is older than the Solar System (4.6 Gyr), which is consistent with uranium having been made in many earlier supernovae and other events.

Answer: 5.94 Gyr
