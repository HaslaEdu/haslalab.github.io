AP Physics 2 · Thermodynamics · Step 3

A sealed, rigid container holds a fixed amount of an ideal gas at 27 °C. The container is heated until the gas is at 327 °C. By what factor does the gas pressure change?

  1. It increases by a factor of 2.
  2. It increases by a factor of 12.1.
  3. It stays the same because the volume is constant.
  4. It increases by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Which temperature scale must be used in the ideal gas law?

Hint 2. For fixed $n$ and $V$, how is $P$ related to $T$?

Hint 3. Convert both temperatures to kelvin, then use $P_2/P_1 = T_2/T_1$.

Solution

With $n$ and $V$ fixed, $P \propto T$ for an ideal gas, with $T$ in kelvin. $T_1 = 300\ \text{K}$ and $T_2 = 600\ \text{K}$, so $P_2/P_1 = 600/300 = 2$. Using Celsius values (327/27 = 12.1) is the classic error, and $\sqrt{2}$ confuses pressure with the rms speed.

Answer: A. It increases by a factor of 2.
