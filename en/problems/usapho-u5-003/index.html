USAPhO · Waves and Optics · Step 6

A spherical water droplet ($n=1.33$) of radius $2.0$ mm hangs on a leaf in sunlight. Considering only paraxial rays (close to the axis through the centre), how far beyond the back surface of the droplet are the sun's rays brought to a focus? Give the answer in mm.

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. Refract at the first surface using $\frac{n_2}{v}-\frac{n_1}{u}=\frac{n_2-n_1}{R}$ with an object at infinity.

Hint 2. The first image is at $nR/(n-1)$ behind the front surface, beyond the back surface, so it acts as a virtual object for the second surface.

Hint 3. Equivalently, a ball lens has focal length $f=\frac{nR}{2(n-1)}$ measured from its centre; subtract $R$.

Solution

At the front surface: $\frac{n}{v_1}=\frac{n-1}{R}$, so $v_1=\frac{nR}{n-1}\approx8.06$ mm, which is $4.06$ mm beyond the back surface. At the back surface (radius $-R$, from $n$ to 1), $\frac1{v}=\frac{n}{4.061}+\frac{n-1}{R}$ gives $v\approx2.03$ mm. This agrees with $f=\frac{nR}{2(n-1)}$ from the centre minus $R$.

Answer: 2.03 mm
