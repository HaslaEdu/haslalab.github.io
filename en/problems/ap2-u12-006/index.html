AP Physics 2 · Magnetism and Electromagnetism · Step 4

A rectangular wire loop moves at constant velocity through a region of space in which there is a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the loop. The loop stays entirely inside the field region the whole time. What is the net induced emf around the loop, and why?

  1. Nonzero, because the loop is moving through a magnetic field.
  2. Zero, because the magnetic flux through the loop does not change.
  3. Nonzero, because the magnetic force on the loop's moving charges is nonzero.
  4. Zero, because a uniform magnetic field exerts no force on moving charges.
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. What does Faraday's law say the emf depends on?

Hint 2. Is the flux $BA\cos\theta$ changing in this situation?

Hint 3. Motional emfs on opposite sides of the loop oppose each other.

Solution

Faraday's law says $\varepsilon = -\Delta\Phi/\Delta t$. Here $B$, the loop area, and the angle are all constant, so $\Phi$ does not change and $\varepsilon = 0$. The magnetic force does act on charges in the sides, producing equal and opposite motional emfs that cancel around the loop. The last choice is wrong because the field does exert forces on moving charges.

Answer: B. Zero, because the magnetic flux through the loop does not change.
