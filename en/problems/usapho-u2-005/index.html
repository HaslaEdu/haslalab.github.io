USAPhO · Rotation and Oscillations — practice problem 10

USAPhO · Rotation and Oscillations · Step 8

A uniform thin rod of length $1.5$ m stands vertically on a perfectly frictionless horizontal ice surface and is given a negligible nudge so that it falls over. The lower end slides freely on the ice and never leaves it. Find the speed of the rod's upper end just as the rod becomes horizontal (just before it strikes the ice), in m/s.

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. With no horizontal force, the centre of mass falls straight down.

Hint 2. If $\theta$ is the angle from the horizontal, $y_{cm}=\tfrac L2\sin\theta$, so $v_{cm}=\tfrac L2\cos\theta\,\dot\theta$. At $\theta=0$ the centre speed is $\tfrac L2\omega$.

Hint 3. Energy at the horizontal: $mg\tfrac L2=\tfrac12m(\tfrac L2\omega)^2+\tfrac12\tfrac{mL^2}{12}\omega^2$.

Solution

No horizontal force acts, so the centre falls vertically. At the moment the rod is horizontal, $v_{cm}=\tfrac L2\omega$ downward. Energy gives $mg\tfrac L2=\tfrac12m\tfrac{L^2}4\omega^2+\tfrac1{24}mL^2\omega^2=\tfrac16mL^2\omega^2$, so $\omega^2=3g/L$. The upper end moves down at $v_{cm}+\tfrac L2\omega=L\omega=\sqrt{3gL}\approx6.64$ m/s. This equals the result for a rod hinged at its base, but the route there is different.

Answer: 6.641 m/s
