AP Physics 2 · Thermodynamics · Step 4

An ideal gas undergoes a cycle on a PV diagram made of four straight steps: from A ($1.0\times10^5$ Pa, $0.010\ \text{m}^3$) the pressure rises at constant volume to B ($3.0\times10^5$ Pa, $0.010\ \text{m}^3$); the gas expands at constant pressure to C ($3.0\times10^5$ Pa, $0.030\ \text{m}^3$); the pressure falls at constant volume to D ($1.0\times10^5$ Pa, $0.030\ \text{m}^3$); the gas is compressed at constant pressure back to A. What net heat, in joules, does the gas absorb per cycle?

Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. What is the change in internal energy of a gas after a complete cycle?

Hint 2. Net heat in equals net work out when $\Delta U = 0$.

Hint 3. Net work is the enclosed area: $(\Delta P)(\Delta V)$ for a rectangle.

Solution

Over a full cycle the state returns to its start, so $\Delta U = 0$ and the first law gives $Q_{net} = W_{net}$. Work is done by the gas on the high-pressure expansion B to C and on the gas on the low-pressure compression D to A; the net work equals the enclosed rectangle. $W_{net} = (3.0\times10^5 - 1.0\times10^5)(0.030-0.010) = 4000\ \text{J}$, so the gas absorbs a net 4000 J per cycle.

Answer: 4000 J
