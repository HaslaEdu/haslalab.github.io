AP Physics C: Mechanics · Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems · Step 5

A flywheel with moment of inertia 0.50 kg m$^2$ starts from rest and is driven by a constant net torque of 4.0 N m for 3.0 s. How much work does the torque do in this time? Answer in joules.

Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Work done by the net torque equals the change in rotational kinetic energy.

Hint 2. Find $\alpha=\tau/I$ and then $\omega$ after 3.0 s.

Hint 3. $W=\tfrac12I\omega^2$ with $\omega=\alpha t=24$ rad/s.

Solution

$\alpha=\tau/I=8.0$ rad/s$^2$ and $\omega=\alpha t=24$ rad/s. $W=\Delta K=\tfrac12(0.50)(24)^2=144$ J. Check: $W=\tau\theta=4.0\times\tfrac12(8.0)(3.0)^2=144$ J.

Answer: 144 J
