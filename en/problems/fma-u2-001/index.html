F=ma · Dynamics and Friction · Step 6

A block is launched up a rough incline of angle $\theta$, comes to rest momentarily, and then slides back down to its starting point. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu=\tfrac12\tan\theta$. What is the ratio (time spent sliding down)/(time spent sliding up)?

  1. $2$
  2. $\sqrt2$
  3. $\sqrt3$
  4. $3$
  5. $1$
Solve it on HaslaLab and have your answer checked →
Hints

Hint 1. Both trips cover the same distance, and each one starts or ends at rest at the turning point.

Hint 2. For equal distances from rest, $t\propto 1/\sqrt a$.

Hint 3. Up: $a_u=g(\sin\theta+\mu\cos\theta)$; down: $a_d=g(\sin\theta-\mu\cos\theta)$.

Solution

With $\mu\cos\theta=\tfrac12\sin\theta$: $a_u=\tfrac32 g\sin\theta$ and $a_d=\tfrac12 g\sin\theta$. The distance $d=\tfrac12 a t^2$ is the same both ways, so $t_d/t_u=\sqrt{a_u/a_d}=\sqrt3$.

Answer: C. $\sqrt3$
