AP Physics 2 · Electric Force, Field, and Potential · Step 5

A parallel-plate capacitor stays connected to an ideal battery. A dielectric slab with constant $\kappa > 1$ is then inserted so that it fills the gap between the plates. Compared with before, what happens to the charge $Q$ on the plates, the potential difference $\Delta V$ across them, and the stored energy $U$?

  1. $Q$ increases, $\Delta V$ is unchanged, and $U$ increases.
  2. $Q$ is unchanged, $\Delta V$ decreases, and $U$ decreases.
  3. $Q$ increases, $\Delta V$ increases, and $U$ increases.
  4. $Q$ decreases, $\Delta V$ is unchanged, and $U$ decreases.
Solve it with instant grading, hints and an AI tutor →
Hints

Hint 1. What fixes $\Delta V$ when the battery stays connected?

Hint 2. How does a dielectric change the capacitance?

Hint 3. $Q = C\Delta V$ and $U = \tfrac12 C\Delta V^2$ with $C \to \kappa C$.

Solution

The battery holds $\Delta V$ constant. The dielectric multiplies the capacitance by $\kappa$, so $Q = C\Delta V$ increases by the factor $\kappa$ as the battery supplies more charge. The energy $U = \tfrac12 C\Delta V^2$ also increases by $\kappa$. The case with $Q$ fixed and $\Delta V$ decreasing applies only if the battery is disconnected.

Answer: A. $Q$ increases, $\Delta V$ is unchanged, and $U$ increases.
