Investigation of the friction force (Solution)

Part 1. Friction on a cylindrical rod

1.1 When the load moves down, at the moment it stops the forces satisfy the relations

$\begin{cases}T_0=mg\\T_1=\gamma T_0\\T_1=k\Delta x_1\end{cases}\ \Rightarrow\ k\Delta x_1=\gamma mg\,.$

From these equations it follows that

$x_2=l_0+\gamma\dfrac{mg}k\,.$(1)

When the load moves up, we have the equations

$\begin{cases}T_1=mg\\T_1=\gamma T_0\\T_0=k\Delta x_1\end{cases}\ \Rightarrow\ \gamma k\Delta x_1=\gamma mg\,,$

from which it follows that

$x_1=l_0+\dfrac{mg}{\gamma k}\,.$(2)

1.2 The required graphs of the experimental dependences of the spring lengths $x_1$ and $x_2$ on the number of suspended loads $n$ are shown in the figure

1.3 The coefficients and their errors are computed by the method of least squares with the formulas:

$Y_i=aX_i+b$

 Calculation of the coefficients:

- the mean values, which define the centre of the experimental points:

$\langle X\rangle=\dfrac{\sum X_i}N\,;\qquad\langle Y\rangle=\dfrac{\sum Y_i}N\,;$

 - the variances (the mean square minus the square of the mean),

$S_X^2=\dfrac{\sum X_i^2}N-\langle X\rangle^2\,;\qquad S_Y^2=\dfrac{\sum Y_i^2}N-\langle Y\rangle^2\,;$

- the covariance coefficient (the mean of the product minus the product of the means):

$R_{XY}=\dfrac{\sum X_iY_i}N-\langle X\rangle\cdot\langle Y\rangle\,;$

-  the coefficients:

$a=\dfrac{R_{XY}}{S_X^2}\,;\qquad b=\langle Y\rangle-a\langle X\rangle\,;$

- the errors of the coefficients:

$\Delta a=t_{N-1,p}\sqrt{\dfrac1{N-2}\left(\dfrac{S_Y^2}{S_X^2}-a^2\right)}\qquad\Delta b=\Delta a\sqrt{S_X^2+\langle X\rangle^2}$

The results of the calculation of the coefficients of the dependences built are

$a_1=(3.66\pm0.15)\,{\rm cm},\quad\varepsilon_a=0.04$
$b_1=(8.9\pm0.7)\,{\rm cm},\quad\varepsilon_b=0.07$(3)
$a_2=(2.19\pm0.14)\,{\rm cm},\quad\varepsilon_a=0.06$
$b_2=(8.1\pm0.6)\,{\rm cm},\quad\varepsilon_b=0.08$(4)

1.4. We rewrite the theoretical formulas so as to express the values of the coefficients found

$x_1=l_0+\dfrac{mg}{\gamma k}=l_0+\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k}n$
$x_2=l_0+\gamma\dfrac{mg}k=l_0+\gamma\dfrac{m_{el}g}kn$(5)

These formulas show that the coefficients of the experimental dependences are expressed through the parameters of the setup as follows:

$b_1=b_2=l_0$
$a_1=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k}$
$a_2=\gamma\dfrac{m_{el}g}k$(6)

These formulas let us calculate the values of the parameters of the setup.

The length of the undeformed spring is approximately

$l_0\approx b_1\approx b_2\,.$(7)

Note that the experimental values of these parameters agree within the errors. To make the result more accurate, the mean value of the two should be taken

$\langle l_0\rangle=\dfrac12(b_1+b_2)=8.5\,{\rm cm}\,.$(8)

Then the error of this value is

$\Delta l_0=\dfrac12\sqrt{(\Delta b_1)^2+(\Delta b_1)^2}\approx0.5\,{\rm cm}\,.$(9)

Finally

$\langle l_0\rangle=(8.5\pm0.5)\,{\rm cm}$(10)

The spring constant is calculated with the formula

$\begin{cases}a_1=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k}\\a_2=\gamma\dfrac{m_{el}g}k\end{cases}\ \Rightarrow\ a_1\cdot a_2=\left(\dfrac{m_{el}g}k\right)^2\ \Rightarrow\ k=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\sqrt{a_1\cdot a_2}}$(11)

Substituting the numerical data in SI units gives

$\bar k=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\sqrt{a_1\cdot a_2}}=\dfrac{0.100\cdot9.81}{\sqrt{3.66\cdot2.19\cdot10^{-2}}}=34.65\dfrac{\rm N}{\rm m}\,.$

The error of the value found is calculated with the formula

$\Delta k=\dfrac{\bar k}2\sqrt{(\varepsilon_{a1})^2+(\varepsilon_{a2})^2}=1.3\dfrac{\rm N}{\rm m}\,.$

Thus we finally obtain

$\bar k=(34.7\pm1.3)\dfrac{\rm N}{\rm m}$(12)

The coefficient of reduction of the tension of the thread can be calculated with the formula

$\begin{cases}a_1=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k}\\a_2=\gamma\dfrac{m_{el}g}k\end{cases}\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a_2}{a_1}=\gamma^2\ \Rightarrow\ \gamma=\sqrt{\dfrac{a_2}{a_1}}\,.$(13)

The numerical value of this coefficient

$\bar\gamma=\sqrt{\dfrac{a_2}{a_1}}=0.773\,,$

and its error

$\Delta\gamma=\dfrac{\bar\gamma}2\sqrt{(\varepsilon_{a1})^2+(\varepsilon_{a2})^2}=0.03\,.$(14)

Finally, the value

$\bar\gamma=0.77\pm0.03$(15)

The coefficient of friction of the thread on the rod can be found with the Euler formula

$\gamma=\exp(-\mu\alpha)\ \Rightarrow\ \mu=-\dfrac{\ln\gamma}\alpha$(15)

With the thread (1) vertical, the angle of wrap around the rod is $\alpha=\pi$. Taking this value into account we get:

$\bar\mu=-\dfrac{\ln\bar\gamma}\pi=0.0818\,.$(20)

The error of the value of the friction coefficient found has to be calculated with the formula

$\Delta\bar\mu=\dfrac{\Delta\bar\gamma}{\pi\bar\gamma}=0.012\,.$(21)

Finally

$\bar\mu=0.082\pm0.012$(20)

The coefficients $\gamma$ and $\mu$ can be calculated with formulas (13) and (15). The results of the calculations are given in the table.

1.6. To check the applicability of the Euler formula, it can be written on a semi-logarithmic scale:

$-\ln\gamma=\mu\alpha$(20)

If this formula holds, the dependence of the quantity $Y=-\ln\gamma$ on the angle $\alpha$ must be linear, and its graph must pass through the origin. The figure shows the graph of this dependence, which confirms that the Euler formula holds in this case.

1.7. To refine the value of the friction coefficient, the arithmetic mean of the three values calculated for the different positions of the thread can be computed.

$\langle\mu\rangle=\dfrac{\mu_1+\mu_3+\mu_2}3=0.0809\,.$

The error of this value is

$\Delta\langle\mu\rangle=\dfrac{\Delta\bar\mu}{\sqrt3}=0.007$

Therefore the final value of the friction coefficient should be taken as

$\langle\mu\rangle=0.081\pm0.007$

Part 2. Sliding friction on a horizontal surface.

2.1. The graphs show that the box does not start moving at once, but only when the mass of the suspended loads exceeds a certain value. Until then the coordinate of the box is $x_0=44.0\,{\rm cm}$, which is the answer to the question of this item.

2.2. It is not hard to write the equilibrium conditions of all the bodies of this setup and to obtain formulas for the measured coordinates of the boundaries of the stagnation zone.

When the suspended loads are slowly displaced down, we can write (see the figure, which shows the tension forces of the threads in the system)

$k(x_2-x_0)=\gamma m_1g-F\ \Rightarrow$
$x_2=x_0+\dfrac{\gamma m_1g-F}k$(21)

When the suspended loads are slowly displaced up, similar calculations lead to the result

$\gamma\left(k(x_1-x_0)-F\right)=m_1g\ \Rightarrow$
$x_1=x_0+\dfrac{\dfrac{m_1g}\gamma+F}k$(22)

2.3. We obtain the formulas for calculating the required parameters of the setup through the measured quantities (the coefficients of the linear dependences). For this we rewrite the theoretical formulas taking into account that the mass of the suspended loads is set by their number, $m_1=m_{el}n_1$.

Then for the left boundary of the stagnation zone we obtain

$x_2=x_0+\dfrac{\gamma m_1g-F}k=\dfrac{\gamma m_{el}g}kn_1+x_0-\dfrac Fk\,.$(23)

Comparing with the formula describing the experimental dependence,

$x_2=a_2n_1+b_2$

we find that the coefficients of this dependence are

$a_2=\dfrac{\gamma m_{el}g}k,\quad b_2=x_0-\dfrac Fk$(24)

Similarly for the right boundary of the stagnation zone:

$x_1=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k}n_1+\left(x_0+\dfrac Fk\right)=a_1n_1+b_1\,,$

from which it follows that

$a_1=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\gamma k},\quad b_1=\left(x_0+\dfrac Fk\right)$(25)

Now it is not hard to find that the parameters of the setup can be obtained from the coefficients of the experimental dependences as

$k=\dfrac{m_{el}g}{\sqrt{a_1\cdot a_2}}$(26)

Theoretically the dependence of the friction force on the number of loads in the box is given by the Coulomb–Amontons law

$F=\mu(m_{el}n_0+m_b)g\,.$(31)

From these formulas, the coefficient of friction of the box on the surface is

$\mu=\dfrac a{m_{el}g}\approx0.35\,.$(32)

And the mass of the box
