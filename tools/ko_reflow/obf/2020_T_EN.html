Question 7. The 1960 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Donald A. Glaser for the invention, in 1952, of the bubble chamber. Although now superseded by other equipment, the bubble chamber played a fundamental role in the field of particle physics. Essentially, the bubble chamber makes it possible to observe the trajectories of moving charged particles, because they leave their “trail” of bubbles in, usually, superheated liquid hydrogen. Suppose that, in a bubble chamber, the decay of a subatomic particle that forms an electron and a positron is captured. Suppose that this decay is observed in a reference frame in which the subatomic particle is initially at rest and in which there is a uniform magnetic field $\vec B$, of magnitude $B = 2.50$ mT, perpendicular to the plane of motion of the resulting electron and positron. Determine the time interval, in s, between the decay and the instant at which the electron and the positron collide. (Neglect the Coulomb interaction between the electron and the positron and the energy losses due to the interaction of the particles with the liquid hydrogen of the bubble chamber.)
