Question 3 – A certain amount of ideal gas is enclosed in the chamber formed by a piston fitted to a vertical cylinder. Initially, the position of the piston is held fixed by a pin, so that the initial pressure of the gas is $p_0 = 1.00\ \mathrm{atm}$ and its volume is 5.00 litres. The external pressure on the piston, $p_{\mathrm{ext}} = 10.0\ \mathrm{atm}$, is given by the sum of the atmospheric pressure and the pressure exerted by a mass $m$ resting on its outer surface. The walls of the cylinder are diathermal, and the assembly is partially immersed in a mixture of water and ice in equilibrium with each other and with the atmosphere (all the subsystems are at a temperature of $0\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$). At a certain moment, the pin is removed and the system is allowed to reach a new equilibrium state. What mass of ice melts in this process?

Question 4 – The paramagnetism of materials can be explained by the alignment, or lack of alignment, of the magnetic dipole moments of their atoms. In a simplified picture, in a convention in which the Cartesian $z$ axis points upward, the $z$ component of the magnetic dipole moment can take only the values $+\mu_0$ (up) or $-\mu_0$ (down), where $\mu_0$ is the magnitude of the magnetic dipole moment of each atom. In the absence of an external magnetic field, the magnetic dipole moments of the atoms are randomly oriented, so that the magnetization $M$ of the system, given by the sum of the magnetic dipole moments, is zero. In the presence of a magnetic field pointing in the $z$ direction, $M$ is nonzero, because the magnetic dipoles tend to align with the external field. However, the alignment is not complete, because in addition to the interaction with the field there are thermal interactions that favour disorder. In paramagnetic materials, the strength of the external field $B$ and the temperature $T$ determine the probability that a magnetic dipole is aligned with the field. Consider a particular situation in which the probability that a magnetic dipole is oriented in the direction of the field is $p = 3/4$. What is the probability that a group of 6 atoms has magnetization $M = 2\mu_0$?
