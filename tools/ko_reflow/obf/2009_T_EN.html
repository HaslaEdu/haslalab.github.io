01. a) A circular hoop of radius $R$ and mass $m$, uniformly distributed, rolls without slipping, in uniform motion, on a horizontal plane, as shown in figure 1.

Considering that the motion of the hoop can be described as the composition of the uniform rectilinear motion of its centre of mass combined with a uniform rotation about this same point, determine, in terms of $m$ and $v$, the total kinetic energy of the hoop.

b) With two hoops identical to the one in the previous item and a rigid rod of length $L$ and negligible mass, a spool was built, sketched below in figure 2. The spokes that support the rod, connecting it rigidly to the hoops, are not shown, and their masses are also negligible. Consider that the spool is initially in uniform motion with velocity $v$ on a horizontal plane and, after a certain time, begins to climb an inclined plane. Figure 3 shows a cross-section of the planes and of the spool.

Determine the maximum height that the rod reaches relative to the horizontal plane when the spool reaches zero velocity. Also determine the deceleration of the spool during the climb. Express your results in terms of variables chosen from among the quantities $m$, $v$, $R$, $L$ and $g$ (acceleration due to gravity).

02. A small metal sphere of mass $m$ was released together with a spherical rubber ball of mass M and radius R, as shown in figure 4.

The mass $M$ is much smaller than $m$, and the volume of the metal sphere is negligible compared with that of the rubber ball. Assuming that: the centres of mass of the small sphere and of the ball always move along the same vertical line; the system strikes the ground and all the collisions involved are perfectly elastic; the vertical distance travelled by the small sphere is much greater than the deformation of the rubber ball; air resistance is negligible in this problem, determine:

a) The approximate velocity with which the small sphere separates from the ball on the way up.

b) The vertical distance travelled by the small sphere on the way up as a function of the distance it travelled on the way down.

03. Figure 5 shows a cubic unit cell of a sodium chloride crystal, with edge $a = 5.6{\cdot}10^{-10}$ m.

a) Determine the volume density of positive electric charge, in C/cm3, due to the Na+ ion, remembering that the elementary charge is 1.6·10-19 C.

b) Consider two point charges of equal magnitude, one positive and the other negative, separated by 0.5 cm, initially at rest. The magnitude of each charge is equal to the amount of charge contained in one cm3 in the answer to the previous item. Determine the energy needed to separate these charges to an infinite distance.

04. Two flat, parallel, square conducting plates of side $d$, separated by a distance $a$ much smaller than $d$, are arranged in isolation, forming a parallel-plate capacitor. One of the plates is grounded and the other is connected by a conducting wire to a conducting sphere of radius $R$. Figure 6 shows a sketch of this system.

A positive electric charge $Q$ was placed on the upper plate of the capacitor. In the situation of electrostatic equilibrium, consider the medium between the plates to be vacuum, and consider that edge effects are negligible, as is the strength of the electric field of any one element (sphere, capacitor or ground) on any other. Determine:

a) The fraction of Q that remains on the upper plate.

b) The magnitude of the electric field at a distance 2R from the centre of the sphere. Express your results as functions of the quantities given in the statement and of universal constants where appropriate.

05. A photon of frequency $f$ has an effective inertial mass m = energy/c, where $c$ is the speed of light. A photon emitted at the surface of a star, as it escapes from the gravitational field, loses energy and its frequency decreases, considering that inertial mass is equal to gravitational mass. Taking into account the change in gravitational potential energy of the star–photon system, determine an expression for the relative change in the frequency of the photon on leaving the star, $\Delta f/f$, in terms of $M$, $R$, $G$ and $c$, where $M$ and $R$ are, respectively, the mass and the radius of the star, and $G$ is the universal gravitational constant.

06. The figure below shows two homogeneous plates with parallel faces, which serve as a heat-conducting medium between two thermal reservoirs at temperatures $T_1 = 100\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $T_2 = 200\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The surfaces of the plates transverse to the flow have equal areas $A$. In addition, the plates have thicknesses $e_1$ and $e_2$ and are made of materials of thermal conductivity k1 and k2, respectively.

a) Determine the temperature profile T(x) inside the plates, considering steady-state conduction, $e_2 = 2e_1$ and $k_2 = 3k_1$.

b) In engineering it is common to introduce a different conceptual point of view for Fourier’s Law. Temperature is regarded as a potential function for the heat flow, and Fourier’s equation takes the form heat flow = (thermal potential difference)/(thermal resistance), which is similar to Ohm’s law in the theory of electric circuits. Define, appropriately, what thermal resistance is in this context and determine the equivalent thermal resistance of the system presented in the previous item.

07. A luminous object and a projection screen are located a distance $L$ from each other. A converging lens of focal length $f$ less than $L/4$ is placed between the screen and the object in such a way that the image of the object is projected onto the screen. Verify that there are two possible positions for the lens, separated by a distance $a$.

a) Determine $a$ in terms of $L$ and $f$.

b) Determine the ratio between the transverse linear magnifications corresponding to the two possible positions of the lens, in terms of $L$ and $a$.
