Question 1. Eratosthenes of Cyrene (3rd century BC) determined the radius of the Earth from one observation and two hypotheses. The phenomenon that caught his attention is that, at the same instant, the direction of incidence of the Sun’s rays varies with the latitude of the observation point. To explain it, he assumed the Sun’s rays to be parallel and the Earth to be spherical. Inspired by this approach, students from Macapá ($M$) and Porto Alegre ($P$), cities approximately on the same meridian, decided to reproduce the experiment. Macapá lies practically on the Equator, and Porto Alegre near the $30^\circ$ South parallel. A direct flight between the two cities, by the shortest route, covers $d = 3\,300\ \mathrm{km}$. Assume that the experiment is done at noon on the equinox, when the Sun shines perpendicularly on Macapá.

(a)Approximately what is the angle of incidence of the Sun’s rays in Porto Alegre?

(b)Determine the radius of the Earth from Eratosthenes’ hypotheses and the experimental results. Justify your result with a diagram.

(c)The same observation can be explained by a flat-Earth model in which the Sun is a point light source at a height $H$ above the plane of the Earth and lies exactly above Macapá at noon on the equinox. Determine $H$. Justify your result with a diagram.

(d)If the flat-Earth model is able to explain the difference in the angle of incidence of the Sun’s rays in different cities, why is it not adopted?
