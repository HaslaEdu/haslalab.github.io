Hints: The entropy change associated with the change in temperature of a body of mass $m$ and specific heat $c$ that goes from a temperature $T_0$ to $T$ is

where ln is the natural logarithm.

You may also use the inequality $\ln(1+x) < x$, for all $x > 1$ and different from 0.

Question 7 – Consider a point charge $q$ at a distance $d$ from the centre of a metal sphere of radius $R$, as shown in the figure below.

To find, for example, the electric fields at each point generated by the configuration above, one could determine the charge distribution on the surface of the conductor (due to the presence of $q$) and take the field at each point to be the sum of the field of $q$ and the contributions of each element of charge on the surface of the sphere. But this seems rather complicated! A very powerful method, called the method of images, can be very efficient for solving problems like this. According to this method, given a conducting object charged with a charge $Q$, we can remove this charge from its surface and stop considering the existence of the conductor, redistributing this charge within the space enclosed by its surface, provided that this surface remains equipotential. In this case, the electric field at any point outside the conductor generated by the charge distribution on its surface is identical to the one generated by this new distribution of image charges. The reverse path is also valid, that is, given a distribution of charges inside an (imaginary) equipotential surface, we can say that if a conducting shell (or even a solid conductor) were placed in the position of this surface and this internal charge were “injected” into the shell, then the external fields (outside the surface) would remain unchanged. Assume that the medium is vacuum ($\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0$).

a)Suppose that the conducting sphere is earthed, that is, its potential is zero. In this case only one image charge $q'$ will be needed and, by symmetry, one can see that it must lie on the line joining the charge $q$ to the centre of the sphere.
