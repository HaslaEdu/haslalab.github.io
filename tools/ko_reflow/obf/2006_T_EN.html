Question 01) The device shown consists of a cylindrical container that holds a piston “E” inside it. Both are thermally insulated and have negligible heat capacity. The piston has mass $m_E = 300\ \mathrm{kg}$, slides without friction and has a circular base of area $S = 100\ \mathrm{cm^2}$. In the region represented by the letter “A” there is $1\ \mathrm{mol}$ ($4.00\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg}$) of helium gas at temperature $T_0 = 300\ \mathrm{K}$, able to balance the pressure exerted by the weight $P_E$ of the piston added to the atmospheric pressure $p_{atm} = 1.00\ \mathrm{atm}$, thus keeping the length “a” equal to $0.600\ \mathrm{m}$. At a given instant the plug is connected to a source of constant voltage equal to $110\ \mathrm{V}$ in order to heat the gas by means of the resistor $R$. The resistor is kept on until the piston, owing to the expansion of the gas, has undergone a total displacement equal to $2b$. Piece “F”, of mass $m_F = 100\ \mathrm{kg}$, simply rests on the top of the container and will be pushed up as the piston rises. This piece has a hole that keeps the air in region “B” at a pressure equal to the atmospheric pressure. Assuming that $c_P$ is the specific heat of helium for isobaric processes, that $c_V = 3125\ \mathrm{J/(kg{\cdot}{}^\circ C)}$ is the specific heat for isochoric processes, that the ratio $\gamma = c_P/c_V$ equals $1.664$, that the length “b” equals $0.200\ \mathrm{m}$ and that the electrical resistance of the resistor $R$ is $1210\ \Omega$, calculate:

a) the quantity of heat $Q_1$ absorbed by the gas for the piston E just to touch piece F.

b) the time interval $\Delta t$ for which the resistor must remain on.

c) the efficiency $\eta$ of the device, considering the work done and the electrical energy consumed.

Question 02) A student improvised a battery “charger” to “charge” the battery of his telephone. By means of a few tests, he found that his “charger” had a constant electromotive force (emf) $E_1 = 4\ \mathrm{V}$ associated with an internal resistance $r_1 = 5\ \Omega$. The battery of his telephone has an emf $E_2$ that varies from $3.2\ \mathrm{V}$ (discharged) to $3.6\ \mathrm{V}$ (charged) and a constant internal resistance $r_2 = 1\ \Omega$. Since, according to the battery manufacturer’s manual, the maximum charging current must not exceed $80\ \mathrm{mA}$ (“safe” charging), the student places a resistor $R$ in the circuit to meet this requirement. The diagram represents the assembly that was made and shows the terminals of the charger (points A and B) and the terminals of the battery (points C and D). Determine:

a) the value of the resistance of the resistor $R$ that allows the safe “charging” of the battery in the shortest possible time for this circuit.

b) the potential difference between C and D at the end of charging.

Question 03) A hollow, conducting spherical body of negligible thickness and with zero total charge has radius $R_B = 50.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ and surrounds a spherical body, concentric with it, which is a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, has radius $R_A = 10.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ and holds an electric charge $Q_A = 1.00\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ of positive electricity. At a given instant a small particle of mass $m = 2.00\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{g}$ carrying a charge $q = 1.00\ \mathrm{nC}$ of positive electricity leaves the surface of the inner body and accelerates; since the larger body has a hole, the particle passes through it and is detected moving at the position $x = 100.0\ \mathrm{cm}$, as shown. The whole system is in vacuum. Under these conditions:

a) calculate the magnitude of the electric force on the particle at the instant it passes through a point between the conductors located $20.0\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m}$ from their centre.

b) calculate the speed of the particle as it passes through the position $x = 100.0\ \mathrm{cm}$.

Question 04) Two fighter planes A and B fly along a straight, horizontal path and are aligned. When they are $600\ \mathrm{m}$ apart, the one behind starts a sequence of shots at the other, at a rate of 1 projectile every quarter of a second. The speed of the projectiles $v_{P/A}$, relative to plane A, is constant and equal to $500\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and, since their flight time is very short, the fall of the projectiles due to gravity is negligible in the analysis of this situation. Considering that the plane behind flies with speed $v_A = 100\ \mathrm{m/s}$, that the speed of the one in front is $v_B = 120\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and that these speeds are constant, calculate:

a) the time the first projectile fired takes to reach the plane in front.

b) the distance between two projectiles fired consecutively.

c) the number of projectiles per second that hit the aircraft in front.

Question 05) The cannon shown fires a grenade of mass $m = 6.00\ \mathrm{kg}$ from the position $O(x_O;y_O)$ ($0\ \mathrm{m}$; $0\ \mathrm{m}$), which reaches its highest point at the position $P(x_P;y_P)$ with coordinates ($3000\ \mathrm{m}$; $1125\ \mathrm{m}$). $20.0\ \mathrm{s}$ after the shot, the grenade explodes and its fragments “a” and “b”, of masses $m_a = 2.00\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $m_b = 4.00\ \mathrm{kg}$ respectively, fall along trajectories coplanar with the trajectory before the explosion. Neglect air resistance and calculate:

a) the coordinates of the point of explosion;

b) the position coordinates $A(x_A;y_A)$ of fragment “a” at the instant when fragment “b”, $1.0$ second after the explosion, hits the ground at a point $B(x_B;y_B)$, whose position is given by the coordinates ($3000\ \mathrm{m}$; $300\ \mathrm{m}$);

c) the value, in N, of the force $F$ of the explosion, constant and lasting $1\ \mathrm{ms}$, that acted on fragment A. (leave the square root indicated)

Question 06) The truck shown in the figure carries a steel coil. The coefficients of static friction $\mu_E$ and of kinetic friction $\mu_C$ between the coil and the truck bed are $0.18$ and $0.15$ respectively. Consider that the truck is moving with a speed of $20\ \mathrm{m/s}$ on a road in two different situations: the first, on a horizontal stretch of the road that has a circular curve with the track banked sideways (fig. 1), and the second (fig. 2), on a straight, horizontal stretch of the road.

a) Calculate, in the first case, the smallest radius of curvature of the track taken by the truck that allows it to complete the curve without its load sliding on the truck bed.

b) Calculate, in the second case, the speed with which the steel coil collides with the cab of the vehicle when the truck is forced to brake with a constant deceleration and stop in exactly $10\ \mathrm{s}$.

Question 07) The device shown contains pulleys coupled as illustrated. The larger pulleys have an angular velocity $\omega$ and are linked by a bar on which a body of mass $m$ simply rests. The distance from the axis of rotation of the pulleys to the pin that connects them to the bar is $R$, and the coefficient of static friction between the body and the bar is $\mu$. With these data, develop an equation that gives the maximum value of $\omega$:

a) for the body to remain resting on the bar when it is at the highest point of its trajectory.

b) for the body to remain resting on the bar, without sliding, when it is at the intermediate point of the trajectory, descending, between the highest point and the lowest point.

Question 08) In vacuum, a particle of mass $1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{kg}$ and positive electric charge $q = 10.0\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ moves at $1.30\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ towards a screen. During its path it is subjected to a magnetic field, uniform and vertical, pointing downwards, of induction $B = 0.50\ \mathrm{T}$. Since the travel time is too short for the effects of the gravitational field to be considered,

a) calculate the travel time of the particle from the point where it is shown to the point of impact.

b) calculate the coordinates $(x;y)$ of the point of impact.
