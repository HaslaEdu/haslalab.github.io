Question 1 – The escape velocity of the Earth is the minimum velocity required for a body at its surface to be launched and no longer be affected by the gravitational attraction of the Earth. Determine the value of the escape velocity for a body at the surface of the Earth.

Question 2 – Two pendulums of the same length $\boldsymbol{L}$ are set up as shown in the diagram below. At the instant $t = 0$ the pendulum of mass $\boldsymbol{M}$ is placed at a height $\boldsymbol{h}$ relative to the horizontal, and the pendulum of mass $\boldsymbol{m}$ remains at rest in the vertical position.

When released, mass $\boldsymbol{M}$ starts to move and collides with mass $\boldsymbol{m}$ ($\boldsymbol{M > m}$). (Neglect all effects due to any kind of friction in this system.)

a)Determine the maximum height, relative to the horizontal, that masses $\boldsymbol{M}$ and $\boldsymbol{m}$ reach after the collision. Use $\boldsymbol{g}$ for the local gravitational acceleration.

b)How much time will elapse after the first collision between the masses until the masses collide again?

Question 3 – A tourist on a trip bought a camera with a focal length of 35 mm that uses a film 36 mm wide. At a certain moment he sees a boat 12 m long and wants to take a picture of it, but he notices that, at that position, the image of the boat fills only ¼ of the width of the film.

a)What is the distance between the tourist and the boat?

b)What must the distance between the tourist and the boat be for the image to fill the whole width of the film?

Question 4 - A certain manufacturer of freezers states that a given model has an annual consumption of 730 kWh.

a)Assuming that the freezer operates 5 hours out of the 24 hours of the day, what power does it consume when in operation?

b)If the freezer keeps the temperature inside it at $-5\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in an environment at $20\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$, what is the maximum theoretical performance of this model?

c)What is the maximum amount of ice that this freezer can produce in 1 hour, starting from water at $20\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

Question 5 – A 250 kg weight hangs from the ceiling of a room by a thin copper wire. The system consisting of the wire and the weight has its fundamental mode of vibration at 440 Hz. The temperature of the room increases by $40\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Given that the coefficient of linear expansion of copper is $1.7\times10^{-5}\ {}^\circ\mathrm{C}^{-1}$, make a reasonable estimate of the change in the frequency of the fundamental mode of vibration of the system with the increase in temperature.

Question 8 – One of the methods used by archaeologists to study the eating habits of ancient indigenous populations in the Americas is based on the technique known as time-of-flight mass spectrometry, using the carbon isotopes 12C (mass $\boldsymbol{m_1}$) and 13C (mass $\boldsymbol{m_2}$) collected from the remains of human bones. The technique of mass spectrometry consists in first ionizing the species to be studied, that is, making the isotopes lose one electron each, and then accelerating them to a certain velocity by means of a potential difference. The ionized atoms, accelerated by a potential difference $\boldsymbol{V}$, are injected into a region of uniform magnetic field $\boldsymbol{B}$, perpendicular to the velocity of the isotopes.

a)Determine the radii $\boldsymbol{R_1}$ and $\boldsymbol{R_2}$ of the orbits for the two species 12C and 13C, respectively, when they are injected into the region of uniform magnetic field $\boldsymbol{B}$.

b)Obtain the value of the radius $\boldsymbol{R_2}$ as a function of $\boldsymbol{R_1}$, $\boldsymbol{m_1}$ and $\boldsymbol{m_2}$.
