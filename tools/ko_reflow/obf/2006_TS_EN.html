Question 01 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The situation presented deals with endothermic gas processes. It consists of an isobaric process, followed by an isochoric one, and ends with an isobaric one.

a)

I) calculation of $T_1$ (isobaric, $0 \rightarrow 1$)

$V_0/T_0 = V_1/T_1 \;\rightarrow\; S{\cdot} a/T_0 = S{\cdot}(a+b)/T_1 \;\therefore\; T_1 = S{\cdot}(a+b){\cdot} T_0/S{\cdot} a$

$T_1 = (0.6+0.2){\cdot}300/0.6 \qquad\rightarrow\qquad \boldsymbol{T_1 = 400}\,\mathbf{K}$

II) calculation of $Q_1$ (isobaric, $0 \rightarrow 1$)

$\gamma = c_p/c_v \qquad c_p = 1.664{\cdot}3125 \qquad c_p = 5200\ \mathrm{J/kg{\cdot}K}$

$Q_1 = m{\cdot} c_p{\cdot}(T_1 - T_0) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; Q_1 = 4{\cdot}10^{-3}{\cdot}5200{\cdot}(400-300) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{Q_1 = 2080}\,\mathbf{J}$

b)

III) calculation of $T_2$ (isochoric, $1 \rightarrow 2$)

$P_1/T_1 = p_2/T_2 \;\;\therefore\;\; T_2 = (p_{atm} + (P_E + P_F)/S)\,/\,(p_{atm} + P_E/S){\cdot} T_1 \quad\rightarrow$

$T_2 = (1{\cdot}10^5 + (300{\cdot}10 + 100{\cdot}10)/100{\cdot}10^{-4})/(1{\cdot}10^5 + 300{\cdot}10/100{\cdot}10^{-4}){\cdot}400 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{T_2 = 500}\,\mathbf{K}$

IV) calculation of $Q_2$ (isochoric, $1 \rightarrow 2$)

$Q_2 = m{\cdot} c_v{\cdot}(T_2 - T_1) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; Q_2 = 4{\cdot}10^{-3}{\cdot}3125{\cdot}(500-400) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{Q_2 = 1250}\,\mathbf{J}$

V) calculation of $T_3$ (isobaric, $2 \rightarrow 3$)

$V_2/T_2 = V_3/T_3 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; S{\cdot}(a+b)/T_2 = S{\cdot}(a+2{\cdot} b)/T_3 \;\;\therefore\;\; T_3 = (0.6+2{\cdot}0.2){\cdot}500/(0.6{\cdot}0.2) \quad\rightarrow$

$\boldsymbol{T_3 = 625}\,\mathbf{K}$

VI) calculation of $Q_3$ (isobaric, $2 \rightarrow 3$)

$Q_3 = m{\cdot} c_p{\cdot}(T_3 - T_2) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; Q_3 = 4{\cdot}10^{-3}{\cdot}5200{\cdot}(625-400) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{Q_3 = 2600}\,\mathbf{J}$

VII) calculation of the time $\Delta t$ for which the resistor is on. (The resistor converts all the electrical energy into heat, and all of this heat is fully absorbed by the gas.)

$Q_{total} = P_{electric}{\cdot}\Delta t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; Q_{total} = V^2{\cdot}\Delta t/R \;\;\rightarrow\;\; Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 = 110^2{\cdot}\Delta t/1210$

$2080 + 1250 + 2600 = 110^2{\cdot}\Delta t/12 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{\Delta t = 593}\,\mathbf{s}$

c)

VIII) calculation of the efficiency $\eta$

$\eta =$ work done/energy consumed   $\eta = [p_0{\cdot}(V_1 - V_0) + p_2{\cdot}(V_3 - V_2)]/Q_{total} \quad\rightarrow$

$\eta = [4{\cdot}10^5 + ((2{\cdot}10^{-3}) + 5{\cdot}10^5 + ((2{\cdot}10^{-3})]/5930 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \eta = 1800/5930 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \eta = 0.3035$

Question 02 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The proposed situation deals with single-loop circuits involving a generator, a receiver and a resistor. Since $E_1 > E_2$, the electric current will flow clockwise (in the diagram shown). Hence it can be stated that $E_1$ is a generator and $E_2$ a receiver. Thus $E_2$ receives electrical energy and stores it as chemical potential energy, as happens in rechargeable batteries.

For the shortest possible charging time, the electric current must be the maximum allowed.

a)

I) calculation of $R$.

$\Sigma E = \Sigma R{\cdot} i \;\;\rightarrow\;\; E_1 - E_{2o} = r_1{\cdot} i_o + R{\cdot} i_o + r_2{\cdot} i_o \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 4 - 3.2 = 5{\cdot}80{\cdot}10^{-3} + R{\cdot}80{\cdot}10^{-3} + 1{\cdot}80{\cdot}10^{-3}$

$0.8 = 0.40 + R{\cdot}80{\cdot}10^{-3} + 0.08 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{R = 4}\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}$

b)

II) calculation of $i_f$

$E_1 - E_{2f} = r_1{\cdot} i_f + R{\cdot} i_f + r_2{\cdot} i_f \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 4 - 3.6 = 5{\cdot} i_f + R{\cdot} i_f + 1{\cdot} i_f \qquad \boldsymbol{i_f = 40}\,\mathbf{mA}$

III) calculation of $V_{cdf}$

$V_{CDf} = E_{2f} + r_2{\cdot} i_f = 3.6 + 1{\cdot}40{\cdot}10^{-3} = \boldsymbol{3.64}\,\mathbf{V}$

Question 03 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The phenomenon involves knowledge of total induction. The inner body holds $1\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ of positive electricity and the body surrounding it, being neutral, will have, by total induction, $1\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ of negative electricity on its inner side and $1\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ of positive electricity on its outer side. The electric field between the two and outside is a divergent radial field, and its variation follows the inverse-square law of distance. The discontinuity of the graph at $r = 50\ \mathrm{cm}$ comes from the fact that this is precisely where the outer conducting body is located.

a)

I) calculation of the field strength for $r = 20\ \mathrm{cm}$

$E_{20} = (1/4\pi\varepsilon_o){\cdot} Q/r^2 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; E_{20} = 9{\cdot}10^9{\cdot}1{\cdot}10^{-6}/(20{\cdot}10^{-2})^2 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; E_{20} = 225\ \mathrm{kV/m}$

II) calculation of the force on the particle

$F_{20} = E_{20}{\cdot} q \quad\rightarrow \boldsymbol{F_{20} = 2.25{\cdot}10^{-4}}\,\mathbf{N}$

b)

III) calculation of the potential of the field at the surface of the inner conductor

$V_A = (1/4\pi\varepsilon_o){\cdot} Q/r_A \;\;\rightarrow\;\; V_A = 9{\cdot}10^9{\cdot}1{\cdot}10^{-6}/10{\cdot}10^{-2} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{V_A = 90000}\,\mathbf{V}$

IV) calculation of the potential at the point 100 cm from the centre of the system

$V_B = (1/4\pi\varepsilon_o){\cdot} Q/r_B \;\;\rightarrow\;\; V_A = 9{\cdot}10^9{\cdot}1{\cdot}10^{-6}/100{\cdot}10^{-2} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{V_A = 9000}\,\mathbf{V}$

V) calculation of the speed of the particle

$\tau = V_{AB}{\cdot} q$  but  $\tau = \Delta E_C \;\;\rightarrow\;\; (V_A - V_B){\cdot} q = (m{\cdot} v_B^2)/2 - (m{\cdot} v_A^2)/2 \;\therefore\; v_B = [2{\cdot}(V_A - V_B){\cdot} q/m]^{1/2}$

$v_B = [2{\cdot}(90000 - 9000){\cdot}1{\cdot}10^{-9}/2{\cdot}10^{-10}]^{1/2} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{v_B = 900}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

Question 04 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The situation represented involves knowledge of relative motion between several moving bodies. The use of the equations associated with the Doppler–Fizeau effect, adapted to the proposed case, was accepted for solving this problem.

a)

I) calculation of the projectile’s travel time to plane B from the equations of motion

projectile

$x_P = x_{0P} + v_P{\cdot} t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x_P = 0 + 600{\cdot} t$

plane B

$x_B = x_{0B} + v_B{\cdot} t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x_P = 600 + 120{\cdot} t$

they meet when  $x_P = x_B \qquad 600{\cdot} t = 600 + 120{\cdot} t \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{t = 1.25}\,\mathbf{s}$

b)

II) calculation of the distance between consecutive projectiles

If the source were at rest, in $0.25\ \mathrm{s}$ the projectiles would be $d = v_P{\cdot} t$ apart, or $d = 150\ \mathrm{m}$

But in $0.25\ \mathrm{s}$ the source (plane A), and therefore the next projectile, moves $\Delta d = v_A{\cdot}0.25$, or

$\Delta d = 100{\cdot}0.25 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \Delta d = 25\ \mathrm{m}$

Therefore the distance between consecutive projectiles is $d' = d - \Delta d$, or $\boldsymbol{d' = 125}\,\mathbf{m}$

c)

III) calculation of the frequency with which the projectiles hit aircraft B

The velocity of the projectiles relative to plane B is:

$v_{P/B} = v_P - v_B = 600 - 120 \qquad\rightarrow\qquad v_{P/B} = 480\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Since they are $125\ \mathrm{m}$ apart from one another, the time interval between their impacts on plane B is:

$d' = v_{P/B}{\cdot}\Delta t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 125 = 480{\cdot}\Delta t \;\;\therefore\;\; \Delta t = 125/480$  or  $\Delta t = 25/96$ seconds per projectile.

This also means 96 projectiles every $25\ \mathrm{s}$ or, which is the same, 3.84 projectiles per second.

Question 05 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The problem situation presented is solved by applying Galileo’s principle of the independence of motions and the knowledge that the internal forces of a system are unable to change the trajectory of the centre of mass (CM) of that system; it also involves the concepts of the impulse of a force and of momentum.

a)

I) calculation of the initial vertical velocity

$v_{Py}^2 = v_{0y}^2 + 2{\cdot} g{\cdot} y_P \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 0^2 = v_{0y}^2 + 2{\cdot}(-10){\cdot}1125 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{v_{0y} = 150}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

II) calculation of the rise time

$v_{Py} = v_{0y} + g{\cdot} t_P \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 0 = 150 + (-10){\cdot} t_P \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{t_P = 15}\,\mathbf{s}$

III) calculation of the horizontal velocity (constant)

$x_P = x_0 + v_x{\cdot} t_P \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 3000 = 0 + v_x{\cdot}15 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{v_x = 200}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

IV) calculation of the position at the instant of the explosion ($t = 20\ \mathrm{s}$)

$x_{20} = x_0 + v_x{\cdot} t_{20} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x_{20} = 0 + 200{\cdot}20 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x_{20} = 4000\ \mathrm{m}$

$y_{20} = y_0 + v_{0y}{\cdot} t_{20} + (1/2){\cdot}(g{\cdot} t_{20}^2) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; y_{20} = 0 + 150{\cdot}20 + (1/2){\cdot}((-10){\cdot}20^2) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{y_{20} = 1000}\,\mathbf{m}$

b)

V) calculation of the position of the centre of mass (CM) of the system 1 second after the instant of the explosion ($t = 21\ \mathrm{s}$)

$x_{21} = x_0 + v_x{\cdot} t_{21} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x_{21} = 0 + 200{\cdot}21 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{x_{21} = 4200}\,\mathbf{m}$

$y_{21} = y_0 + v_{0y}{\cdot} t_{21} + (1/2){\cdot}(g{\cdot} t_{21}^2) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; y_{21} = 0 + 150{\cdot}21 + (1/2){\cdot}((-10){\cdot}21^2) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{y_{21} = 945}\,\mathbf{m}$

VI) calculation of the position of fragment A at the instant $t = 21\ \mathrm{s}$ from the position of fragment B and of the centre of mass of the system

$x_{21} = m_A{\cdot} x_{A21} + m_B{\cdot} x_{B21}/(m_A + m_B) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 4200 = 2{\cdot} x_{A21} + 4{\cdot}3000/(2+4) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{x_{A21} = 6600}\,\mathbf{m}$

$y_{21} = m_A{\cdot} y_{A21} + m_B{\cdot} y_{B21}/(m_A + m_B) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 945 = 2{\cdot} y_{A21} + 4{\cdot}300/(2+4) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{y_{A21} = 2235}\,\mathbf{m}$

Question 06 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The problem deals with two situations, both critical:

I) the first deals with impending sliding when the truck goes round the curve (the diagram shows this situation). Since the load cannot start to slide, we have:

a)

$\rightarrow$ vertical axis: (equilibrium along the vertical axis)

$N{\cdot}\cos\alpha - F_A{\cdot}\sin\alpha - P = 0$    or

$m{\cdot} g = N{\cdot}\cos\alpha - \mu{\cdot} N{\cdot}\sin\alpha \qquad (1)$

$\rightarrow$ horizontal axis: (the centripetal force ($F_C$) is the resultant horizontal force)

$F_C = N{\cdot}\sin\alpha + F_A{\cdot}\cos\alpha$    or

$mv^2/R = N{\cdot}\sin\alpha + \mu{\cdot} N{\cdot}\cos\alpha \qquad (2)$

dividing (1) by (2):

$R = (v^2/g){\cdot}[\cos\alpha - \mu{\cdot}\sin\alpha]/[\sin\alpha + \mu{\cdot}\cos\alpha]$

$R = (20^2/10){\cdot}[(12/13) - 0.18{\cdot}(5/13)]/[(5/13) + 0.18{\cdot}(12/13)]$

$R = (20^2/10){\cdot}[12 - 0.18{\cdot}5]/[5 + 0.18{\cdot}12] \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{R = 62.01}\,\mathbf{m}$

b)

II) the second deals with braking on a straight, horizontal path.

Since the deceleration of the truck is fixed, the student should check whether the static friction force ensures a deceleration equal to that of the truck. The deceleration of the truck is:

$v = v_0 + a{\cdot} t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 0 = 20 + a{\cdot}10 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{a = -2}\,\mathbf{m/s^2}$

III) The friction force can provide the acceleration $a_E$

$F_{AE} = m{\cdot} a_E \;\rightarrow\; \mu_E{\cdot} N = m{\cdot} a_E \;\rightarrow\; \mu_E{\cdot} m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot} a_E \;\therefore\; a_E = -1.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ (the coil slides, and its deceleration $a_B$ is provided by the kinetic (or sliding) friction force)

IV) calculation of the deceleration

$F_{AC} = m{\cdot} a_B \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \mu_C{\cdot} N = m{\cdot} a_B \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \mu_C{\cdot} m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot} a_B \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{a_B = -1.5}\,\mathbf{m/s^2}$

V) The speed with which the coil hits the cab is the relative velocity between the coil and the cab, $v_{B/C}$

$v_{B/C}^2 = v_{0B/C}^2 + 2{\cdot} a_{B/C}{\cdot} x_{A/C} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; v_{B/C}^2 = (v_{0B} - v_{0C})^2 + 2{\cdot}(a_B - a_C){\cdot} x_{A/C} \quad\rightarrow$

$v_{B/C}^2 = (0)^2 + 2{\cdot}[(-1.5) - (-2)]{\cdot}2 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{v_{B/C} = \sqrt2}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

Question 07 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

The problem deals with the discussion of the stability of the body in two extreme situations. At the highest point of the trajectory (fig. 1)

stability exists as long as there is some minimum value greater than zero for the normal reaction $N$. The student should note that at this point there is only the vertical component of the acceleration. Therefore:

a)

I) calculation of the vertical acceleration $a_{max1}$

$P - N = m{\cdot} a \;\;\rightarrow\;\; m{\cdot} g - N = m{\cdot} a \;\;\rightarrow\;\; m{\cdot} g - 0 = m{\cdot} a_{max1}$

II) calculation of the maximum angular velocity $\omega_{max1}$

$m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot}\omega_{max1}^2{\cdot} R \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{\omega_{max1} = \sqrt{(g/R)}}$

b)

At the intermediate point (fig. 2), analysing along a horizontal axis, the body is at the point of maximum elongation, minimum velocity and maximum acceleration. Then stability is guaranteed if the static friction force can fully play the role of the centripetal force. The student should note that at this point there is only the horizontal component of the acceleration. Thus:

III) calculation of the velocity, vertical acceleration $a_{max2}$

$F_A = m{\cdot} a \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \mu{\cdot} m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot} a \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \mu{\cdot} m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot} a_{max2}$

IV) calculation of the maximum angular velocity $\omega_{max2}$

$\mu{\cdot} m{\cdot} g = m{\cdot}\omega_{max2}^2{\cdot} R \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{\omega_{max2} = \sqrt{(\mu{\cdot} g/R)}}$

Question 08 - 3rd year (6.0 points)

This is a problem involving Galileo’s principle of the superposition of motions, in which a charged particle travels inside a uniform magnetic field. Since its velocity makes an angle with the field lines, the student should consider two superposed motions: a vertical one under the action of a zero resultant force, and a horizontal one under the action of a force that is perpendicular to it and imposes on it a uniform circular motion. The angle $\alpha$ defines the direction of the initial velocity with respect to a horizontal line of the vertical plane perpendicular to the screen, and the angle $\beta$ defines, in the horizontal plane, the sector of the circular arc, from the starting position to the arrival position, described by the particle under the action of the magnetic force.

a)

I) calculation of the velocity components

$v_x = v{\cdot}\cos\alpha \;\;\rightarrow\;\; v_x = 1300{\cdot}120/(120^2 + 50^2)^{1/2} \qquad\rightarrow\quad \boldsymbol{v_x = 1200}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

$v_y = v{\cdot}\sin\alpha \;\;\rightarrow\;\; v_x = 1300{\cdot}50/(120^2 + 50^2)^{1/2} \qquad\rightarrow\quad \boldsymbol{v_x = 500}\,\mathbf{m/s}$

II) calculation of the radius of the horizontal projection of the trajectory

$R = m{\cdot} v_x/(B{\cdot} q) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; R = 1{\cdot}10^{-8}{\cdot}1200/(0.5{\cdot}10{\cdot}10^{-6}) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{R = 2.4}\,\mathbf{m}$

III) calculation of the angle swept up to the impact

$\sin\beta = x/R \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \sin\beta = 1.20/2.40 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \sin\beta = 0.50 \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{\beta = 30^\circ}$

IV) calculation of the period $T$ of the circular trajectory

$v_x = 2{\cdot}\pi{\cdot} R/T \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 1200 = 2{\cdot}3{\cdot}2.4/T \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{T = 12{\cdot}10^{-3}}\,\mathbf{s}$

b)

V) calculation of the time taken to reach the screen

$360^\circ/T = 30^\circ/\Delta t \;\;\therefore\;\; \boldsymbol{\Delta t = 1{\cdot}10^{-3}}\,\mathbf{s}$

VI) calculation of the coordinates of the impact

$x = R - R{\cdot}\cos\beta \;\;\rightarrow\;\; x = -2.4 - (-2.4{\cdot}0.87) \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{x = -0.312}\,\mathbf{m}$

$y = v_y{\cdot}\Delta t \;\;\rightarrow\;\; y = 500{\cdot}1{\cdot}10^{-3} \;\;\rightarrow\;\; \boldsymbol{y = 0.50}\,\mathbf{m}$
