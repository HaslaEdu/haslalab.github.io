1.Trajectory of a ballistic missile

As shown in Figure 1, a ballistic missile is launched upward from point A on the ground and, after some time in flight, falls back to another point B on the ground. A and B subtend an angle $\theta$ at the Earth’s centre O, and the initial velocity at launch is parallel, but opposite, to the final velocity on returning to the ground. Neglect air resistance and the rotation of the Earth; $R$ is the radius of the Earth, and the missile obeys Kepler’s laws during its motion. Answer the following questions:

(a)What kind of curve is the trajectory of the missile? Where is its focus?

(b)If a low-altitude artificial satellite orbiting the Earth (in a circular orbit whose radius can be taken equal to the Earth’s radius) has period $T_0$, what is the flight time of the missile from A to B? Express it in terms of $T_0$ and $\theta$.

(c)What is the maximum height above the ground reached by the missile?

(d)Now the missile is instead launched horizontally from point A. If the missile is not to fall back to the ground, what is the minimum launch speed? Explain, and sketch its complete trajectory.

Note 1:At launch a ballistic missile is propelled by a rocket; once the rocket’s fuel is burnt out, it is acted on only by the Earth’s gravity. The missile trajectory discussed in this problem is its trajectory in the Earth’s gravitational field after it has acquired its initial velocity; the motion during the initial rocket-propelled stage is not considered.

Note 2:The area of an ellipse is $\pi ab$, where $a$ and $b$ are its semi-major and semi-minor axes; the area of a circle is $\pi R^2$, where $R$ is its radius.

3.Transport of single electrons

This problem explores the transport of single electrons by the tunnelling effect. On an insulating substrate there is a submicron-sized metal grain, region A in Figure 3; three metal wires lie close to it, each forming a capacitor with the metal grain, with equivalent capacitances $C_L$, $C_R$ and $C_G$, as marked in the figure. Each wire is separated from the metal grain by an insulating oxide layer; the oxide layers of $C_L$ and $C_R$ are thin, so that electrons can cross them by quantum tunnelling, entering or leaving the metal grain; the oxide layer of $C_G$, on the other hand, is thick, so that electrons cannot cross it in the same way. Batteries of voltages $V_L$, $V_R$ and $V_G$ are now connected to the three capacitors respectively, and the negative terminal of each battery is grounded.

Figure 3

(a)If the net charge on the metal grain is $Q=-ne$ ($-e$ is the charge of an electron, $n$ an integer), find the potential $V_A$ of the metal grain in terms of $Q$, $C_i$ and $V_i$ (here and below, $i=L,R,G$).

(b)Find the total electric potential energy $U$ of the three capacitors in terms of $Q$, $C_i$ and $V_i$.

(c)If a single electron crosses the oxide layer of $C_L$ (or $C_R$) into the metal grain and stays there, so that the charge on the grain changes by $\Delta Q=-e$, answer the following:

(c-1)What is the change $\Delta V_A$ in the potential of the metal grain? And what is the change in the charge of each capacitor? Express both in terms of $e$ and $C_i$.

(c-2)If the electron tunnels through the oxide layer of $C_L$, find the work done by each of the three batteries and their total work $W_L$, in terms of $e$, $C_i$ and $V_i$.

(c-3)If the electron tunnels through the oxide layer of $C_R$, find the work done by each of the three batteries and their total work $W_R$, in terms of $e$, $C_i$ and $V_i$.

(d)Continuing from (c): suppose that, of the net charge $Q=-ne$ on the metal grain, a part was obtained by tunnelling through the oxide layer of $C_L$,

namely $-n_Le$, and the rest by tunnelling through the oxide layer of $C_R$, namely $-n_Re$, i.e. $n=n_L+n_R$. What is the total work $W$ done by the three batteries? Express it in terms of $e$, $n_L$, $n_R$, $C_i$ and $V_i$.

(e)The energy $E=U-W$ is the electrostatic energy of the metal grain; its value depends on the net charge on the metal grain. Let $E(n)$ denote the lowest electrostatic energy when the net charge of the metal grain is $Q=-ne$. If $V_L=V_R=0$ but $V_G$ ≠ $0$, answer the following:

(e-1)Find the expression for $E(n)$ in terms of $e$, $n$, $V_G$ and $C_i$.

(e-2)Ignoring the effect of temperature, the result of (e-1) shows that the voltage $V_G$ can change the electrostatic energy of the metal grain. When $V_G$ is tuned to certain specific values, electrons can tunnel through the oxide layer of $C_L$ or $C_R$, entering or leaving the metal grain. If a single electron enters the metal grain by tunnelling, so that its net number of electrons increases from the original $n$ to $n+1$, what should $V_G$ be? (Hint: calculate the energy difference $E(n+1)-E(n)$.)

(f)Continuing from (e): if the net number of electrons in the metal grain is to be fixed at $n$, what is the range of $V_G$? Under this condition electrons cannot enter or leave the metal grain, and the left and right wires do not conduct; this is called the “Coulomb blockade effect”. To observe the Coulomb blockade, however, the temperature must be low enough. Estimate the upper limit of the thermal energy $k_\mathrm BT$, where $k_\mathrm B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $T$ the absolute temperature of the metal-grain system.

4.A salt-water surface raised by electrostatic attraction

A small plastic ball of diameter $1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ carries a uniformly distributed positive charge of $1.0\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm C$. The charged ball is hung by an insulating thread above a large salt-water surface, the distance between the lowest point of the ball and the salt-water surface being $1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$. As a result, the salt water directly below the ball is attracted by the electrostatic force and bulges upward slightly, as shown in Figure 4. Neglecting the surface tension of the salt water and taking its density as $1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, what is the height of the bulge of the salt water directly below the ball? 5.Size of the spot of parallel light focused by a plano-convex lens

Figure 5 shows an incident ray parallel to the optical axis of a plano-convex lens, which after refraction by the lens crosses the optical axis at point Q. Let the refractive index of the lens be $n$ ($n>1$), the radius of curvature of the convex surface of the lens $R$, and the aperture of its plane surface $D$, with $D<R$; the distance between the incident ray and the optical axis is $\rho$, and the thickness of the lens at its centre is $\delta$. Answer the following questions:

Figure 5

(a)Let $q$ be the distance between the vertex A of the lens and point Q. Using ray tracing in geometrical optics, find $q$ as a function of $n$, $\rho$ and $R$.

(b)Continuing from (a), show that under the condition $\rho\ll R$ (keeping only terms of first order in $\rho/R$), all incident rays parallel to the principal axis (called paraxial rays) give the same value of $q$ after refraction by the lens, i.e. they converge at the same point Q. The point Q obtained in this case is called the focus F, and the corresponding value of $q$ is called the focal length $f$.

(c)A screen perpendicular to the optical axis is now placed at the focus F, and a diaphragm of adjustable aperture is attached to the plane surface of the plano-convex lens. The diameter of the diaphragm is set to $d$ (its maximum value is $D$). If a parallel beam of light, parallel to the principal axis of the lens, now illuminates the whole aperture of the diaphragm, what is the diameter $d'$ of the light spot on the screen? Express it in terms of $n$, $d$ and $R$ (in the calculation keep only the lowest-order term in $(d/2R)$).

(d)According to the diffraction theory of light waves, the smaller the aperture of the diaphragm, the more pronounced the diffraction. After a parallel light wave passes through a small hole of diameter $d$, the diameter of the smallest light spot obtained in the focal plane is known to be $d''$, the two being related by $d''=1.22\,f\lambda/d$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the incident light. Combining the two effects described in (c) and (d), and taking the diameter of the light spot in the focal plane to be $s=d'+d''$, what aperture diameter $d$ of the diaphragm gives the smallest light spot? What is the diameter $s_\mathrm{min}$ of the smallest spot obtained? Express them in terms of $n$, $\lambda$ and $R$.

6.A flatcar set sliding by a temperature difference between its two ends

A sealed cylindrical gas container is fixed on a flatcar, which can slide freely on smooth rails, as shown in Figure 6. The mass of the empty container together with the flatcar is $M=150\ \mathrm{kg}$, and the length of the container is $L=10\ \mathrm m$. Initially the container holds $m=120\ \mathrm{kg}$ of an ideal gas, the temperature around the container is $T_0=300\ \mathrm K$, and the pressure of the gas in the container is $P_0=150\ \mathrm{atm}$. The right end face of the container is now heated to $332\ \mathrm K$ and kept at this temperature, while its left end face remains at $300\ \mathrm K$. Assume that the gas in the container can be treated as an ideal gas and undergoes no chemical change as the temperature changes. When the gas in the container has reached equilibrium again (the gas temperature in the container can then be taken to vary linearly along the central axis of the cylinder):

(a)What is the pressure of the gas in the container?

(b)By how much has the centre of the container itself moved?
