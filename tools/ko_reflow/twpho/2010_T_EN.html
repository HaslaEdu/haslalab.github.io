1.Motion of a two-particle system in a conservative force field

The masses of two particles are $m_1$ and $m_2$. The interaction between them is a conservative force, whose potential energy can be written as $U(r) = ar^2-2br+c$, where $r$ is the radial distance between the two particles and $a$, $b$, $c$ are all positive. Let $m_1=m$, $m_2=2m$. Answer the following questions:

(a)If no other external force acts, what is the distance $r_0$ between the two particles at static equilibrium (in terms of $a$, $b$, $c$)?

(b)If no other external force acts, the two particles may perform simple harmonic motion in the centre-of-mass reference frame of the two-particle system. What is its angular frequency $\omega_0$ (in terms of $a$, $b$, $c$, $m$)?

(c)Now place the two particles in the Earth’s uniform gravitational field $\vec g = -g\hat z$, where $g$ is the gravitational acceleration at the Earth’s surface. Initially particle $m_1$ is held fixed, and its position is taken as the origin of the reference frame; particle $m_2$ is then also at rest at its new equilibrium point. Let $b=5mg$. What is the $z$ coordinate of particle $m_2$ (in terms of $r_0$)?

(d)Continuing from (c): if at $t=0$ particle $m_1$ is released from rest at the origin and falls freely, then when the centre of mass of the two-particle system has fallen a distance $30\,r_0$ from its initial position, what are the coordinate (in terms of $r_0$) and the velocity (in terms of $r_0$, $\omega_0$) of $m_1$?

(e)Continuing from (c): if at $t=0$, at the instant particle $m_1$ is released, $m_1$ is given an impulse $P$ along the $+x$ direction, what must $P$ be (in terms of $m$, $r_0$, $\omega_0$) for the motion of the two particles in the centre-of-mass reference frame of their system to be simple uniform circular motion?

2.Coefficient of rolling friction of a cylinder on a soft plane

When an ideal cylinder rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal plane, the velocity of the contact point is zero; so although friction can act between the cylinder and the plane, the friction does no work, and the centre of mass of the cylinder can move with constant velocity. Experience, however, tells us that the cylinder sooner or later stops moving and comes to rest, which shows that so-called rolling friction exists. Rolling friction has many causes, such as the adhesion of surface molecules to the cylinder and the electrostatic interaction between the surface and the cylinder. Some of these causes have nothing to do with the interaction between the surface and the cylinder; the most important one is that real cylinders and planes are never ideal rigid bodies, so deformation necessarily occurs. Hence, if the normal force exerted by the cylinder on the plane is $N$, the cylinder experiences a corresponding frictional torque $\tau = \mu_r N$, which slows the cylinder down until it stops and comes to rest; here $\mu_r$ is called the coefficient of rolling friction (it has the dimension of length).

When a real cylinder rolls on a plane, both deform to some degree, and the degree of deformation depends on their relative hardness. In this problem we consider a hard cylinder rolling on a soft plane. Since the cylinder is much harder than the plane, its cross-section remains circular during rolling, while the part of the plane in contact with the cylinder deforms, as shown in Figure 1(a).

4.Electric dipoles of gas molecules

Consider a uniform gas of density $\rho$ with permittivity $\varepsilon=\kappa\varepsilon_0$ ($\kappa$ is the dielectric constant and $\varepsilon_0$ the permittivity of vacuum); the molecules of this gas are electrically neutral, and each has mass $m$.

(a)The space between the two metal plates of a parallel-plate capacitor is filled with this gas, and the charge per unit area on the surface of the positive plate is $\sigma$. What is the average value of the electric dipole moment $\mu$ of each gas molecule?

In what follows, the field of the parallel-plate capacitor is not considered.

(b)Suppose that an ion of charge $Q$ and the molecules of this gas can all be treated as point particles. When the distance between a gas molecule and the ion is $r$, what is the electrostatic force exerted by the ion on the gas molecule?

(c)Suppose that an ion of mass $M$ and charge $Q$ and the gas molecules can all be treated as uniform spheres; the ion has the mean kinetic energy of the thermal motion of the gas molecules, and its charge $Q$ is uniformly distributed over its surface. The gas is in thermal equilibrium at pressure $P$ and obeys the ideal-gas equation. When the ion is in the gas, gas molecules can be attracted around it, forming a “cluster” (or cluster ion) of several gas molecules and the ion. What, approximately, is the maximum distance $r_c$ between the centres of the ion and a gas molecule?

(d)The dielectric constant of air is $\kappa=1.0006$ and the diameter of an air molecule is about $d=3.3\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m$. At a pressure of one atmosphere, an ion formed from an air molecule has charge $Q=e=1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm C$. Using the result of (c), explain whether this ion can attract other neutral air molecules to form a cluster ion.

Note: $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}$

6.Locating the point of an explosion

An explosive explodes at some place on the ground. A person A far from the point of explosion first feels a vibration parallel to the ground, followed by a vibration perpendicular to the ground, the time interval between the two being $2.5\ \mathrm s$; another person B at a different location has the same experience, but with a time interval of $5.0\ \mathrm s$. The straight-line distance between A and B is known to be $17.3\ \mathrm{km}$, the speed of longitudinal seismic waves is $4000\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and that of transverse seismic waves is $2000\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Using these data, calculate:

(a)the distances from the point of explosion to A and to B;

(b)the angle between the lines joining the point of explosion to A and to B.
