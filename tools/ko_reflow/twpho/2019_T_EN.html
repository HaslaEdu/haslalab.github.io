Mathematical formulas that may be useful ($t$ is time, $x$ is any physical quantity)

1. $f'(x)\equiv\dfrac{df}{dx}$, $f''(x)\equiv\dfrac{d^2f}{dx^2}=\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{df}{dx}\right)$;

$\dot x(t)\equiv\dfrac{dx}{dt}$, $\ddot x(t)\equiv\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$.

2. $\displaystyle\int(ax+b)^m dx=\frac{(ax+b)^{m+1}}{(m+1)a}$, $m$ ≠ $-1$; $\displaystyle\int(ax+b)^{-1}dx=\frac{1}{a}\ln(ax+b)$;

$\dfrac{d}{dx}(ax+b)^m=ma\cdot(ax+b)^{m-1}$; $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(ax+b)=a\cdot(ax+b)^{-1}$.

3. $\dfrac{de^{ax}}{dx}=ae^{ax}$, $\dfrac{d\sin ax}{dx}=a\cos ax$, $\dfrac{d\cos ax}{dx}=-a\sin ax$;

$\displaystyle\int e^{ax}dx=\frac{1}{a}e^{ax}$, $\displaystyle\int\sin ax\,dx=-\frac{1}{a}\cos ax$, $\displaystyle\int\cos ax\,dx=\frac{1}{a}\sin ax$.

4. For $|x|\ll1$: $(1+x)^\alpha\approx1+\alpha x$,

$e^x\approx1+x$, $\sin x\approx x$, $\cos x\approx1-\dfrac{x^2}{2}$.

5.The linear first-order differential equation with constant coefficients $ax'(t)+bx(t)=0$ has the general solution

$x(t)=\alpha e^{st}$

where $s$ is the root of the linear equation $as+b=0$,

and $\alpha$ is an integration constant determined by the initial condition $x(0)$.

6.The linear second-order differential equation with constant coefficients $ax''(t)+bx'(t)+cx(t)=0$ has the general solution

$x(t)=\alpha_1e^{s_1t}+\alpha_2e^{s_2t}$

where $s_1$, $s_2$ are the roots of the quadratic equation $as^2+bs+c=0$,

and $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ are integration constants determined by the initial conditions $x(0)$ and $x'(0)$.

3.Ionization by electrons in a gas

An electron of charge $-e$ in a gas in a strong electric field is accelerated, and its kinetic energy can become large enough to ionize a gas molecule A that it strikes, splitting it into a positive ion $\mathrm A^+$ and an electron $\mathrm e^-$. Only ionization by electrons of the type $\mathrm A\to\mathrm A^+ +\mathrm e^-$ is considered in this problem, and recombination of electrons and ions is negligible. A simple model is used below to study the ionization by electrons in a gas when the system has reached a steady state, a basic and important topic for gas-filled ionizing-radiation detectors.

As shown in the figure below, two parallel-plate electrodes are a distance $d$ apart; the strong electric field between them is uniform and constant, and the space is filled with a gas of neutral molecules at a fixed temperature $T$. The negative plate (at the lower potential) is at $x=0$ and the positive plate at $x=d$. The number of ionization electrons produced per unit length by each electron travelling through the gas is denoted by the constant $\alpha$, called the first Townsend coefficient.

$x$-axis direction

Figure 4

(A)The negative plate (at $x=0$) is uniformly illuminated with ultraviolet light, so that it emits $n_0$ free electrons per unit time along the $x$-axis. These electrons move through the gas along the $x$-axis towards the positive plate, making ionizing collisions with gas molecules. Assume that the electric field is unaffected by the electrons and ions, and neglect ionization by the ions; the first Townsend coefficient of the electrons is $\alpha$. Find the total number $n_d$ of electrons reaching the positive plate (at $x=d$) per unit time.

(B)Continuing from the previous part: experiments show that when $\alpha d$ is large, the total number of electrons reaching the positive plate per unit time exceeds the value predicted above. It has therefore been proposed that the positive ions produced in ionizing collisions of electrons with molecules (as many as the ionization electrons) move towards the negative plate and, after being accelerated by the field, can also ionize gas molecules, producing electrons. Let the constant $\beta$ be the number of ionization electrons produced per unit length by each travelling positive ion, and extend the model of part (A) to include ionization by both electrons and positive ions. What then is the total number $N_d$ of electrons reaching the positive plate per unit time?

6.Quartz oscillator

Quartz oscillators and filters. A main use of piezoelectric quartz crystals in electronic circuits is to control the frequency of oscillators and filters. As shown in Figure 8(a), metal electrodes are plated on both faces of a suitably cut thin quartz plate, which is connected to an AC source; the piezoelectric effect then produces a mechanical wave in the crystal. Let the wavelength of this wave be $\lambda$ and the length of the crystal along its direction of propagation be $\ell$. When the two satisfy the resonance condition $n(\lambda/2)=\ell$ ($n$ a positive integer), the wave forms a standing wave in the crystal, of frequency $f_s=v/\lambda$, $v$ being the propagation speed of the mechanical wave in the medium. As the AC current excites the mechanical vibration of the quartz through the piezoelectric effect, the quartz behaves like a series inductor–capacitor–resistor (LCR) circuit driven by an AC source, as shown by the equivalent circuit on the right of Figure 8(b). In this equivalent LCR circuit, L, $C$ and R correspond to the mass, stiffness and mechanical loss (or damping) of the quartz crystal. Moreover, as both faces of the quartz plate carry metal electrodes, it can be regarded as a parallel-plate capacitor $C_0$, in parallel with the equivalent LCR circuit, as shown by the equivalent circuit on the left of Figure 8(b). The impedance of a typical quartz oscillator has a minimum at the mechanical resonance frequency $f_s$; in addition, owing to the resonance between the equivalent inductance $L$ and the two equivalent capacitances ($C$ and $C_0$), the oscillator has an impedance maximum at another frequency $f_\mathrm a$ (the anti-resonant frequency), as shown in Figure 8(c). Based on the above, answer the following questions.

Figure 8

(A)From the above properties of the quartz oscillator, calculate $f_s$ and $f_\mathrm a$ in terms of the parameters $L$, $C$, $C_0$, etc. in the figure.

(B)Young’s modulus of quartz is $Y=80.0\ \mathrm{GPa}$ and its density is $\rho=2.65\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Approximately what length is suitable for a 1.00 MHz quartz oscillator? (Assume the standing wave is the fundamental, $n=1$.)

(C)The coupling coefficient $\kappa$ of a piezoelectric material can be defined as:

$\kappa^2\equiv\dfrac{\text{mechanical energy stored in the system}}{\text{total energy stored in the system}}$. Show that $\kappa^2=1-\dfrac{f_s^2}{f_\mathrm a^2}$.

(D)A typical X-cut quartz crystal has $\kappa=0.100$. Given $f_s=1.00\ \mathrm{MHz}$, $C_0=5.00\ \mathrm{pF}$, $R=20.0\ \Omega$, find the equivalent inductance $L$ and capacitance $C$ of this quartz crystal.

(E)Continuing from (D), the quality factor $Q$ of the quartz oscillator can be written as:

$Q=\dfrac{1}{2\pi f_sRC}$. Calculate the quality factor of this quartz oscillator.
