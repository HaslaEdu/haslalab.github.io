1.Simple harmonic motion of a half-cylinder on a horizontal table

A half-cylinder of radius $R$ and mass $M$ is placed on a horizontal table with its flat face upward, tilted at a small angle to the horizontal, as in Figure 1 on the right. The half-cylinder is released from rest; the initial tilt of its flat face is very small, so its motion can be approximated as simple harmonic. The perpendicular distance from the centre of mass of the half-cylinder to its axis is $\tfrac{4R}{3\pi}$, its moment of inertia about its centre of mass is $I$, and the gravitational acceleration is $g$. Answer the following three parts:

(a)If the table is frictionless, estimate the period of this periodic motion. (5 points)

(b)If the table has friction and the half-cylinder, after being released from rest, only rolls without slipping on the table, and the initial tilt of the half-cylinder from the horizontal is $\theta_0$, what is its maximum angular velocity? (10 points)

(c)Continuing from the previous part, for the half-cylinder not to slip on the table, at least how large must the coefficient of static friction of the table be? (10 points)

6.Specific heat of solids

(a)When the temperature of a system rises by $dT$ it absorbs heat $dQ$; its specific heat is defined as $C=dQ/dT$. For a solid, assuming that the work done by the solid on its surroundings during the temperature change is negligible, show that $C_P\approx C_V=\tfrac{dU}{dT}$, where $U$ is the total energy of the system and $C_P$ and $C_V$ are the specific heats at constant pressure and at constant volume. From here on the specific heat of the solid is written $C$. (4 points)

The periodic arrangement of atoms in a crystal is called a lattice; the lattice vibrates at any temperature, and the lattice vibrations contribute to the specific heat. Just as quantizing electromagnetic waves gives photons, quantized lattice vibrations are called phonons. Like that of a photon, the energy of a phonon can be written $E=\hbar\omega=hf$, with $\hbar=\tfrac{h}{2\pi}$, where $h$ is Planck’s constant, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, equal to $2\pi f$, and $f$ is the phonon frequency; the phonon wavelength can be written $\lambda=\tfrac{2\pi}{k}$. Corresponding to three-dimensional real space, $k$ also forms a three-dimensional reciprocal space. In reciprocal space, the number of phonon states $dN$ is related to the volume $dV_k$ occupied by the phonons in reciprocal space by $dN=\tfrac{V}{8\pi^3}dV_k$, where $V$ is the volume of the solid.

(b)In the Debye model the phonon dispersion relation is $\omega=vk$, where $v$ is a constant whose physical meaning is the speed of sound in the solid. Calculate the density of states $\rho(\omega)\equiv\tfrac{dN}{d\omega}$. (6 points)

(c)Lattice vibrations contribute part of the specific heat. Given that the low-temperature phonon specific heat $C_l$ is proportional to $\sim T^n$, what is the value of $n$? (5 points)

(d)Figure 11 is a plot of the processed data from an experiment measuring the specific heat of aluminium as a function of temperature. At very low temperatures the specific heat shows an anomalous jump because aluminium becomes superconducting; ignore it for now. The specific heat of aluminium contains contributions from phonons, $C_l$, and from conduction carriers, $C_e$. Write down the relation between the low-temperature specific heat $C$ of aluminium and the temperature $T$. (5 points)
