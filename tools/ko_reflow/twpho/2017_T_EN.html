3.Magnetic force microscope

Scanning probe microscopes play an important role in research in surface physics. The instrument has a micrometre-scale cantilever; one end of the cantilever is fixed to an oscillator, and a fine protruding tip is mounted at its free end. The whole cantilever forms a spring oscillator system; in the absence of magnetic forces, the equation of motion of the probe can be written as:

$M\dfrac{d^2z}{d^2t}+b\dfrac{dz}{dt}+kz=F_0e^{i\omega t}$

where $M$ is the mass of the probe system, $b$ is the damping coefficient (weak damping is assumed), $k$ is the spring constant in the absence of magnetic forces, and $F_0e^{i\omega t}$ is the driving force of the vertical oscillation. The steady-state solution of this equation of motion has the form $z(t)=Ae^{i(\omega t-\phi)}$, where $A$ is the amplitude and $\phi$ is the phase angle; both the amplitude and the phase angle are functions of the driving angular frequency $\omega$. As shown in Figure 4, the probe of the scanning magnetic force microscope can be regarded as a small magnetic moment along the $z$ direction, and the tip is kept at a fixed distance from the surface under test;

3

through the magnetic force the microscope can explore the distribution of magnetic domains on the surface under test.

The working principle of the magnetic force microscope: in the magnetic field of the surface under test the probe has a “magnetic potential energy” $U=-mH_z$, where $m$ is the magnetic moment and $H_z$ the magnetic field strength at point $z$. As the field strength varies with distance, the probe feels a non-uniform magnetic force along $z$, which changes the resonant angular frequency of the cantilever. As the tip scans the surface, the system converts the change of the resonant angular frequency into a bright/dark contrast: a rise appears as a “bright” level, the opposite as a “dark” level. A change of the resonant angular frequency also changes the amplitude and the phase angle, so the microscope can use changes of the resonant angular frequency, phase angle and amplitude for detection. Based on the above, answer the following questions.

(A)Let $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/M}$. Find the amplitude and the phase angle of the probe as functions of the driving angular frequency when no magnetic force acts on it. (6 points)

In the weak-damping case $(b\to0)$, if the change of the resonant angular frequency is used as the mechanism for detecting the magnetic force, derive the change of the resonant angular frequency $(\Delta\omega)$ as a function of $m$, $\omega_0$, $a$ and $z$ (accurate to first order in $a$). 4.Rolling with slipping

The horizontal ground coincides with the $xy$ plane of a Cartesian coordinate system, and on this ground there is a uniform solid sphere of mass $m$ and radius $R$. At time $t=0$ the sphere starts from the origin O, rolling with slipping; its angular velocity of rotation about its centre of mass is $\vec\omega_0=\omega_0(\hat x+\hat y)/\sqrt2$, and the velocity of its centre of mass is $\vec v_0=\sqrt2\,\omega_0R\hat y$. In addition, the moment of inertia of the sphere about its centre of mass is $I=mk^2=2mR^2/5$, the coefficient of kinetic friction between the sphere and the ground is $\mu_\mathrm{k}$, and the gravitational acceleration is $-g\hat z$.

6.Gunn oscillator circuit

The Gunn diode is a transferred-electron device, generally made from the semiconductor gallium arsenide. This diode exhibits a distinctive non-linear $I-V$ (current–voltage) relation, as shown in Figure 7. The most peculiar property of the Gunn diode is that under suitable operating conditions, e.g. with the terminal voltage of the diode between $V_1$ and $V_2$ in Figure 7, the device can show negative differential resistance, i.e. $r_d\equiv dV/dI<0$. Negative differential resistance means that, for AC signals near this operating point, the device does not dissipate energy but instead feeds energy back into the circuit. Because of this special property, Gunn diodes are widely used to make generators of microwaves up to terahertz (THz) waves. Figure 8 shows a simplified Gunn oscillator circuit. In the right half of the circuit, the Gunn diode and a series resistor $R$ are connected through a radio-frequency choke (RFC) to a DC voltage source $V_b$. Here the RF choke is a protective element that keeps RF alternating current out of the DC voltage source. The left half of the circuit is an $LC$ oscillator circuit, which can produce an alternating current $i(t)$. Assume here that the energy loss of the RF choke is negligible; $r_d=-r$ $(r>0)$, where $r$ is a constant in the voltage range between $V_1$ and $V_2$; the DC voltage across the Gunn diode is $V$ and the AC voltage is $v_{out}(t)$. Answer the following questions:

(A)If $i(0)=0$, $i'(0)=\alpha$, $i\ll I$, find the alternating current $i(t)$. (9 points)

(B)State the conditions for the circuit to oscillate, and find the oscillation frequency. (6 points)

(C)When oscillation occurs, the voltage drop across the $LC$ part of the circuit is $v_{in}(t)$ (which can be regarded as an AC signal input source). Taking $\sqrt{L/C}\gg R$, find $v_{out}/v_{in}$ at this time (this is the voltage gain). (3 points)

(D)While the circuit oscillates, the DC voltage of the diode is fixed at $V=(V_2-V_1)/2$. Find the total power consumed by the Gunn diode in operation. (7 points)
