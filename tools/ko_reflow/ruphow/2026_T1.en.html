Gas with Variable Heat Capacity (9 points)

When ideal gases are studied, their molar heat capacity at constant volume $C_V$ is usually assumed to be a constant that does not depend on temperature and is determined by the number of atoms in a gas molecule. In reality, however, this heat capacity can vary. At very low temperatures the thermal energy is not sufficient to excite rotational motion of the molecules, and they can be treated as monatomic. Therefore the heat capacity at low temperatures is lower than at high temperatures. In this problem we consider several cases of a varying heat capacity. The equation of state of the ideal gas (that is, the relation between pressure, volume and temperature) remains unchanged.

Part A. Heat capacity depending linearly on temperature (5.7 points)

In this part we assume that, over the whole temperature range of interest, the heat capacity of one mole of the gas at constant volume depends on temperature as $C_V = C_0 + aT$ ($C_0$, $a > 0$ are known constants independent of temperature). All processes are carried out with one mole of the gas. In all answers of this part you may use the quantities $C_0$, $a$ and the universal gas constant $R$.

Find how the internal energy of this gas depends on temperature. Assume that the internal energy is zero at $T = 0$.

Let the gas undergo an isobaric process; the initial volume is $V_1$, the final volume is $V_2$, the pressure is $p$. Find the heat $Q_p$ supplied to the gas.

Part B. Piecewise linear heat capacity (3.3 points)

As a more realistic model of the temperature dependence of the heat capacity of a gas, the following can be proposed. For $T < T_1$ the heat capacity $C_V$ is constant and equal to the heat capacity of a monatomic gas, $C_1 = 3R/2$. In the temperature range $T_1 < T < T_2$ the heat capacity grows linearly, reaching at $T = T_2$ the value $C_2 = 5R/2$ corresponding to a diatomic gas. At higher temperatures the heat capacity remains constant. We consider one mole of such a gas, for which $T_2 = 2T_1$. Let the initial temperature of the gas be $T_A = T_1/2$. The temperature of the gas is raised to $T_B = 3T_1$ in different ways.

Find the amount of heat $Q_V$ that must be supplied to the gas in isochoric heating. Express the answer in terms of $T_1$, $R$.

Find the amount of heat $Q_P$ that must be supplied to the gas in isobaric heating. Express the answer in terms of $T_1$, $R$.

Let the initial volume of the gas be $V_0$. What will the final volume be if the temperature was raised adiabatically? Find the work done in this process. Express the answers in terms of $T_1$, $R$, $V_0$.
