This problem studies electric circuits containing a nonlinear element whose current–voltage characteristic is shown in Fig. 1.

The current–voltage characteristic of the nonlinear element consists of two segments:

1. $I = I_0\cdot(U/U_0)$ for $U \le U_0$, where $I_0 = 10\,\mathrm{mA}$ and $U_0 = 5\,\mathrm{V}$.

2. $I = I_0 = \mathrm{const}$ for $U \ge U_0$.

Part A. DC circuit (3.0 points)

Fig. 2 shows an electric circuit consisting of resistors with resistances $R$, $2R$, $4R$, $6R$ and $8R$, an ideal DC voltage source $\mathcal{E}$ and the nonlinear element described above. This part of the problem studies how the current through the nonlinear element depends on the source voltage.

Since, apart from the part of the circuit between nodes $A$ and $B$, the circuit consists entirely of DC voltage sources and resistors, the dependence of the current $I_{AB}$ flowing from node $A$ to node $B$ on the voltage $U_{AB} = \varphi_A - \varphi_B$ across this part of the circuit can be written in a form that does not depend on the element connected between nodes $A$ and $B$:

Determine $\mathcal{E}_0$ and $r_0$. Express your answers in terms of $\mathcal{E}$ and $R$.

Find the current $I_{AB}$ as a function of $\mathcal{E}$. Express your answer in terms of $\mathcal{E}_0$, $r_0$, $R$, $U_0$ and $I_0$.

Find the power $P_\mathrm{NE}$ dissipated in the nonlinear element as a function of $\mathcal{E}$. Express your answer in terms of $\mathcal{E}$, $R$, $U_0$ and $I_0$.

Sketch a qualitative graph of $P_\mathrm{NE}(\mathcal{E})$. Mark all the characteristic points on the graph and calculate the corresponding values.
