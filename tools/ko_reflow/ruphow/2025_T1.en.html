In this problem you are asked to analyse the dynamics of the following system: a uniform, flexible, inextensible tape of linear density $\lambda$ is wound on a rigid, uniform, thin-walled cylinder. One end of the tape is fixed to the surface of the cylinder, and the other end is attached to a vertical wall. The total mass of the system is $M$. The tape is wound around the cylinder a very large number of times, and the thickness of the wound layer of tape is negligible compared with the radius of the cylinder.

Initially the cylinder touches the wall at the point where the tape is attached. The cylinder is released with zero initial velocity, and the tape starts to unwind. Assume that the straight part of the tape never comes off the wall. During the motion the tape does not slip on the cylinder. The free-fall acceleration is $g$. Sliding friction and any other energy losses in the system can be neglected.

In all parts of the problem, assume that the cylinder axis descends by a distance many times larger than the cylinder radius. Also, unless stated otherwise, assume in all parts that the layers of tape are pressed tightly against each other and that the tape does not unwind completely.

Part A. Motion without detachment (6.7 points)

Suppose that at some moment the mass of the cylinder together with the tape remaining on its surface has decreased to $m$.

Take the potential energy $W_p$ of the system in the Earth's gravitational field to be zero in the initial position. Find the dependence of the potential energy $W_p$ on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $M$, $\lambda$, $g$ and $m$.

Let $v$ be the speed of the cylinder axis.

Express the kinetic energy $E_k$ of the system in terms of $m$ and $v$ for $m \gg \lambda R$ (here $R$ is the unknown radius of the cylinder).

Obtain the dependence of the speed $v$ of the cylinder axis on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $M$, $\lambda$, $g$ and $m$.

Find the dependence of the momentum $p$ of the system on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $M$, $\lambda$, $g$ and $m$.

Let $a$ be the acceleration of the cylinder axis, $T_1$ the tension of the tape at the point where it is attached to the wall, and $T_O$ the tension of the tape at the point where the vertical part of the tape touches the cylinder.

Find the dependence of the acceleration $a$ of the cylinder axis on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $g$, $M$ and $m$.

Find the dependence of the tension $T_1$ of the tape at the point where it is attached to the vertical wall on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $g$, $M$ and $m$.

Find the dependence of the tension $T_O$ of the tape at the point where the vertical part of the tape touches the cylinder on the mass $m$. Express the answer in terms of $g$, $M$ and $m$.

Part B. Detachment condition (5.3 points)

The results you obtained in Part $A$ are applicable as long as the tape does not start to come off the surface of the cylinder. This part of the problem is devoted to finding the position at which the tape starts to come off the surface of the cylinder. Unless stated otherwise, assume that the tape does not unwind completely.

Write down the condition relating the tension $T_O$, the speed $v$ and the linear density $\lambda$ under which the tape does not come off the cylinder surface. Assume that the tape tension is constant along the whole first turn of the tape around the cylinder. Also assume that $v^2 \gg gR$.

At what value $m_c$ does the tape start to come off the surface of the cylinder? Express the answer in terms of $M$.

Find the values $T_1(m_c)$, $T_O(m_c)$ and $a(m_c)$. Express the answers in terms of $M$ and $g$.

Find the value $v(m_c)$. Express the answer in terms of $M$, $g$ and $\lambda$.

What values can $v(m_c)$ take for a fixed length $L$ of the tape, if by that moment the tape has not yet unwound completely? Express the answer in terms of $g$ and $L$.
