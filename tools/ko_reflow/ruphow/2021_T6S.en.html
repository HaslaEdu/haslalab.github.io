This method requires numerical integration (calculators such as the Casio fx-991 can do it). When the numerical integration is done by hand (on calculators such as the Casio fx-82), a considerable error may arise because the integral diverges near 1.

Consider the field created by a semi-infinite thread with linear charge density $\lambda$ at a distance $x$ from its end (in the plane perpendicular to the thread and passing through its end). In particular, we are interested only in the component of the field parallel to the thread: $E_\parallel$. This quantity can be obtained at once by the method of virtual displacements, or the thread can be divided into infinitesimal segments of length $dh$ and integrated over the range $h \in [0, \infty)$.

Returning to the original problem, the whole semi-infinite cylinder can be divided into semi-infinite rectangles of width $Rd\alpha$ (in fact, semi-infinite threads).
