In everyday life it seems to us that light rays in the atmosphere around us travel in straight lines. However, this is not so, and the bending of the rays goes unnoticed only because the refractive index of air is practically indistinguishable from unity. On large scales, and when certain atmospheric phenomena occur, it can no longer be neglected. In the Earth’s atmosphere an example of such phenomena is the mirage, and in the atmospheres of other planets (for example, Venus) a practically unlimited distance to the horizon. In this problem we consider each of these phenomena in detail.
