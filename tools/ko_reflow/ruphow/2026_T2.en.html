Rotating Medium (11 points)

Most physical systems are not conservative because of the presence of dissipative forces. Friction is an example of such forces. In this problem we consider examples of such systems with the simplest case of friction, viscous friction.

Part A. Medium at rest (4 points)

A particle of mass $m$ moves in a medium at rest, in which a viscous friction force $\vec{F}_\mathrm{fr} = -\varepsilon m\vec{v}$ acts on it, where $\vec{v}$ is the velocity of the particle. The free-fall acceleration is $\vec{g}$; initially the particle was at the origin and had velocity $\vec{v}_0$.

Find the trajectory $\vec{r}(t)$ of this particle. Express the answer in vector form in terms of $\vec{v}_0$, $\vec{g}$, $\varepsilon$, or express the components of the vector $\vec{r}(t)$ in a Cartesian coordinate system as functions of time in terms of the components of the vectors $\vec{v}_0$, $\vec{g}$.

How does the power of energy losses $P(t)$, which is dissipated as heat, depend on time? Express the answer in terms of $\vec{v}_0$, $\vec{g}$, $\varepsilon$, $t$, $m$.

Now consider the motion of the particle in the absence of gravity, but now a rotating force of constant magnitude $F_x = F_0\cos(\omega t)$, $F_y = F_0\sin(\omega t)$ acts on it.

Find the loss power $P$ dissipated as heat a long time $t \gg 1/\varepsilon$ after the force has been switched on. Express the answer in terms of $F_0$, $\varepsilon$, $m$, $\omega$.

Part B. Rotating medium (7 points)

A particle of mass $m$ is in a medium rotating with angular velocity $\omega$ about the vertical axis $z$; the free-fall acceleration is $\vec{g} = -g\vec{e}_z$, the friction force is $\vec{F} = -\varepsilon m\vec{v}_\mathrm{rel}$, where $\vec{v}_\mathrm{rel}$ is the velocity of the particle relative to the medium. At the initial moment the particle is at rest at the point with coordinates $(x_0, y_0, z_0) = (r_0, 0, 0)$.

Find the dependence $z(t)$. Express the answer in terms of $r_0$, $\varepsilon$, $g$.
