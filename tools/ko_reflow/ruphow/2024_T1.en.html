The Sliding Rope

A smooth inclined plane makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal. The surface of the inclined plane passes smoothly into two weightless pulleys 1 and 2 touching it; their radius can be considered negligibly small.

A flexible rope of constant linear mass density $\lambda$ is thrown over the pulleys; its ends are initially at the same horizontal level. The length of the part of the rope hanging on the left is $L_1$, and the length of the section on the inclined plane is $L$. The free-fall acceleration is $g$. Denote by $x$ the downward displacement of the left end of the rope from its initial position (see the figure).

This problem studies the dynamics of the rope.

Part A. Weightless pulleys (7.5 points)

The rope is taken out of equilibrium by giving it a small velocity, so that its left end starts to move down. Assume that the rope does not lose contact with the pulleys during the motion.

Find the speed $v$ and the tangential acceleration $a$ of the rope at the moment when the left end has descended by $x$. Express your answer in terms of $g$, $L_1$, $L$, $\alpha$ and $x$.

Find the displacement $x_1$ of the left end of the rope at the moment when the tension in the part of the rope lying on the inclined plane is constant along the length of this part. Express your answer in terms of $L_1$, $L$ and $\alpha$.

For what relations between $L_1$, $L$ and $\alpha$ is $x_1$ smaller than the initial length of the right part of the rope?

Find the maximum tension in the rope at the moment when its left end has moved by a distance $x$. Express your answer in terms of $\lambda$, $g$, $L_1$, $L$, $\alpha$ and $x$.

The expressions obtained are valid as long as the rope moves without losing contact with the pulleys.

Now let us study under what conditions the rope loses contact.

Find the displacements $x_{\max(1)}$ and $x_{\max(1)}$ of the left end of the rope at which it starts to lose contact with pulleys 1 and 2, respectively, assuming that the contact with the other pulley is maintained. Express your answers in terms of $L_1$, $L$ and $\alpha$.

From now on assume that $L = 2L_1$. The length $L = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, and the free-fall acceleration is $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Calculate the speed of the rope at the moment it loses contact with one of the pulleys if the inclined plane makes the angles $\alpha_1 = 30^\circ$, $\alpha_2 = 45^\circ$ and $\alpha_2 = 60^\circ$ with the horizontal.
