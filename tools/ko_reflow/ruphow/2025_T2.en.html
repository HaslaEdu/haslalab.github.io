As is well known, the efficiency is the ratio of the useful work done by the working substance to the amount of heat supplied to it. Loosely speaking, this coefficient shows how effectively the heat coming from some heater is “converted” into work.

This problem considers a cycle performed with an ideal gas and consisting of two isobars and two isochores (see Fig. 1). The adiabatic index of the gas is known and equal to $\gamma$. Suppose that heat is removed from the gas by a refrigerator operating on a reverse Carnot cycle between the gas and the surroundings. Let $A_\mathrm{g}$ be the work done by the gas during one cycle described above, and $A_\mathrm{ref}$ the work done on the working substance of the refrigerator during one cycle of the working gas. Heat is supplied to the gas by heat exchange with the surroundings. In this case it is natural to introduce, instead of the efficiency, the “effectiveness” $\eta$ of the cycle:

$\eta = \dfrac{A_\mathrm{g}}{A_\mathrm{ref}}.$

Assume that the change in the gas temperature during one cycle of the refrigerator is negligible.

Fig. 1

Express the molar heat capacities of the gas in an isobaric process, $C_P$, and in an isochoric process, $C_V$, in terms of $R$ and $\gamma$.

Let the maximum temperature in the cycle be equal to the temperature of the surroundings $T$; in the isochoric cooling the temperature decreases from $T$ to $T - t_1$, and in the isobaric cooling from $T - t_1$ to $T - t$.

Express the ratios $p_2/p_1$ and $V_2/V_1$ (notation of Fig. 1) in terms of $T$, $t_1$ and $t$.

Let the amount of gas be $\nu$.

Express the work $A_\mathrm{g}$ in terms of $\nu$, $R$, $T$, $t_1$ and $t$.

For a refrigerator, the coefficient of performance $\chi$ is defined by the formula:

$\chi = \dfrac{Q_-}{A_\mathrm{ref}},$

where $Q_-$ is the heat removed from the gas during one cycle.

Find $\chi$ at the moment when the gas temperature is $T - \tau$. Express the answer in terms of $\tau$ and $T$.

Find $A_\mathrm{ref}$. Express the answer in terms of $\nu$, $R$, $\gamma$, $T$, $t_1$ and $t$.

Introduce the notation $y = t/T$ and $x = t_1/t$.

Express $\eta$ for the system described in terms of $x$, $\gamma$ and $y$. Calculate its value for $\gamma = 5/3$, $x = 0.5$, $y = 0.5$.

Now let us analyse what maximum $\eta$ can be obtained for different values of $y$ and $x$. Take it as known that the best $\eta$ is achieved for $y \ll 1$.

Find the dependence $\eta(x)$ for $y \ll 1$. Express the answer in terms of $\gamma$ and $x$.

If necessary, you may use the formula $\ln(1 + \xi) \approx \xi - \xi^2/2$ for $\xi \ll 1$.

Find the value of $x$ at which the effectiveness $\eta$ is maximal. Express the answer in terms of $\gamma$.

Find the maximum possible effectiveness $\eta_{max}$. Express the answer in terms of $\gamma$. Calculate its value for $\gamma = 5/3$.
