## T1-A [3.0 pts]. Hydrostatic gate

Two water reservoirs are separated by a vertical wall MN, as shown in the figure 1a. The left reservoir can receive water from a source. It must be ensured that the maximum possible difference between the water levels is $\Delta h = 1.41\ \text{m}$. To achieve this, a slot of vertical size $a\sqrt{2}/2$ is cut in MN and sealed with a solid cubic block of side length $a$. The block has density $3\rho_0$, with $\rho_0$ the density of water. The block is fully submerged, fixed to the wall at $O$ and can rotate without friction about the axis perpendicular to the plane of the figure that passes through point $O$.

> **Figure description (Fig. 1a).** Vertical cross-section of a rectangular tank divided by the thin vertical wall MN (M at the top, N at the tank floor). A tap pours water into the left reservoir. The water surface in the left reservoir is higher than that in the right reservoir by $\Delta h$ (double arrow drawn next to the wall between the two surfaces). The wall is interrupted by the slot: its upper section ends at point $O$, and the lower section (down to N at the floor) begins a vertical distance $a\sqrt2/2$ below $O$. The cube is drawn as a hatched square of side $a$ rotated by $45^\circ$ (a "diamond": one vertex pointing left, one up, one right, one down). Point $O$ lies on the upper-left face of the square (the face joining the left vertex to the top vertex), at a distance $\tfrac12 a$ from the left vertex, i.e. at the midpoint of that face; $O$ is the hinge. Hence only a small right-isosceles triangular part of the block, containing the left vertex, sticks out into the left reservoir: it is bounded by the half of the upper-left face from $O$ to the left vertex, the half of the lower-left face from the left vertex to the point $a\sqrt2/2$ directly below $O$, and the vertical slot between those two points. The top end of the lower wall section touches the lower-left face of the block at that point, sealing the slot. The rest of the block (top, right and bottom vertices) lies in the right reservoir; the whole block is below the water surface of the right reservoir.

**T1-A1 [3.0 pts.]** Calculate the value of $a$ that ensures the maximum permissible difference in water levels is not exceeded.

## T1-B [3.5 pts]. Electron-positron pair

At a certain instant of time, a positron $e^+$ is located at a distance of $100a_0$ from an electron $e^-$. The particles are moving in such a way that their velocities are antiparallel at that instant, perpendicular to their separation, as shown in figure 1b. It is known that each particle has an angular momentum of magnitude $\mu\hbar$ with respect to the system's center of mass, where $\mu$ is a dimensionless numerical factor. Assume that the only interaction between $e^+$ and $e^-$ is electrostatic (both particles have the same mass $m$ and equal magnitude of the charge but of opposite sign). Furthermore, assume that this system remains isolated and that its dynamics are classical and non-relativistic.

*The Bohr radius $a_0$ is given by $a_0 = 4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2/(me^2)$.*

> **Figure description (Fig. 1b).** The positron $e^+$ is at the top and the electron $e^-$ directly below it; the vertical segment joining them is labelled $100a_0$. At this instant the velocity of $e^+$ points horizontally to the right and the velocity of $e^-$ points horizontally to the left (both perpendicular to the separation).

**T1-B1 [1.0 pts.]** If $\mu = 4$, the system is bound, meaning that the particles move in a closed orbit around the system's center of mass. Find the maximum separation distance between $e^+$ and $e^-$ in terms of $a_0$.

**T1-B2 [2.5 pts].** If $\mu = \frac{15}{2}$, the system is unbound, meaing that the particles are no longer in closed orbit around the system's center of mass. Let $\vec u_\infty$ be the velocity of $e^+$ relative to $e^-$ as the separation distance between them tends to infinity. Calculate the angle between $\vec u_\infty$ and the initial line of motion of $e^+$. Give your answer in degrees.

*Hint 1: The eccentricty $\varepsilon$ of a conic trajectory for the present case is given by*

$$\varepsilon = \sqrt{1 + \frac{4L^2E}{k^2e^4m}}$$

*where $E$ and $L$ are the total energy and the magnitude of the total angular momentum, respectively. $k$ is Coulombs constant $k = 1/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$.*

*Hint 2: The equation of a conic in polar coordinates is*

$$r = \frac{a}{1 - \varepsilon\cos(\theta)}$$

## T1-C [3.5 pts]. Photodissociation of ozone

A photon with angular frequency $\omega$ strikes an ozone molecule O$_3$ at rest, dissociating it (breaking it apart) into an oxygen molecule O$_2$ and an oxygen atom O, as shown in figure 1c. The photon is absorbed in this process. Let $U_i$ and $U_f$ be the ground state energies of the ozone and oxygen molecules, respectively. Assume that this system remains isolated and that its dynamics is classical (potential energies do not contribute to mass and the motion of the Oxygen atoms are non-relativistic). Use the relation $p = E/c$ for the linear momentum of the photon, where $E$ is its energy.

> **Figure description (Fig. 1c).** A photon $\gamma$ (wavy arrow) travels horizontally from left to right and hits the ozone molecule (three atoms). A horizontal dashed line continues the photon's direction. After the collision the O$_2$ molecule (two atoms) moves off to the upper right along a dashed line making angle $\theta$ above the horizontal (photon) direction, and the single O atom moves off to the lower right.

**T1-C1 [2.5 pts].** If the angle that the momentum of the outgoing O$_2$ with respect to the incident photon is $\theta$, determine the minimum angular frequency $\omega_{\min}$ required for this dissociation to occur at this angle. Write your answer in terms of $\hbar$, $c$, $\theta$, $\Delta U = U_f - U_i$, and the mass $m$ of an oxygen atom.

**T1-C2 [1.0 pts].** If $\theta = \pi/6$, calculate the value of $\hbar\omega_{\min} - \Delta U$. Give your answer in electronvolts (eV). Take $\Delta U = 1.10\ \text{eV}$ and $m = 16.0$ amu.

*Hint:*

$$(1+x)^r = 1 + rx + \frac{r(r-1)}{2!}x^2 + \frac{r(r-1)(r-2)}{3!}x^3 + \cdots$$
