A2. Substituting the form of the solution into the wave equation:

$$\frac{\partial^2A}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2A}{\partial y^2} + \left(\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon}{c^2} - \beta^2\right)A = 0.$$

Considering that $\partial^2A/\partial y^2 \ll \partial^2A/\partial x^2$, and that outside the fiber $\varepsilon = 1$, we get:

$$k_{in} = \sqrt{\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon(\omega)}{c^2} - \beta^2},\qquad k_{out} = \sqrt{\beta^2 - \frac{\omega^2}{c^2}}$$

A3. We can write Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction for a rectangular circuit with vertices at the points $(a/2 - \delta a, y, z)$, $(a/2 - \delta a, y, z + \Delta z)$, $(a/2 + \delta a, y, z + \Delta z)$, $(a/2 + \delta a, y, z)$. At $\delta a \to 0$ the derivative of the magnetic flux tends to 0, while the circulation of the electric field strength keeps the same form; hence the circulation is also equal to 0. At $\delta a \to 0$ the circulation can be expressed as $\Gamma = (E_{\tau 1} - E_{\tau 2})\Delta z = 0$, hence

$$E_{\tau 1} = E_{\tau 2}$$

A5. The proof is similar to A3, but instead of the law of electromagnetic induction, the circulation theorem for the magnetic field strength is used.

A6. Using the differential form of Faraday’s law we get:

$$-\mu_0\frac{\partial H_z}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x}.$$

The derivative of $H_z$ with respect to time can be expressed as

$$\frac{\partial H_z}{\partial t} = i\omega H_z,$$

and therefore

$$H_z = E_0\,\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{i}{\mu_0\omega}\frac{\partial A}{\partial x}\exp(i\omega t - i\beta z)\right)$$

A7. To find $H_z$, differentiate $A$ with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{\partial A}{\partial x} = -k_{in}\sin(k_{in}x),\quad |x| < a/2;\qquad \frac{\partial A}{\partial x} = -k_{out}\exp\bigl(-k_{out}(x - a/2)\bigr)\cos(k_{in}a/2),\quad x > a/2.$$

Equating $H_z$ on both sides of the interface:

$$\tan(k_{in}a/2) = \frac{k_{out}}{k_{in}}$$

Let us bring the expression in brackets to a common denominator and divide the squared modulus of the numerator by the squared modulus of the denominator:

B6.

$$\omega_{res}\tau = \pi(1 + 2n)$$

B7. Let us find the depth of the absorption peak by expanding the expression for the power ratio in a Taylor series:

$$\eta(\omega_{res}) \approx \frac{(1 - \kappa - t_s^2/2)^2}{(1 - \kappa + t_s^2/2)^2} \approx 0,\qquad \Delta\eta_{max} = 1 - \eta(\omega_{res}) \approx 1,$$

or by substituting the value of $\varphi$ from the previous item without expanding: $\eta(\omega_{res}) \approx 1.6\cdot 10^{-6} \approx 0$, $\Delta\eta_{max} \approx 1$.

The sharpness is the ratio of $\omega$ to the width of the region in which $\Delta\eta > \Delta\eta_{max}/2$. Let us estimate the width of this region. Put

$$\varphi = \delta + \pi(1 + 2n),\qquad \delta \ll \pi,$$

and expand in a Maclaurin series in $\delta$:

$$\eta \approx \frac{\delta^2 + (1 - \kappa - t_s^2/2 + \delta^2/2)^2}{\delta^2 + (1 - \kappa + t_s^2/2 + \delta^2/2)^2} \approx \frac{\delta^2}{\delta^2 + (1 - \kappa + t_s^2/2)^2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

From here we get

$$\delta \approx 1 - \kappa + t_s^2/2 \approx 0.01,$$
$$Q = \frac{\omega_{res}}{\Delta\omega} = \frac{\pi(1 + 2n)}{2\delta} \approx \frac{\pi n}{1 - \kappa + t_s^2/2} = \pi\cdot 10^4 = 3.14\cdot 10^4$$

C2. The field amplitude at the exit of the EOM equals

$$E_{EOM}(t) = f(t)\cos(\omega(t)t) = \beta\cos(\omega t) + \frac{1 - \beta}{2}\Bigl(\cos(\Omega + \omega)t + \cos(-\Omega + \omega)t\Bigr),$$

$$\beta(\omega_\mu)L + \pi = 2\pi(\mu + \mu_0).$$
