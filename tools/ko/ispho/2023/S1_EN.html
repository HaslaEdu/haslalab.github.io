Solution

A1. From thermodynamics we know that $v = \sqrt{3kT/m}$, where $m$ is the particle’s mass and $k$ is the Boltzmann constant. The characteristic jump time between inter-nodes is inversely proportional to the transition probability times the particle’s speed, and the diffusion coefficient is inversely proportional to the jump time; therefore

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}},\qquad D(T) \propto v\exp(-E_g/kT) \propto \sqrt{T}\exp(-E_g/kT)$$

A2. First, let us find the rms particle displacement along one of the axes (call it $x$). The motion along it happens as in the one considered in the preparatory lectures, so the mean square displacement is

$$\overline{x^2} = na^2,$$

where $n$ is the total number of jumps. The number of jumps can be estimated as $n = t/\tau$. With $D = a^2/2\tau$ we obtain

$$\overline{x^2} = 2Dt.$$

Finally, because the motions along different axes can be considered independent, $\overline{{y^2}} = \overline{{z^2}} = 2Dt$. By the Pythagorean theorem,

$$\Delta r_{sq} = \sqrt{\overline{x^2} + \overline{y^2} + \overline{z^2}} = \sqrt{6Dt}$$

A3. Let us use the result of the previous task to find the diffusion coefficient. Because $\Delta r_{{sq}}^2 = 6Dt$, $D = \Delta r_{{sq}}^2/6t$. Combining this with A1, $\Delta r_{{sq}}^2/(6t\sqrt{{T}}) \propto \exp(-E_g/kT)$. The easiest way now is to find the slope of the dependence of $Y = \ln\left(\Delta r_{{sq}}^2t^{{-1}}T^{{-1/2}}\right)$ on $X = 1/T$.

How the accuracy is guaranteed. First, it is good to have $\Delta r_{{sq}} \gg 1$. Second, $1/T$ changes by $0.9$, and $E_g$ is proportional to the slope. To guarantee $2\,\%$ accuracy the errors at the minimum and maximum temperatures must satisfy $\sigma_Y(0.1) + \sigma_Y(1.0) \le 0.02\cdot 0.9E_g$. Because $Y = \ln[\Delta r_{{sq}}^2T^{{-1/2}}]$ (up to a constant), this means $2\varepsilon_r(0.1) + 2\varepsilon_r(1.0) \le 0.018E_g$, i.e. it suffices that $\varepsilon_r = \sigma_r/\Delta r_{{sq}} \le 0.0045E_g$ at every point. B1. From the question paper we can conclude that the grain boundaries perpendicular to the diffusion flow do not affect the effective diffusion coefficient. Of the three families of boundaries (total relative volume $\delta$), the two parallel to the diffusion direction carry $2\delta/3$, so

$$D_{eff} \approx \frac{2\delta D_{gb}}{3}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \alpha = \frac{2}{3}$$

B2. The simulation results are better for larger $D_{{gb}}/D$ and smaller $\delta$. It is also better to use the maximum simulation time, since type A diffusion appears on large time scales. So let us take $(t, D_{{gb}}/D, \delta) = (5, 5, 0.005)$ as the starting point. The plots in B.nb should ideally have the slope $k = -1/(4D_{{eff}}t)$, so in the program’s units

$$D_{eff} = \frac{1}{4|k|t} = 1 + \alpha\delta(D_{gb}/D - 1).$$

With the coordinates $X = \delta(D_{{gb}}/D - 1)$ and $Y = \dfrac{{1}}{{4|k|t}} - 1$ this dependence is linear. The best way to make $X$ change considerably is to increase $\delta$. Lowering the Width slider in B.nb also helps against edge effects. The data points ($t = 5$, $D_{{gb}}/D = 5$):
