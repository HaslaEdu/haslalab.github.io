General instructions: experimental exam (20 points)

HPhO mock exam 1

If you do not follow these rules, you may be disqualified.

The experimental exam lasts 5 hours and is worth 20 points in total. It has two problems: E‑1 “Water creeping into paper” (10 points) and E‑2 “Colours made by stress” (10 points).

In HPhO rounds the experiments are done on the HaslaLab virtual bench. This sample paper is written for real equipment; without it, practise the analysis with the sample measurements in the solution.

Before the exam

  • Do not look at the problems before the exam screen opens. Only the following may be on your desk:
    (1) pencils, pens and an eraser, (2) one scientific calculator, (3) a ruler, (4) the printed answer sheets and writing sheets

During the exam

  • Record your measurements in the tables on the answer sheets, with the quantity and its unit at the head of each column. Write each reading with the digits you actually read.
  • Draw graphs on the grids of the answer sheets. Label the axes with quantities and units, mark the scales and draw the fitted line or curve. Find a slope from two points on the fitted line, not from data points, and mark the two points on the graph.
  • Write each final answer in its box on the answer sheet, with significant figures that match its uncertainty. Write on the front of each page only. Nothing written on the back of a page is graded.
  • For working, use only the Writing Sheets. Cross out with a large X anything you do not want graded.
  • You may not leave your seat during the exam, and you may not get help from anyone, from AI, from the internet or from books. Questions about the problems are not answered.

After the exam

  • Stop writing as soon as the end of the exam is announced.
  • Photograph every answer sheet and every writing sheet you used, one page at a time, and upload them before the upload closes, in this order: Answer Sheets (A), Writing Sheets (W), each in page order.
  • Write only your candidate code on the answer sheets and writing sheets. Do not write your name, your school or anything else that identifies you.

Detailed instructions

  • Unless stated otherwise, use the constants and data given in each problem. State the units of the quantities in every numerical calculation.
