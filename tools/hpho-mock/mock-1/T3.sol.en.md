## Part A - Hyperbolic flyby (2.8 pt)

::: intro
Syllabus mechanics (conservation of energy and angular momentum, Kepler motion). The polar form of the hyperbolic orbit and $\cos\nu_\infty = -1/e$ are given in the text [5].
:::

::: q A.1 0.5pt
Speed at perihelion.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Far away the potential energy is zero and the speed is $v_\infty$, so

$$\tfrac12 v_q^2 - \frac{GM_\odot}{q} = \tfrac12 v_\infty^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; v_q = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + \frac{2GM_\odot}{q}}.$$

With $q = 0.500\,\mathrm{AU} = 7.480\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m}$,

$$v_q = \sqrt{9.00\times10^{8} + 3.549\times10^{9}} = 6.67\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} = 66.7\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.$$

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $v_q = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + 2GM_\odot/q}$ | 0.3 |
| $v_q = 66.7\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ (66–67) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.2 0.5pt
Impact parameter.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The angular momentum per unit mass is $bv_\infty$ far away and $qv_q$ at perihelion, so

$$b = \frac{qv_q}{v_\infty} = \frac{7.480\times10^{10}\times6.67\times10^{4}}{3.00\times10^{4}} = 1.66\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.11\,\mathrm{AU}.$$

To reach a perihelion of 0.5 AU the body must be aimed 1.11 AU from the Sun. The Sun's gravity pulls it further in than it was aimed.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $bv_\infty = qv_q$ | 0.3 |
| $b = 1.11\,\mathrm{AU}$ (1.09–1.13) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.3 0.5pt
Eccentricity.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The perihelion distance is $q = a(e-1)$, and the energy gives $a = GM_\odot/v_\infty^2$. Hence

$$e = 1 + \frac{q}{a} = 1 + \frac{qv_\infty^2}{GM_\odot} = 1 + \frac{7.480\times10^{10}\times9.00\times10^{8}}{1.327\times10^{20}} = 1.507.$$

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $e = 1 + qv_\infty^2/GM_\odot$ | 0.3 |
| $e = 1.507$ (1.50–1.52) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.4 0.7pt
Deflection angle.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$\cos\nu_\infty = -\frac1e = -0.6636 \;\Longrightarrow\; \nu_\infty = 131.56^\circ.$$

The incoming asymptote lies at $\nu = -\nu_\infty$ and the outgoing one at $\nu = +\nu_\infty$. The angle between the two velocities is

$$\delta = 2\nu_\infty - 180^\circ = 2\times131.56^\circ - 180^\circ = 83.1^\circ.$$

The Sun turns the visitor through 83 degrees. Its speed far away is still exactly the same on the way in and on the way out.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\nu_\infty = \arccos(-1/e) = 131.6^\circ$ | 0.3 |
| $\delta = 2\nu_\infty - 180^\circ$ | 0.2 |
| $\delta = 83.1^\circ$ (82–84) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.5 0.6pt
Estimate of the time spent within 1 AU.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The speed at $r = 1\,\mathrm{AU}$ is

$$v_1 = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + \frac{2GM_\odot}{1\,\mathrm{AU}}} = \sqrt{9.00\times10^{8} + 1.774\times10^{9}} = 5.17\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.$$

The perihelion, 0.5 AU, is of the same order as 1 AU. The orbit therefore cuts deep across the circle, the path inside it is about one diameter, $2\,\mathrm{AU}$, and the speed stays between $v_1$ and $v_q$ without large changes. So

$$t \approx \frac{2\,\mathrm{AU}}{v_1} = \frac{2\times1.496\times10^{11}}{5.17\times10^{4}} = 5.8\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 67\,\text{days}.$$

Using $v_q$ instead gives 52 days, so the true value lies between 50 and 70 days, a little over two months. That is all the time there is to observe the body. It is why the interstellar objects found so far faded from view a few weeks after discovery.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| States the approximation: path length $\sim 2\,\mathrm{AU}$, speed $\sim v_1$ | 0.3 |
| $v_1 = 5.17\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ | 0.1 |
| $t \approx 60$ days (accept 45–80 days) | 0.2 |
:::

## Part B - How often do they come? (1.7 pt)

::: intro
Syllabus mechanics (conservation of angular momentum) and probability and statistics (cross-section and count rate). No further concepts needed.
:::

::: q B.1 0.8pt
Gravitationally focused cross-section.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Applying the relation of A.2 to $q_\mathrm{max}$,

$$b_\mathrm{max} = \frac{q_\mathrm{max}v_{q,\mathrm{max}}}{v_\infty} = q_\mathrm{max}\sqrt{1 + \frac{2GM_\odot}{q_\mathrm{max}v_\infty^2}},$$

$$\sigma = \pi b_\mathrm{max}^2 = \pi q_\mathrm{max}^2\left(1 + \frac{2GM_\odot}{q_\mathrm{max}v_\infty^2}\right).$$

The factor in parentheses is the gravitational focusing factor. For $q_\mathrm{max} = 1\,\mathrm{AU}$,

$$1 + \frac{2\times1.327\times10^{20}}{1.496\times10^{11}\times9.00\times10^{8}} = 1 + 1.97 = 2.97,$$

$$\sigma = \pi(1.496\times10^{11})^2\times2.97 = 2.09\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{m^2}.$$

The Sun casts a net almost three times wider than its geometric area.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $b_\mathrm{max} = q_\mathrm{max}v_{q,\mathrm{max}}/v_\infty$ | 0.3 |
| $\sigma = \pi q_\mathrm{max}^2[1 + 2GM_\odot/(q_\mathrm{max}v_\infty^2)]$ | 0.2 |
| Focusing factor $2.97$ | 0.1 |
| $\sigma = 2.09\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ (2.0–2.2) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q B.2 0.6pt
Number passing per year.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$n = \frac{0.10}{(1.496\times10^{11})^3} = 2.99\times10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}},$$

$$\mathcal{R} = n\sigma v_\infty = 2.99\times10^{-35}\times2.09\times10^{23}\times3.00\times10^{4} = 1.87\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},$$

$$\mathcal{R}\times3.156\times10^{7} = 5.9\ \text{per year}.$$

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $n = 2.99\times10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ | 0.2 |
| $\mathcal{R} = n\sigma v_\infty$ | 0.2 |
| $5.9$ per year (5–7) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q B.3 0.3pt
Comparison with the number found.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

About 53 should have passed in nine years, and 3 were found. The detection efficiency is only $3/53 \approx 6\,\%$. Most were missed because they were too faint or crossed parts of the sky that were not being watched. The number density $n$ in this problem must therefore include the bodies that were missed, not only those that were observed.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Concludes that the detection efficiency is low, of order $10\,\%$ | 0.3 |
:::

## Part C - The force that pushes the orbit off course (3.5 pt)

::: intro
Syllabus mechanics (conservation of momentum, variable-mass systems), thermodynamics (latent heat) and the angular momentum of a rigid body. The notation $A_1/r^2$ for the non-gravitational acceleration is given in the text [1, 3].
:::

::: q C.1 1.2pt
Non-gravitational acceleration.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The volume and mass of the spheroid are

$$V = \tfrac43\pi a_1a_2a_3 = \tfrac43\pi\times300\times50\times50 = 3.14\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{m^3},$$

$$M = \rho V = 500\times3.14\times10^{6} = 1.57\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{kg}.$$

At distance $r$ the solar flux is $S = S_0(1\,\mathrm{AU}/r)^2$, and the absorbed power is

$$P = (1-A)\,S\,A_\odot.$$

At $r = q = 0.5\,\mathrm{AU}$, $S = 1361/0.25 = 5444\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, so

$$P = 0.96\times5444\times3.75\times10^{4} = 1.96\times10^{8}\,\mathrm{W}.$$

All of this power goes into sublimation:

$$\dot m = \frac{P}{L_s} = \frac{1.96\times10^{8}}{2.8\times10^{6}} = 70.0\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}.$$

The gas leaves in one direction with speed $u$, and the reaction gives

$$F = \dot m\,u = 70.0\times400 = 2.80\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}, \qquad a_\mathrm{ng} = \frac{F}{M} = \frac{2.80\times10^{4}}{1.57\times10^{9}} = 1.78\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.$$

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $M = \rho\cdot\tfrac43\pi a_1a_2a_3 = 1.57\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{kg}$ | 0.3 |
| $P = (1-A)S_0(1\,\mathrm{AU}/r)^2A_\odot = 1.96\times10^{8}\,\mathrm{W}$ | 0.3 |
| $\dot m = P/L_s = 70.0\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | 0.2 |
| $F = \dot m u$, $a_\mathrm{ng} = F/M$ | 0.2 |
| $a_\mathrm{ng} = 1.78\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ (1.7–1.9) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.2 0.5pt
$A_1$ and the ratio to solar gravity.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Since $a_\mathrm{ng} \propto S \propto r^{-2}$, it has the form $a_\mathrm{ng} = A_1/r^2$. With $r$ in AU,

$$A_1 = a_\mathrm{ng}(q)\times(0.500)^2 = 1.78\times10^{-5}\times0.250 = 4.45\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.$$

At perihelion the Sun's gravitational acceleration is $GM_\odot/q^2 = 2.37\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, so

$$\frac{a_\mathrm{ng}}{g_\odot} = \frac{1.78\times10^{-5}}{2.37\times10^{-2}} = 7.5\times10^{-4}.$$

This is less than a thousandth of gravity. Precise tracking of the orbit still reveals a force this small.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $A_1 = 4.45\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ (4.2–4.7) | 0.3 |
| $a_\mathrm{ng}/g_\odot = 7.5\times10^{-4}$ (7–8) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.3 0.6pt
Accumulated velocity change.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$\tau \approx \frac{q}{v_q} = \frac{7.480\times10^{10}}{6.67\times10^{4}} = 1.12\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{s} = 13.0\,\text{days},$$

$$\Delta v \approx a_\mathrm{ng}\tau = 1.78\times10^{-5}\times1.12\times10^{6} = 20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}, \qquad \frac{\Delta v}{v_\infty} = 6.7\times10^{-4}.$$

The velocity changes by less than a thousandth. Over several months of position predictions this grows into an offset of tens of thousands of kilometres, which is the deviation actually observed.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\tau = q/v_q = 1.12\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{s}$ | 0.2 |
| $\Delta v = 20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (18–22) | 0.2 |
| $\Delta v/v_\infty = 6.7\times10^{-4}$ | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.4 0.4pt
Fraction of mass lost.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$\Delta M = \dot m\tau = 70.0\times1.12\times10^{6} = 7.8\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{kg}, \qquad \frac{\Delta M}{M} = \frac{7.8\times10^{7}}{1.57\times10^{9}} = 5.0\,\%.$$

One passage costs a twentieth of the mass. A body like this cannot pass through the Solar System many times.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\Delta M = \dot m\tau = 7.8\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{kg}$ | 0.2 |
| $\Delta M/M = 5.0\,\%$ (4.5–5.5) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.5 0.8pt
Outflow torque and spin.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$I = \tfrac15M(a_1^2 + a_2^2) = \tfrac15\times1.57\times10^{9}\times(9.00\times10^{4} + 2.50\times10^{3}) = 2.91\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2},$$

$$\Omega = \frac{2\pi}{T_\mathrm{rot}} = \frac{2\pi}{2.628\times10^{4}} = 2.39\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}, \qquad L = I\Omega = 6.95\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}.$$

With the outflow point offset by $0.01a_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$, the torque is $N = F\times3.0 = 8.4\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, and

$$\Delta L = N\tau = 8.4\times10^{4}\times1.12\times10^{6} = 9.4\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\Delta L}{L} = 1.4\times10^{1}.$$

An offset of only $1\,\%$ changes the angular momentum by 14 times its present value. If this were the whole story, the spin would run away during the perihelion passage and the body would be torn apart by its own rotation. The observed body is intact, so one of three things must hold. The outflow is nearly symmetric and the torques cancel, or the real outflow rate is much smaller than estimated here, or the orbital deviation has some cause other than sublimation.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $I = \tfrac15M(a_1^2 + a_2^2) = 2.91\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ | 0.2 |
| $L = I\Omega = 6.95\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | 0.2 |
| $\Delta L = F(0.01a_1)\tau$, $\Delta L/L \approx 14$ | 0.2 |
| Names one of: symmetric outflow, overestimated outflow rate, a different cause | 0.2 |
:::

## Part D - Why has it not broken apart? (2.0 pt)

::: intro
Syllabus mechanics (circular motion, self-gravity, stress). The Roche limit formula is given in the text [4].
:::

::: q D.1 0.6pt
Rotational breakup period.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

At the equator of a sphere of radius $R$ and density $\rho$, the centripetal acceleration must not exceed the surface gravity:

$$\Omega^2R \le \frac{G\cdot\tfrac43\pi R^3\rho}{R^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \Omega^2 \le \tfrac43\pi G\rho.$$

$$T_\mathrm{min} = \frac{2\pi}{\Omega_\mathrm{max}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{G\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{6.674\times10^{-11}\times500}} = 1.68\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{s} = 4.67\,\mathrm{h}.$$

Note that $R$ drops out. The observed $T_\mathrm{rot} = 7.3\,\mathrm{h}$ is longer than $4.67\,\mathrm{h}$, so a spherical body would hold together by self-gravity alone, with no cohesion at all.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\Omega^2R \le \tfrac43\pi G\rho R$, $R$ cancels | 0.3 |
| $T_\mathrm{min} = \sqrt{3\pi/(G\rho)} = 4.67\,\mathrm{h}$ (4.5–4.8) | 0.2 |
| $T_\mathrm{rot} > T_\mathrm{min}$, so a sphere would be safe | 0.1 |
:::

::: q D.2 0.8pt
Tensile stress in an elongated body and the cohesion it needs.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

A homogeneous rod of length $2a_1$ and cross-section $A_c$ spins with angular velocity $\Omega$ about an axis through its centre. The tension carried by the central cross-section is the total centripetal force needed by the outer half:

$$T = \int_0^{a_1}\rho A_c\Omega^2x\,dx = \tfrac12\rho A_c\Omega^2a_1^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \sigma_\mathrm{rot} = \frac{T}{A_c} = \tfrac12\rho\Omega^2a_1^2.$$

$$\sigma_\mathrm{rot} = \tfrac12\times500\times(2.39\times10^{-4})^2\times(300)^2 = 1.29\,\mathrm{Pa}.$$

The sphere of equal volume has radius $R_\mathrm{eq} = (3V/4\pi)^{1/3} = (300\times50\times50)^{1/3} = 90.9\,\mathrm{m}$, so the compression from self-gravity is

$$\sigma_\mathrm{grav} = \tfrac23\pi G\rho^2R_\mathrm{eq}^2 = \tfrac23\pi\times6.674\times10^{-11}\times(500)^2\times(90.9)^2 = 0.29\,\mathrm{Pa}.$$

The body therefore needs a cohesion of at least

$$\sigma_c = \sigma_\mathrm{rot} - \sigma_\mathrm{grav} = 1.29 - 0.29 = 1.0\,\mathrm{Pa}.$$

One pascal is far less than the strength of bread dough, or even of a heap of dry sand. A very weak cohesion is enough to explain why the body is intact. The surprise is not that the required value is large. It is that it cannot be exactly zero.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $T = \int_0^{a_1}\rho A_c\Omega^2x\,dx$, $\sigma_\mathrm{rot} = \tfrac12\rho\Omega^2a_1^2$ | 0.3 |
| $\sigma_\mathrm{rot} = 1.29\,\mathrm{Pa}$ (1.2–1.4) | 0.2 |
| $R_\mathrm{eq} = 90.9\,\mathrm{m}$, $\sigma_\mathrm{grav} = 0.29\,\mathrm{Pa}$ | 0.2 |
| Required cohesion $\approx 1.0\,\mathrm{Pa}$ (0.8–1.2) | 0.1 |
:::

::: q D.3 0.6pt
Roche limit.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$d = 1.26R_\odot\left(\frac{\rho_\odot}{\rho}\right)^{1/3} = 1.26\times6.957\times10^{8}\times\left(\frac{1408}{500}\right)^{1/3} = 1.26\times6.957\times10^{8}\times1.412 = 1.24\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{m},$$

$$d = 8.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{AU}.$$

The perihelion $q = 0.500\,\mathrm{AU}$ is 60 times farther out than the Roche limit, so tidal disruption is no concern at all. The threats to this body are its own spin (D.2) and the outflow torque (C.5), not the Sun's tides.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $d = 1.24\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{m} = 8.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{AU}$ (8–9) | 0.4 |
| $q/d \approx 60$, no tidal disruption | 0.2 |
:::
