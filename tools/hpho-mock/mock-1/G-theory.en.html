General instructions: theoretical exam (30 points)

HPhO mock exam 1

If you do not follow these rules, you may be disqualified.

The theoretical exam lasts 5 hours and is worth 30 points in total. It has three problems: T‑1 “Why does an hourglass run steadily?” (10 points), T‑2 “Hall thruster” (10 points) and T‑3 “A visitor from interstellar space” (10 points).

The time left is shown on the exam screen, with a reminder 30 minutes, 15 minutes and 5 minutes before the end.

Before the exam

  • Do not look at the problems before the exam screen opens. Only the following may be on your desk:
    (1) pencils, pens and an eraser, (2) one scientific calculator, (3) a ruler, (4) the printed answer sheets and writing sheets

During the exam

  • Write your final answers on the Answer Sheets, in the box for each question, with any explanation the box asks for. Write on the front of each page only. Nothing written on the back is graded.
  • For working, use only the Writing Sheets, whose page labels start with W. Cross out with a large X anything you do not want graded. Use the front of each page only and keep the margins clear. Nothing written on the back of a page is graded.
  • Write concisely and legibly. Use equations, logical symbols and sketches to make your ideas clear, and avoid long sentences. You need not use every given variable in your answers.
  • Unless a question asks for it, you need not state the uncertainty of an answer.
  • You may not leave your seat during the exam, and you may not get help from anyone, from AI, from the internet or from books. Questions about the problems are not answered.

After the exam

  • Stop writing as soon as the end of the exam is announced.
  • Photograph every answer sheet and every writing sheet you used, one page at a time, and upload them before the upload closes, in this order: Answer Sheets (A), Writing Sheets (W), each in page order.
  • Write only your candidate code on the answer sheets and writing sheets. Do not write your name, your school or anything else that identifies you.

Detailed instructions

  • Unless stated otherwise, use the constants and data given in each problem. State the units of the quantities in every numerical calculation.
