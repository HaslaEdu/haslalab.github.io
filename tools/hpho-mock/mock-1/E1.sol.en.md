::: box General rules
- This is an experimental problem, so marks are given for acceptance ranges rather than for a single true value. Where two ranges appear in brackets, the wider range earns partial marks and the narrower range full marks.
- Where an uncertainty is asked for and missing, only half of the marks for that item are given. An uncertainty between $1/3$ and $3$ times the reference value is accepted.
- A graph needs axis labels with units, and full marks require the data points to cover at least half of the graph area.
- A carried-forward error is penalised only once.
- In Part D, numerical results earn no marks unless the procedure is described.
:::

## Part A - Checking the imbibition law (3.0 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: fluid mechanics (surface tension, viscous flow) and the mathematics appendix (logarithmic plots, least-squares line, error propagation). Washburn's law is outside the syllabus and is given in the text [2].
:::

::: q A.1 0.8pt
Data $L(t_{\text{time}})$.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

For typical paper the front takes about $500\,\mathrm{s}$ to reach $L = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Because the front is blurred, the uncertainty in $L$ should be taken larger than the smallest ruler division, at least $\pm1\,\mathrm{mm}$. The data points should be spaced evenly in $L$, not in $t_{\text{time}}$, so that they are balanced on the logarithmic plot.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| 8 or more points, spread evenly over $L = 2$–$15\,\mathrm{cm}$ | 0.4 |
| Uncertainties of both $L$ and $t_{\text{time}}$ recorded | 0.2 |
| $\delta L \geq 1\,\mathrm{mm}$ (reflects the blurred front) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.2 0.9pt
Slope of the log-log plot.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Since $L = (K t_{\text{time}})^{1/2}$, $\log L = \tfrac12\log t_{\text{time}} + \tfrac12\log K$ and the slope should be $n = 0.500$. Real measurements give about

$$n = 0.50 \pm 0.03.$$

A value of $n$ clearly below 0.5 means that the container was not closed well and evaporation crept in (without the lid one gets $n \approx 0.47$).

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Both axes labelled with units, points cover at least half of the graph | 0.3 |
| Slope $n = 0.50$ (0.44–0.56) | 0.3 |
| $\delta n$ from least squares or from maximum and minimum slopes | 0.2 |
| States agreement with Washburn's prediction $1/2$ | 0.1 |
:::

::: q A.3 0.8pt
Determination of $K$.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The graph of $L^2$ against $t_{\text{time}}$ is a straight line through the origin with slope $K$:

$$K = 2.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}.$$

A clearly non-zero intercept indicates a systematic error in the immersion depth or in the starting time.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Straight $L^2$–$t_{\text{time}}$ line, passage through the origin checked | 0.3 |
| $K = 2.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ (1.4–2.8) | 0.3 |
| $\delta K$ calculated | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.4 0.5pt
Dynamic effective pore radius.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$r_d = \frac{2\mu_w K}{\gamma_w} = \frac{2\times1.00\times10^{-3}\times2.0\times10^{-5}}{0.0720} = 5.6\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.56\,\mathrm{\mu m}.$$

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $r_d = 2\mu_w K/\gamma_w$ | 0.2 |
| $r_d = 0.56\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ (0.4–0.8) | 0.2 |
| $\delta r_d/r_d = \delta K/K$ | 0.1 |
:::

## Part B - What is paper made of? (2.0 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: measurement techniques (mass and length, measuring a stack) and error propagation.
:::

::: q B.1 0.7pt
Thickness and absorbed mass.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Measuring a stack of $n$ strips reduces the uncertainty in the thickness by a factor $1/n$. Chromatography paper is about $t = 0.18\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick. At $L = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ the absorbed mass is about

$$m = \varphi\rho_w b t L \approx 0.65\times1000\times0.015\times1.8\times10^{-4}\times0.12 = 0.21\,\mathrm{g},$$

a little over 20 divisions of a $0.01\,\mathrm{g}$ balance. The strip must be weighed at once, because the value drops by evaporation if there is any delay.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Thickness measured on a stack, $t = 0.18\,\mathrm{mm}$ (0.15–0.22) | 0.4 |
| $m = 0.21\,\mathrm{g}$ (0.12–0.30), weighed at once | 0.3 |
:::

::: q B.2 0.8pt
Porosity.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$\varphi = \frac{m}{\rho_w b t L} = 0.65.$$

The uncertainty is dominated by $t$: $\delta\varphi/\varphi = \sqrt{(\delta m/m)^2 + (\delta t/t)^2 + (\delta L/L)^2} \approx 10\,\%$.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\varphi = m/(\rho_w b t L)$ | 0.3 |
| $\varphi = 0.65$ (0.45–0.80) | 0.3 |
| $\delta\varphi$ calculated, $t$ identified as the dominant term | 0.2 |
:::

::: q B.3 0.5pt
Comparison of $r_d$ with the fibre spacing.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Cellulose fibres are about $20\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ in diameter, and at a porosity of 0.65 the gaps between them are of the same order, about $10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. That is some 20 times larger than $r_d = 0.56\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Possible reasons: (i) the real flow path winds about and is much longer than the apparent length (tortuosity); (ii) the pore sizes are widely spread, and the narrowest throats control how fast the front advances; (iii) the contact angle is not zero, so $\cos\theta < 1$. So $r_d$ is not a geometric hole size. It is a measure of the resistance to flow.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Gap size estimated as $\sim10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, noted to be at least 10 times $r_d$ | 0.3 |
| At least one of: tortuosity, pore-size distribution, $\cos\theta < 1$ | 0.2 |
:::

## Part C - Changing the surface tension (2.5 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: fluid mechanics (surface tension) and error propagation. No further concepts are needed [3].
:::

::: q C.1 1.0pt
$K_s$ of the surfactant solution.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

In the same paper a lower surface tension makes the imbibition slower. One obtains about

$$K_s = 8.5\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}.$$

A strip must not be used twice, and no trace of the previous liquid should remain when the dish is changed.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| 6 or more points, straight $L^2$–$t_{\text{time}}$ line | 0.4 |
| $K_s = 8.5\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ (6–12) | 0.4 |
| $\delta K_s$ calculated | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.2 0.9pt
Surface tension of the surfactant solution.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

For the same paper, take $r_d\cos\theta$ to be the same. Then

$$\frac{K_s}{K_w} = \frac{\gamma_s/\mu_s}{\gamma_w/\mu_w} \;\Longrightarrow\; \gamma_s = \gamma_w\frac{\mu_s}{\mu_w}\frac{K_s}{K_w} = 72.0\times1.05\times\frac{8.5}{20} = 32\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}.$$

This agrees well with typical values for surfactant solutions above the critical micelle concentration.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\gamma_s = \gamma_w(\mu_s/\mu_w)(K_s/K_w)$ | 0.4 |
| $\gamma_s = 32\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}$ (24–42) | 0.3 |
| $\delta\gamma_s$ calculated | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.3 0.6pt
Direction of the error from the contact-angle assumption.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

What is actually measured is $\gamma\cos\theta$:

$$\gamma_s^{\text{meas}} = \gamma_s^{\text{true}}\,\frac{\cos\theta_s}{\cos\theta_w}.$$

A surfactant improves wetting, so $\theta_s < \theta_w$ and $\cos\theta_s > \cos\theta_w$. Ignoring the difference in contact angle therefore gives a value of $\gamma_s$ larger than the true one. The $32\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}$ found above is an upper bound.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| States that the measured quantity is $\gamma\cos\theta$ | 0.3 |
| $\cos\theta_s > \cos\theta_w$, so an overestimate (upper bound) | 0.3 |
:::

## Part D - Evaporation: design your own measurement (2.5 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: thermodynamics (evaporation), experimental design and error budget. The saturation-length formula is given in the text [4].
:::

::: q D.1 1.0pt
Measurement of the saturation length.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

With evaporation $d(L^2)/dt_{\text{time}} = K\,(1 - (L/L_{\max})^2)$, so the front never stops. It approaches $L_{\max}$ asymptotically, with a time constant of 48 minutes. A **rate** criterion such as "less than $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ in 10 minutes" is met only after 2.6 h, at $L = 23.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Instead, plot $\Delta(L^2)/\Delta t_{\text{time}}$ against $L^2$. The points lie on a straight line whose intercept on the $L^2$ axis is $L_{\max}^2$; its intercept on the vertical axis should equal $K$ from A.3. Set up the strip at the start of the exam and stop when $L$ passes 90 % of the extrapolated $L_{\max}$ (about 80 min, $L \approx 21.6\,\mathrm{cm}$). The result is $L_{\max} = 24\,\mathrm{cm}$.

If the same measurement is repeated with the strip inside the lidded container, $L_{\max}$ becomes clearly larger. This control experiment shows that evaporation is the cause.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Quantitative criterion for ending the run and method for obtaining $L_{\max}$ | 0.4 |
| $L_{\max} = 24\,\mathrm{cm}$ (16–32) | 0.3 |
| $\delta L_{\max}$ calculated | 0.1 |
| Closed-container control confirms evaporation as the cause | 0.2 |
:::

::: q D.2 0.9pt
Evaporation rate.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$E = \frac{\varphi\rho_w t K}{4L_{\max}^2} = \frac{0.65\times1000\times1.8\times10^{-4}\times2.0\times10^{-5}}{4\times(0.24)^2} = 1.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.$$

Per day this is $0.9\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$, a water layer of $0.9\,\mathrm{mm}$ per day, which is typical of evaporation indoors. Since $L_{\max}$ enters squared, it dominates the uncertainty.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $E = \varphi\rho_w t K/(4L_{\max}^2)$ | 0.3 |
| $E = 1.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ (0.4–2.5) | 0.3 |
| Notes that $\delta E/E \approx 2\,\delta L_{\max}/L_{\max}$ dominates | 0.3 |
:::

::: q D.3 0.6pt
Comparison with an independent measurement.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The simplest independent measurement is to follow the loss of mass of water in a dish over time. If a dish of area $A$ loses $\Delta m$ in a time $\Delta t$, then $E = \Delta m/(A\,\Delta t)$. If the two values differ, possible causes are: (i) the wet paper is more exposed to air flow than the dish surface, so it evaporates faster; (ii) the paper is not completely saturated, so its effective thickness is less than $t$; (iii) $L_{\max}$ was extrapolated from data that stopped too early. Agreement within a factor of 3 counts as good.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Independent method (e.g. mass loss of a dish) carried out and $E$ calculated | 0.3 |
| Quantitative comparison of the two values | 0.1 |
| At least one cause of the difference identified | 0.2 |
:::
