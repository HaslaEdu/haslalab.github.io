A $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ communications satellite is launched to geostationary orbit, and its orbit must then be changed by $2.2\,\mathrm{km/s}$. With a chemical rocket this takes $261\,\mathrm{kg}$ of propellant. Half the satellite would be fuel.

Electric propulsion does the same job with $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of propellant. The price is thrust: it is less than the weight of an apple, and the orbit change takes four months. Satellite designers have faced this choice between mass and time since the 1970s.

The device that makes the trade possible is the Hall thruster. In an annular discharge chamber the electrons are held by a magnetic field and circle in the azimuthal direction. The electric field set up by this electron cloud pushes only the heavy xenon ions out along the axis, up to $21\,\mathrm{km/s}$. It is a separator that works on the mass difference: the light particles stay trapped and the heavy ones leave.

### Model

- The discharge chamber is an annulus of mean radius $R_c$ and width $w$ $(w \ll R_c)$. The ions are accelerated over an axial length $L$. The axial direction is $+z$, the azimuthal direction $+\theta$ and the radial direction $+r$.
- The magnetic field is radial with uniform magnitude $B$. The electric field is axial and uniform, and the potential difference across the acceleration region is $U_d$.
- Xenon enters as neutral atoms and is ionized once inside the chamber, becoming Xe$^+$. Each ion starts from rest and is accelerated through the whole potential difference $U_d$ (except in C.3).
- Neglect the magnetic force on the ions while they cross the chamber. The electrons are taken to be almost immobile along the axis; they only drift in the azimuthal direction.
- Downstream, the ion beam meets electrons emitted by the neutralizer and becomes electrically neutral. Neglect the propellant and power used by the neutralizer.

## Part A - How fast are the ions ejected? (2.5 pt)

::: q A.1 0.5pt
A Xe$^+$ ion starts from rest and is accelerated through the whole potential difference $U_d$. Express its exit speed $v_i$ in terms of $e$, $U_d$ and $m_i$, and find its value.
:::

::: q A.2 0.7pt
For an ion current $I_i$, express the ion mass ejected per unit time and the thrust $F$ in terms of $m_i$, $e$, $I_i$ and $v_i$. Find $F$ in mN.
:::

::: q A.3 0.3pt
Find the specific impulse $I_{sp} = v_i/g_0$.
:::

::: q A.4 0.5pt
The fraction of the supplied propellant that is actually ionized and accelerated is called the propellant utilization $\eta_m$. Express $\eta_m$ in terms of $I_i$, $\dot m$, $m_i$ and $e$, and find its value.
:::

::: q A.5 0.5pt
Find the number density $n_i$ of the ions crossing the acceleration region. Compare it with the plasma density $n_e$ in Table 1 and explain in one line why the two differ.
:::

## Part B - What the magnetic field does (3.2 pt)

The magnetic field does not push the ions. It has one job only: to stop the electrons from running straight to the anode. Once the electrons are trapped, a potential difference can be held across the channel, and this potential difference accelerates the ions.

::: q B.1 0.8pt
Take the electron thermal speed to be $v_{te} = \sqrt{2eT_e/m_e}$. Find the radius $r_{Le}$ of the circle an electron describes in the field $B$, and compare it with the channel width $w$.
:::

::: q B.2 0.5pt
Find the radius $r_{Li}$ of the circle described in the same field by an ion moving at speed $v_i$, and compare it with the acceleration length $L$. Combine the two results into a single inequality relating $r_{Le}$, $L$ and $r_{Li}$, and state how it connects to the working principle of the device.
:::

::: q B.3 0.6pt
In uniform, mutually perpendicular fields $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, find the drift velocity at which the net force on an electron vanishes. Give its direction and its magnitude in terms of $E$ and $B$. Find the drift speed $v_d$ for the values of this problem.
:::

::: q B.4 1.3pt
Let $I_H$ be the Hall current flowing in the azimuthal direction (the total azimuthal current through the cross-section $L\times w$ of the acceleration region). Show that the axial component of the Lorentz force exerted on it by the magnetic field equals the thrust. Use this to express $I_H$ in terms of $F$, $B$ and $R_c$, find its value, and state how many times larger it is than the discharge current $I_d$. Then work back from this $I_H$ to the electron number density in the acceleration region, compare it with $n_e$ in Table 1, and discuss whether the model is self-consistent.
:::

## Part C - Ionization and losses (2.0 pt)

::: q C.1 0.7pt
A neutral xenon atom crossing the chamber travels on average a distance $\lambda_\mathrm{ion} = v_n/(n_e\langle\sigma v\rangle)$ before it is ionized by an electron collision. Find this distance and compare it with the acceleration length $L$. Explain why the $\eta_m$ found in A.4 is close to 1.
:::

::: q C.2 0.5pt
The effective energy needed to turn one xenon atom into an ion is $\varepsilon$. Find the fraction of the total discharge power $P = U_d I_d$ that goes into ionization.
:::

::: q C.3 0.8pt
In a real thruster the ion beam spreads with half-angle $\theta$, so only the axial momentum contributes to the thrust, and the ions fall through only $U_\mathrm{eff} = 0.90\,U_d$. Include both corrections and find the thrust and the efficiency defined with the total propellant flow rate,

$$\eta_a = \frac{F^2}{2\dot m P}.$$

The measured values are $83\,\mathrm{mN}$ and $\eta_a = 51\,\%$. Give at least one cause of the remaining difference.
:::

::: q D.1 0.8pt
A satellite of initial mass $m_0$ uses up all of its propellant $m_p$. Find its change of velocity $\Delta v$. Find how much propellant a chemical engine with exhaust speed $v_\mathrm{ch}$ would need for the same $\Delta v$, and how many times the electric propellant mass this is.
:::

::: q D.2 0.5pt
At the flow rate used in A.4, find the time in days needed to use up the propellant $m_p$.
:::

::: q D.3 0.5pt
The discharge power $P$ is supplied by solar cells at $1\,\mathrm{AU}$. Find the required array area.
:::

::: q D.4 0.5pt
