## Part A - Building the polariscope (1.5 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: optics (polarization, Malus's law, birefringence). The stress-optic law is outside the syllabus and is given in the text [1].
:::

::: q A.1 0.6pt
Checking the crossed setting.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

By Malus's law, $I = I_0\cos^2\alpha$, the field is darkest when the transmission axes are at $90^\circ$. Near extinction the brightness changes most slowly with angle. Finding the two ends of the dark range and taking the midpoint gives a more precise setting.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Extinction angle $= 90^\circ$ ($88$–$92^\circ$) | 0.4 |
| Justified by $I \propto \cos^2\alpha$ | 0.2 |
:::

::: q A.2 0.9pt
The role of the quarter-wave plates.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

With linear polarizers only, a dark band (the isoclinic) turns with the specimen as it is rotated. It appears where a principal stress direction is parallel to a polarizer axis and has nothing to do with the size of the stress. Two quarter-wave plates at $45^\circ$ turn the setup into a circular polariscope. The light entering the specimen then has no preferred direction, so the isoclinics vanish and only the isochromatics, which show the stress difference, remain. Fringe orders can be counted only in this setting.

Fringes seen with no load come from residual stress frozen in during moulding, or from stress applied by the clamp. Record this initial order as $N_0$ and subtract it from later readings.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Observes that the dark band turns with the specimen in the linear polariscope | 0.2 |
| Identifies it as an isoclinic, independent of stress magnitude | 0.2 |
| Circular polariscope: isoclinics vanish, only isochromatics remain | 0.3 |
| No-load fringes $=$ residual or clamp stress, corrected with $N_0$ | 0.2 |
:::

## Part B - Measuring the stress fringe value (2.6 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: mechanics (stress) and the mathematics appendix (least-squares line, error propagation).
:::

::: q B.1 1.0pt
$N$ against $F$ data.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

In simple tension $\sigma_2 = 0$ and $\sigma_1 = F/(b_1 t) = F/(1.20\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2})$. The fringe order is therefore uniform over the specimen, and as the load rises the fringes move in from the edges of the specimen. The order is found by raising the load slowly and counting the dark fringes that pass a fixed point. A non-integer order is read by interpolating between two neighbouring dark fringes, or by taking the brightest position as a half order.

Between $0.5$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $N$ grows from about $1.2$ to $7.4$.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| At least 6 steps, $N$ spread from 1 to about 7 | 0.4 |
| Clear description of how non-integer orders were read | 0.3 |
| Uncertainties of both $N$ and $F$ given | 0.3 |
:::

::: q B.2 1.0pt
$f_\sigma$ and $C$.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

$$N = \frac{t}{f_\sigma}\,\frac{F}{b_1 t} = \frac{F}{b_1 f_\sigma} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{slope } s = \frac{dN}{dF} = \frac{1}{b_1 f_\sigma}.$$

The measured slope is about $s = 0.25\,\mathrm{N^{-1}}$, so

$$f_\sigma = \frac{1}{b_1 s} = \frac{1}{0.0200 \times 0.25} = 2.0\times10^{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}},$$

$$C = \frac{\lambda}{f_\sigma} = \frac{546\times10^{-9}}{200} = 2.7\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{Pa^{-1}} = 2.7\times10^{3}\ \text{Brewster}.$$

Polyurethane is more than 30 times as sensitive as polycarbonate. A load of a few kilograms is enough to produce countable fringes.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Derives $s = 1/(b_1 f_\sigma)$ | 0.3 |
| $f_\sigma = 2.0\times10^{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ ($1.5$–$2.6\times10^{2}$) | 0.3 |
| $C = 2.7\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{Pa^{-1}}$ ($2.1$–$3.6$) | 0.2 |
| Computes $\delta f_\sigma$ | 0.2 |
:::

::: q B.3 0.6pt
Meaning of the intercept; error budget.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

A non-zero intercept is the order already present at zero load: the residual and clamp stress seen in A.2. A negative value means the weight of the hanger itself was left out.

The uncertainty is dominated by how precisely $N$ is read. If a fringe is read to $\pm0.2$ of an order, the relative error at $N = 2$ is as large as $10\,\%$. The easiest way to halve it is to increase the load and read at higher orders. The same $\pm0.2$ at $N = 6$ is $3\,\%$. Taking more data points and fitting the slope by least squares has the same effect.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Intercept $=$ residual or clamp stress (or hanger weight) | 0.2 |
| Shows quantitatively that the reading error in $N$ dominates | 0.2 |
| Specific improvement (read at higher orders, more data points, etc.) | 0.2 |
:::

## Part C - A stress map inside a beam (2.9 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: mechanics (bending moment, stress distribution) and graph analysis. The bending formula $\sigma = My/I$ is given in the text [2].
:::

::: q C.1 1.0pt
Fringe positions across the depth.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

In a cross-section of the cantilever the fringes appear as horizontal lines placed symmetrically above and below the neutral axis. $I = th^3/12 = 6.00\times10^{-3}\times(0.0250)^3/12 = 7.81\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m^4}$. With $100\,\mathrm{g}$ on a lever arm of $60\,\mathrm{mm}$, $M = 0.981\times0.060 = 5.9\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The surface stress is $\sigma_{\max} = M(h/2)/I = 9.4\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and the surface order is $N_{\max} = \sigma_{\max}t/f_\sigma = 2.8$. So 2 or 3 fringes are seen near each surface.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| One section chosen, fringe positions $y_N$ tabulated | 0.4 |
| Symmetry about the neutral axis checked, both sides measured | 0.3 |
| Uncertainty of $y_N$ given | 0.3 |
:::

::: q C.2 1.0pt
Linear distribution and the neutral axis.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Since $\sigma = My/I$, $N = \sigma t/f_\sigma \propto y$, and the graph of $N$ against $y_N$ should be a straight line through the origin. Extrapolating the line to $N = 0$ gives the experimental neutral axis. For a homogeneous rectangular section it should coincide with the geometric centre, and in practice it lies within $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ of $h/2$. A larger offset means the specimen was clamped at an angle or an axial load was also present.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Shows that the $N$–$y_N$ graph is linear | 0.4 |
| Neutral axis found by extrapolation, within $\pm1\,\mathrm{mm}$ of the geometric centre | 0.4 |
| Names a cause of an offset (tilted clamping, axial load) | 0.2 |
:::

::: q C.3 0.9pt
$f_\sigma$ again, from bending.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

The slope is $dN/dy = Mt/(I f_\sigma)$, so

$$f_\sigma = \frac{Mt}{I\,(dN/dy)}.$$

With $dN/dy = 2.8/0.0125 = 224\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$,

$$f_\sigma = \frac{5.9\times10^{-2}\times6.00\times10^{-3}}{7.81\times10^{-9}\times224} = 2.0\times10^{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}},$$

in agreement with B.2.

If the two values disagree, the tension value (B.2) is the more reliable. In tension the stress is uniform over the specimen and $\sigma_1 - \sigma_2 = F/A$ holds exactly. In bending, systematic errors enter from (i) stress concentration near the clamped end, (ii) uncertainty in the lever arm and the point where the load acts, and (iii) the change in moment caused by large deflection.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Derives $f_\sigma = Mt/[I(dN/dy)]$ | 0.3 |
| $f_\sigma = 2.0\times10^{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ ($1.4$–$2.8\times10^{2}$) | 0.3 |
| Judges the tension value more reliable, with specific reasons | 0.3 |
:::

## Part D - How much does a hole raise the stress? (3.0 pt)

::: small
Syllabus: mechanics (stress concentration) and experimental design. The infinite-plate value $K_t = 3$ is given in the text [3, 4].
:::

::: q D.1 1.2pt
Design and execution of the $K_t$ measurement.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

A good design contains the following.

**Choice of load.** If the far-field order is about 3, the order at the edge of the hole is about 9, and both can be read precisely. A load of $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $\sigma_\infty = F/(b_2 t) = 19.6/(1.80\times10^{-4}) = 0.109\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $N_\infty = \sigma_\infty t/f_\sigma = 3.3$.

**Definition of "far".** The effect of the hole falls below $2\,\%$ at about three hole diameters, so the far-field order is read at least $3d = 18\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the centre of the hole along the axis. The gripped ends of the specimen must be avoided.

**The difficulty at the edge.** The fringes are crowded at the edge and easy to miscount. The surest method is to raise the load slowly from zero and count the fringes that pass one point beside the hole. A rough machined hole surface also produces local false fringes. It is better to read at points about $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ outside the edge and extrapolate to the edge than to read right at it.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| Load chosen so that the far-field order is about 3, with reason | 0.3 |
| "Far" defined quantitatively as $\geq 3d$ | 0.3 |
| Method for the edge order (counting while loading, etc.) | 0.3 |
| $N_\infty$ and $N_{\text{edge}}$ given with uncertainties | 0.3 |
:::

::: q D.2 0.9pt
The value of $K_t$.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

Both readings are on the same specimen, so $t$ and $f_\sigma$ cancel and the ratio of orders equals the ratio of stresses.

$$K_t = \frac{N_{\text{edge}}}{N_\infty} = \frac{9.4}{3.3} = 2.9,$$

$$\frac{\delta K_t}{K_t} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\delta N_{\text{edge}}}{N_{\text{edge}}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\delta N_\infty}{N_\infty}\right)^2} \approx 6\,\% \qquad (\text{for } \delta N = \pm0.2),$$

so $K_t = 2.9 \pm 0.2$. The uncertainty of $f_\sigma$ cancels and does not enter. This is the strength of the method.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $K_t = N_{\text{edge}}/N_\infty$, notes that $t$ and $f_\sigma$ cancel | 0.3 |
| $K_t = 2.9$ ($2.4$–$3.4$) | 0.3 |
| Computes $\delta K_t$ | 0.3 |
:::

::: q D.3 0.9pt
Effect of the finite width.
:::

#### SOLUTION:

With a hole the load must pass through the remaining width $b_2 - d$, so a $K_t$ referred to the far-field stress also contains the effect of the reduced section. Here $b_2/d = 5.0$, and the net-section stress is

$$\sigma_{\text{net}} = \frac{F}{(b_2 - d)\,t} = \frac{b_2}{b_2 - d}\,\sigma_\infty = 1.25\,\sigma_\infty,$$

already $25\,\%$ higher. The finite width should therefore make $K_t$ larger. In fact Howland's solution gives $2.85$ at $d/b_2 = 0.2$, slightly below the infinite-plate value $3.00$. The free edges smooth the stress peak, and this effect is a little larger than the increase from the reduced section. Full marks are given for pointing out that the two effects act in opposite directions.

Causes of the remaining difference include (i) roughness and residual stress at the machined hole surface, (ii) a systematic undercount of the crowded fringes at the edge, and (iii) a stress that is not fully uniform through the thickness of the specimen.

#### MARKING SCHEME:

::: marking
| $\sigma_{\text{net}}/\sigma_\infty = b_2/(b_2 - d) = 1.25$ | 0.3 |
| Points out that section reduction and free-edge relief act in opposite directions | 0.3 |
| Names at least one cause of the remaining difference | 0.3 |
:::
