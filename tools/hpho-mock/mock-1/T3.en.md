In October 2017 a telescope in Hawaii picked up a strange point of light. When its orbit was fitted, it came out as a hyperbola, not an ellipse. The object had never been bound to the Sun. It had left another star and was passing through the Solar System, the first such visitor ever seen.

A few months later something stranger turned up. The object was slowly drifting off the orbit computed from gravity alone. The deviation was very small, but observational error could not explain it. Something was streaming off the surface and pushing the body like a very weak rocket.

In this problem we follow one such visitor from start to finish. We ask what orbit it follows, how often such bodies arrive, what pushes them off their orbits, and why this long, thin body has not yet broken apart.

### Model

- Treat the body as a two-body problem in which only the Sun's gravity acts. Its speed far from the Sun is $v_\infty$ and its perihelion distance is $q$. Neglect perturbations by the planets.
- The body is a homogeneous prolate spheroid with semi-major axis $a_1 = 300\,\mathrm{m}$, semi-minor axes $a_2 = a_3 = 50\,\mathrm{m}$ and density $\rho$. It spins about an axis perpendicular to the long axis with period $T_\mathrm{rot}$.
- All the sunlight that the surface does not reflect goes into sublimating ice. The gas leaves with speed $u$ in the direction towards the Sun. The mean area projected towards the Sun is $A_\odot$.
- Interstellar bodies are spread uniformly around the Sun with number density $n$. All of them have the same $v_\infty$ and arrive from random directions.

## Part A - Hyperbolic flyby (2.8 pt)

::: q A.1 0.5pt
Use conservation of energy and of angular momentum to express the speed at perihelion, $v_q$, in terms of $v_\infty$, $GM_\odot$ and $q$. Find its value.
:::

::: q A.2 0.5pt
Find the impact parameter $b$, the distance between the actual asymptote and the line through the Sun parallel to $\mathbf{v}_\infty$. Give its value in AU.
:::

::: q A.3 0.5pt
Express the eccentricity $e$ of the orbit in terms of $q$, $v_\infty$ and $GM_\odot$. Find its value.
:::

::: q A.4 0.7pt
The asymptotes of the hyperbola point in the directions where the true anomaly $\nu_\infty$ satisfies $\cos\nu_\infty = -1/e$. Find the deflection angle, that is, the angle between the incoming and the outgoing velocity.
:::

::: q A.5 0.6pt
Estimate how long the body stays within $1\,\mathrm{AU}$ of the Sun. Do not integrate exactly. Use a reasonable approximation and state which approximation you used and why.
:::

## Part B - How often do they come? (1.7 pt)

::: q B.1 0.8pt
Find the largest impact parameter for which the perihelion is smaller than $q_\mathrm{max}$. From it, express the effective cross-section $\sigma$ in terms of $q_\mathrm{max}$, $GM_\odot$ and $v_\infty$. For $q_\mathrm{max} = 1\,\mathrm{AU}$, find the gravitational focusing factor and the value of $\sigma$.
:::

::: q B.2 0.6pt
Use the number density $n$ to find how many interstellar bodies with perihelion inside $1\,\mathrm{AU}$ pass by per year.
:::

::: q B.3 0.3pt
Three interstellar objects were actually discovered between 2017 and 2025. Compare this with B.2 and state in one line what you conclude.
:::

## Part C - The force that pushes the orbit off course (3.5 pt)

::: q C.1 1.2pt
At distance $r$ from the Sun, find in turn the absorbed radiant power, the mass sublimated per second, and the non-gravitational acceleration $a_\mathrm{ng}$ produced by the reaction of the outflow. Compute the mass of the body directly from the given semi-axes and density. Find the value of $a_\mathrm{ng}$ at $r = q$.
:::

::: q C.2 0.5pt
Write $a_\mathrm{ng} = A_1/r^2$. Find the value of $A_1$ and the ratio of $a_\mathrm{ng}$ to the Sun's gravitational acceleration at $r = q$.
:::

::: q C.3 0.6pt
Take the time during which the non-gravitational acceleration acts effectively near perihelion to be $\tau \approx q/v_q$. Find the velocity change $\Delta v$ accumulated in this time. What fraction of $v_\infty$ is it?
:::

::: q C.4 0.4pt
Find the fraction of the total mass lost in the same time.
:::

::: q C.5 0.8pt
If the gas leaves from a point away from the centre of mass, it exerts a torque on the spin. Suppose the outflow point is offset from the centre of mass by $0.01\,a_1$ along the long axis. Compare the change in angular momentum during $\tau$ with the present spin angular momentum. The moment of inertia of a homogeneous spheroid about an axis along a short axis is $I = \tfrac15 M(a_1^2 + a_2^2)$. What does the result imply?
:::

## Part D - Why has it not broken apart? (2.0 pt)

::: q D.1 0.6pt
A rubble pile with no cohesion at all is held together only by its own gravity. Express the lower limit $T_\mathrm{min}$ of its rotation period in terms of $G$ and $\rho$, and find its value. Compare it with the observed $T_\mathrm{rot}$.
:::

::: q D.2 0.8pt
Model the body as a homogeneous rod of length $2a_1$ and constant cross-section. Find the tensile stress that the rotation produces at the middle of the long axis. Estimate the compressive stress from self-gravity as $\tfrac23\pi G\rho^2 R_\mathrm{eq}^2$, where $R_\mathrm{eq}$ is the radius of the sphere of equal volume. Find the minimum cohesion the body needs to stay intact.
:::

::: q D.3 0.6pt
A body with no cohesion survives the Sun's tidal force only if its perihelion lies outside the Roche limit $d = 2.44\,R_\odot(\rho_\odot/\rho)^{1/3}$. Find $d$ in AU and compare it with $q$.
:::
