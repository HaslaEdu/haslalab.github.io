`monthly-problems.mjs` defines the public subquestion forms shared by the Worker and native printable HTML sheets. Every answered part includes both a final answer and working; a part may be left completely blank for partial submission. The Worker validates known part IDs, enforces field limits, stores the complete structured sheet, and sends its labelled text to the existing grader. Unanswered parts receive zero, and awarded points require evidence from the corresponding part's user-entered answer or working.

Each account has at most three accepted submissions per problem, across typed and photo-derived answers and after the monthly deadline. The server atomically saves a pending submission before calling the grader. A request identifier makes network retries idempotent and rejects identifier reuse with different content. Invalid forms, drafts and OCR do not consume attempts. Accepted pending/error submissions do count because the answer is preserved; the existing administrator regrade acts on that same submission. Legacy submissions count toward the same limit.

Each problem records the account's highest graded score, rather than adding attempts: 85, 90 and 88 points record 90 points. The monthly total is the sum of these per-problem best scores submitted within the original window. Final ranking ties retain the live ranking's earliest-attempt rule, with account ID as an internal deterministic final tie break.

From November 2026 onward, final monthly ranks 1, 2 and 3 receive 9, 6 and 3 exam tokens. The result announcement time is the latest closing timestamp among the month's non-retired problems (November: December 1, 2026 00:00 KST). The primary Worker cron waits for on-time pending/error grades to be resolved, then freezes the final result and credits the existing token balance and `lab_ledger` in one transaction. AI-suspected ungraded attempts are excluded. Repeated cron runs cannot pay twice; practice after closing and later regrades cannot alter the published final snapshot or trigger another award. Public GETs and the read replica only select the result and its award descriptor; they never initialize schema or credit tokens. Deleted winners remain anonymous in a published result. Unoccupied prize ranks are not paid.

The photo mode is separate from the unchanged LaTeX fields. It opens the same `sim/scan.html` environment as exam mode through `sim/monthly-scan.html`: QR handoff, full-page capture, automatic/manual corner correction, rotation, original comparison and retakes. The selected task is the printed answer-sheet page number; continuation working sheets attach to that page. The desktop shows uploaded pages and read-only extracted final answers and working. Upstage OCR is partitioned on the server into that page's allowed parts, retaining only text supported by the transcript. A failed extraction remains visible for removal and retake and cannot consume a grading attempt. Printable sheets are native HTML at `sim/monthly-answer-sheet.html`; trusted figures use transparent SVGs and inherited theme colors.

`POST /api/monthly/problems/:id/scan` creates an owner-scoped two-hour upload grant; only its hash is stored. `GET` with `scan_id` polls the capture. The phone uses `X-Lab-Token` for `/api/monthly/up` and its page upload/removal routes, and cannot submit for grading or read another account. Grants travel in a URL fragment that is immediately removed from the address bar and use a separate tab-session namespace from exam grants. The phone sends the processed image and initial capture; the initial capture survives local rotation. Monthly photo submission sends only `scan_id`, `request_id`, sheet version and language. The server constructs the structured answer, freezes the capture before grading, and permits one accepted attempt per capture. Network retries recover its immutable accepted answer even if the grant expired. A new attempt requires a fresh capture; typed and photo modes share the same three-attempt cap.

Each printable A4 page has four solid black corner marks for scan alignment. The earlier per-part `POST /api/monthly/problems/:id/photos` route remains compatible with saved drafts; its successful OCR retains the capture and recognized text, and the server validates every supplied `photo_id`. Full-page capture instead uses the dedicated upload grant and retains original/OCR/extraction records, including failed extraction for retaking. Own submission history offers authenticated capture viewing through `GET /api/monthly/photos/:photoId`, with no token in URLs and with object URLs revoked on navigation. Monthly captures and submitted answers persist independently of the exam-mode temporary-image cleanup.

Only the total score is released immediately for native monthly problems. Criterion scores, evidence and corrections remain private in submit responses, idempotent replays and own submission reads until the exact exclusive cutoff. For November this is December 1, 2026 at 00:00 KST. Responses expose `feedback_held` and `feedback_available_at`; the browser also suppresses stale cached details before that time. Exam-mode immediate feedback is unchanged.

Typed work receives a separate conservative review for explicit AI-generation artifacts. Style or LaTeX alone cannot justify withholding a grade; uncertain or failed reviews continue to normal grading. A supported suspicion is saved as `ai_suspected` with no numeric score, consumes one accepted attempt, and can be reviewed/regraded by an administrator without a new attempt. The UI explains false positives and recommends the student's own handwritten solution without claiming a photograph proves authorship. Verified photo OCR exempts a part only when its complete final answer and working match after whitespace/math-delimiter normalization; typed replacements and additions remain eligible for review. Client `via_ocr` flags cannot grant this exemption. Screening plus grading may take about two minutes; transport does not impose a shorter abort and network retries retain the original request ID.

Native monthly public presentation omits the supplied-material/editor provenance labels in headers and statement/solution tails. Private source content and the historical publication JSON remain available for administration and publication verification.

The Worker imports the JSON through `src/weekly-content.js`. Its existing D1 binding publishes the reviewed batch atomically on the first monthly/legacy weekly/problem request. Editorial details:

- Proton chain: retain the periodic displacement cell, infinite neighbour sum, zero-mode exclusion, and finite particle-count qualifications. Web point allocations are integer weights totalling 100; original allocations remain in the source metadata and statement.
- Walls and free targets: retain the exact finite-N geometry, admissible ordered-collision domain, and conditional minimum defining the critical coefficient. The model includes the full proof and numerical appendix; optional checks are not required for full marks. Its 12 tabulated results and small-N missed-target counterexample were independently reproduced during conversion.
